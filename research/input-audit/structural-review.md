# The Plague Road — Structural Review (whole-book pass)

Scope: all 25 corrected outlines, read as one book. This covers plot shape, climaxes, how the threads are woven, and where the outline repeats itself. Prose and continuity are covered in the other audit files.

Short verdict: the bones are strong. The book has a real midpoint (Kel'Thuzad), a real dark turn (Stratholme), a mirror structure (Kel'Thuzad and Arthas), and a planted-clue villain (Mal'Ganis). Its weaknesses are the usual ones for a planned book:
- The theme is stated more often than it is acted out.
- The investigation repeats one beat shape.
- The last act spends its length on travel and on systems failing, not on choices.
- The secrets and seized objects the book carefully sets up never go off.

Most of the fixes below rewire material that is already there. Few add new material.

---

## 1. Act the theme out instead of stating it

**Problem.** Jaina's lesson ("being right alone is not enough") is stated as an inner realisation at least eight times: Ch6 B5, Ch12 B7, Ch14 B10, Ch18 B2, Ch18 B5, Ch20 B9, Ch22 B4 and Ch25 B2. Each one will become a paragraph of epiphany in the prose, which is exactly where narrator-seep and summary slop come from. The book ends with the theme spoken aloud through a wall (Ch25 B2). That is the weakest way to land it.

Great books land their theme in one act that cannot be read any other way. Think of the lie in *Atonement*, or Stevens leaving Miss Kenton at the bus stop in *The Remains of the Day*. Speeches don't do that work. This outline already holds the acts it needs. They just aren't wired as the thematic hinges.

**The twin sins (already in the outline, not yet linked):**
- **Sylvanas, Ch9 B3:** she calls the move before Jaina's read is finished, and Lyriel dies. That is Sylvanas being right alone.
- **Sylvanas, Ch11 B6:** she arms Arthas by "giving him a war he knows how to want."
- **Jaina, Ch16 B8:** she gives Arthas a clean yes or no ("not cleanly, not now"), and he turns it into the Culling. That is Jaina being right alone.

**Fix:**
- **Ch16 B8.** Make Jaina's answer a solo act. Sylvanas reads Arthas (she has read him correctly since Ch6) and starts to step in, or reaches for Jaina's arm. Jaina answers over her because the answer is true. The answer is correct, it is given without the partner, and it kills a city. That turns the theme from an idea into a guilt Jaina carries.
- **Ch17 B6 and Ch18.** On the wall, each woman knows she put a hand on this: Sylvanas pointed Arthas (Ch11) and Jaina pulled the trigger (Ch16). The "crack closes through shared witness" beat becomes shared guilt. That is stronger, and it needs no dialogue to work.
- **Ch21 / Ch23. Pay off Ch9.** Sylvanas sees an opening and waits for Jaina's read, the inverse of the trap. Ch21 B11 already has her saying she "would not change the partner". Put the waiting on the page so the reader sees the change.
- **Cut** the inner restatements in Ch12 B7, Ch14 B10, Ch18 B2 and Ch20 B9, or shrink each to one concrete image. Keep the honesty exchange at sea (Ch20 B6/B9) because it is dialogue under pressure, not narration.
- **Ch25 B2.** Replace the spoken thesis with an act. Jaina packages the counter, and she addresses it to Sylvanas by name as the person who will deliver it. The line through the wall is practical, not reflective. The reader does the summing up.

## 2. The throne room: make the spell impossible alone

**Problem.** Ch22 B15 lays out the plan in full (frost, then arcane, then fire, with Sylvanas buying three to five seconds). Ch23 then runs it more or less as told. There is a long-standing craft rule for heists and assaults: if the audience hears the plan, the plan must break; if it works, don't tell it first. As written, the climax confirms the briefing.

The climax also splits the labour: Jaina casts all three schools, then Sylvanas shoots. The two halves happen one after the other rather than fused, so the climax doesn't express the book's answer.

**Fix:**
- **The plan breaks at the third school.** After arcane crosses the seam, Jaina has nothing left for the fire. She puts the last of it into Sylvanas's arrowhead, and Sylvanas's arrow delivers the fire through the seam. The kill is the spell. Neither woman could have made it alone.
- **Payoffs this creates:**
  - Ch1 opens on a clean arrow kill (Sylvanas in her element). The book closes its action on an arrow that only works because of the mage.
  - Ch21 B10's "her best opening arrow proves ordinary lethal standards no longer apply" becomes a setup, not just a defeat beat.
  - Ch25 doesn't need the thesis speech (see 1).
- **Keep B5** (the first arrow aimed at the sword arm to pull Arthas off Terenas). It is good.
- **Arthas should speak to Jaina once, by her first name.** In Ch15 B4 he pointedly calls her "Lady Proudmoore". In the throne room the death knight says "Jaina." It is the old intimacy returning at the worst moment, and it costs one line. Right now the outline gives Jaina no feeling at all about killing the man she once loved, which is the biggest emotional gap in the climax.
- **Optional, darker (tragic irony, as in *Oedipus*):** their arrival triggers the murder. Arthas meant to take the throne slowly, and Sylvanas's arrow forces his hand. That makes the prosecution in Ch24 partly right, so the trial becomes morally hard rather than simply unjust. Only take this if you want Book 1 to end on guilt as well as injustice.

## 3. Detonate the Scholomance secret at the trial

**Problem.** Three planted items never go off:
- Ch14 B3: Jaina keeps the room to herself (what happened between her and Kel'Thuzad).
- Ch13 B8: she carries out Kel'Thuzad's journals.
- Ch24 B4: the prosecution enters the journals as evidence.

The journals are only mentioned in passing at the trial, and the secret is never exposed. Chekhov's gun applies: a secret kept in Act 2 should be exposed by the enemy at the worst moment in Act 3.

**Fix (three linked changes):**
- **Ch12, give the debate a trade engine.** As written, Ch12 is several days of argument with no leverage, so it will read static. Use the quid pro quo from *The Silence of the Lambs*: Kel'Thuzad gives each piece of the waking spell only in exchange for something true about Jaina (Antonidas, Arthas, what she would do with the knowledge). She pays each time. This gives the chapter an engine, and it makes "the room" a concrete thing she is ashamed of, not a mood. Kel'Thuzad writes the sittings up in his journal, and she doesn't know.
- **Ch14 B3.** What Jaina withholds is specifically what she told him.
- **Ch24 B4/B5, the turn.** Ashcombe reads from the journals. Kel'Thuzad describes Jaina as a willing, gifted student, in his own hand, with her own confessions quoted. Sylvanas learns the secret in public, from the enemy. Her reaction is the relationship's final test. She can either refuse to look at Jaina, or give the half-step forward-right that, since Ch13, has meant she is verifying. Either way, the trial now does something to the pair rather than only to their reputation.

This also fixes a structural problem in the trial (see 5).

## 4. Sylvanas's last choice: refuse the rescue

**Problem.** Sylvanas's arc climaxes at Ch19 (defying the recall). After that she reacts rather than chooses. In Ch24 she is passive: things are done to her, Vereesa disavows her, and Lor'themar arrives with "force enough to matter politically and still not enough to open the door."

**Fix.**
- **Ch24 B11.** Lor'themar's three hundred can open one door: hers. Quel'Thalas will extract its own citizen under diplomatic pressure. The human mage is not covered.
- **Sylvanas says no.** Her lie was "the world beyond the border is someone else's problem." This is the cleanest possible refutation, carried out in a cell. The model is Socrates in the *Crito*, refusing an escape his friends have paid for. That scene is remembered for the reasons given at the open door, not for the trial before it.
- **The cost pays off Lor'themar.** Ch19 B5 established that he spent nearly everything, and she throws it back. That is a real price, not a reported one.
- **Ch25 then opens with both women in cells by choice on one side.** That is a stronger starting state for Book 2.
- **Reorder the commutation.** Lor'themar arrives during the trial, not after it, and his force is what buys the commutation from death to imprisonment. Ch24 B9's "commutation is political management" then has a visible cause.

## 5. The trial needs a moment of hope (Ch24)

**Problem.** Scene 3 is called "The Betrayals" and is a list: Antonidas blocked, Uther dismissed, Vereesa disavows, verdict. The end state is known from the story arc, so the reader watches each failure arrive on schedule. Kafka can do that because the absurdity is the point. Here it is a procedural.

**Fix.** Use the *To Kill a Mockingbird* shape:
1. Uther's testimony actually lands, and the court wavers. Thornton is visibly moved. That is the moment of hope.
2. Ashcombe's answer is the journals (see 3), and the room turns.
3. Vereesa's disavowal follows as the political nail.
4. The verdict comes, softened by Lor'themar's arrival.

This puts one rise and one fall on the scene instead of a flat line, and makes the loss measurable by how close it came.

## 5b. A possible sixth face for Mal'Ganis (choose with care)

If you want the condemnation to be steered rather than purely institutional, the clerk who hands up the journals in Ch24 could speak the farewell. This makes Mal'Ganis the hand behind the verdict. It strengthens the Ch25 reveal (he was in the courtroom), but it weakens the "good men losing to systems built to delay" mirror in the story arc. My recommendation is not to do it. Keep the courtroom human, and let the visitor in Ch25 be the first time the enemy and the system touch.

## 6. Mal'Ganis: the clue stays rare, but it is never the priority

**Problem.** The farewell is marked as a pattern at sighting 2 (Ch6 B7: "Sylvanas marks it at once as a pattern"). It sharpens in Ch9, cannot be dismissed in Ch15, and cannot be called coincidence in Ch17, yet nobody acts on it until Ch25. A competence-thriller lead who notices a rare, suspicious phrase five times and never chases it reads as a plot hole.

**Decision (author):** the phrase stays uncommon. Innocent characters do not say it.

**Fix: make the not-chasing a cost the plot charges every time.** Mal'Ganis's job in the story arc is to turn correct investigation into late investigation. He hides inside urgency. Each sighting happens when something bigger is already on fire, so Sylvanas has a fresh, good reason to let the face go. Her certainty grows, and her chances to act on it shrink. That is the le Carré pattern: Smiley's clue is in the file all along, and the only thing he lacked was the time and standing to read it.
- **Ch3:** the phrase sticks for a second, then the road pulls them apart. (No change.)
- **Ch6:** she notices it is the second time and asks Jaina. Jaina gets no magical read. The man is already gone up the road. Two is not a pattern yet, so she files it as wrong without calling it a pattern. (Tone down "marks it at once as a pattern".)
- **Ch9:** she hears it secondhand from Cyndia while wounded and with Lyriel dead. On the ridge she asks Cyndia to describe the robed man: a robe, at a distance, walking away. There is nothing to hunt.
- **Ch15:** Cyndia hears it in the rush to leave camp and reports it. Sylvanas is carrying the Scholomance crack and the Stratholme clock, so she tells Cyndia to keep a record of where, when and what face. Cyndia starts keeping one.
- **Ch17:** Sylvanas hears it herself in the service lane, and for once she has a face in front of her. She turns to go after the carter. Behind her the city starts breaking. She chooses the wall. This is the one active choice not to pursue, and it is the right choice, which is why it hurts.
- **Ch25:** the recognition is the thing she never had time for. With nothing left to do in a cell, there is finally time.

**The gaze is the second tell.** At every sighting the outlines already have him looking past the party toward his work: back up the road to Millhaven (Ch3), at the granary roofs (Ch6), at the column forming (Ch15), back at the wall (Ch17). In Ch25 the visitor doesn't monologue. He passes through the cells on his way to look at the blade, glances past the women toward the vault, and says only the farewell, in Kaldorei. Sylvanas recognises the look and the words together. Cut Ch25 B5's board-widening speech to one or two lines at most. Cormac McCarthy's Judge Holden is frightening because he is everywhere and rarely explains himself.

**Weave the sightings into consequences (second-read payoffs that are already almost there):**
- **Ch3 B9.** The carter asks about "the young grain-man". Lyriel answers truthfully and mentions the girl's testimony. That innocent answer is the leak. It tells him the pair have a witness and are on the grain trail. That is why the depot in Ch5 is cleaned ahead of them, a cause the outline currently doesn't give.
- **Ch3 and Ch6 second-read notes.** He looks over their riding formation. In Ch9 the trap "understands ranger spacing well enough to weaponize it". On a second read, he learned it on the road, and the woman who answered him dies in it.
- **The result.** The five sightings stop being passive observation. Each one turns into an enemy move later. That is what turns a planted clue into a plot.

## 7. Break the repeated investigation beat (Ch3 to Ch9)

**Problem.** Ch3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 share one shape: arrive, each woman solves her half, "the pieces lock", a witness dies before talking.
- The two halves lock in Ch4 B7, Ch5 B7, Ch7 B6, Ch8 B8 and again in Ch15 B5.
- A witness dies unquestioned four times: Ch4 (the initiate takes poison), Ch5 (the overseer is already dead), Ch8 (death-curse mid-sentence) and Ch9 (the courier dies silent).
- "Too late" is the outcome of nearly every chapter from Ch5 to Ch24.

The lateness is thematic (Mal'Ganis turns a correct investigation into a late one), but one note played eight times flattens into weather. Good procedurals vary the kind of obstacle each time. Tana French and le Carré both make each interview fail in a different way.

**Fix:**
- **Keep only two dead witnesses.** Ch4 (the poison) teaches the rule, and Ch8 (the curse) escalates it.
- **Ch5: the overseer is alive and lies.** He sends them the wrong way, and they lose a day finding out. This is a different failure that costs time, which also feeds the "late" theme.
- **Ch9: the courier talks, and what he says is wrong** (it is Mal'Ganis's misdirection), or he simply escapes. The Hearthglen dispatch can survive either way.
- **Give one early, clean win that holds.** Ch4's village gets saved: they stop the grain, and the village lives. That gives the reader a measure of what the pair can do when they are on time, which makes Stratholme crueller.
- **Pay the win off at the trial.** A sworn letter from the Ch4 militia captain is entered for the defence and ruled irrelevant. It is the one piece of evidence that is simply true. (The Ch4 village is too far from the Southshore–Capital road to reuse in Ch22's garland village, so the trial is the right place.)
- **Merge the "pieces lock" moments.** By Ch7 the reader knows the partnership works. Ch7 B6 can be a two-line exchange, not a scene.

## 8. Act 3 spends too long travelling

**Problem.** Ch17 (the Culling) is the emotional peak at 68% through the book, which is the right spot. After it:
- **Ch18:** a full chapter of the wall and its aftermath, ending with "north, unspoken".
- **Ch19:** a full chapter deciding north.
- **Ch20:** the voyage north (Jaina pays in blood).
- **Ch21:** the cave.
- **Ch22:** 16 beats, five scenes, and the voyage back (Jaina pays in blood again).

That is two chapters of decision and two sea crossings that run the same cost beat.

**Fix:**
- **Merge Ch18 and Ch19.** The wall (Scenes 1 and 3, minus the restatements) plus Vereesa arriving at the field camp the same night. North gets decided in the same chapter as the trauma that motivates it. That keeps grief and choice in contact, which is where the drama is.
- **Ch22: compress the return crossing into a jump cut.** Something like "Eleven days. Jaina didn't remember most of them." Keep only the nosebleed and the three-school breakthrough (B9). Spend the saved length on the capital approach and the Antonidas meeting. That is the more valuable material: the kingdom celebrating its own killer.
- **Thin out the dispatch beats.**
  - Antonidas calls in Ch10, 14, 18 and 19 and appears in Ch22. Drop Ch14's call so each remaining call is different in kind: caution, then an offer of extraction (love), then the cut. His arrival in person in Ch22 then lands as a surprise.
  - Lor'themar's bad-news runners arrive in Ch9, 11, 14 and 19 (via Vereesa). Cut Ch14's.

## 9. Lyriel's death needs a life first

**Problem.** From Ch2 to Ch8, Lyriel is a name in cast lists: "Still with the riding party." "Still riding point and perimeter." Her death in Ch9 is meant to break the pack permanently and drive Sylvanas's guilt, but the reader has no picture of her.

**Fix.**
- **Give her one trait and one scene with Jaina before Ch9.** Two options:
  - she is the only ranger who talks to the mage on the road and teaches her something small (elven riding, or a camp habit Jaina keeps using later);
  - she jokes where Cyndia won't.
- **Pay the habit off after her death.** Jaina keeps the habit after Lyriel dies. That sits alongside Cyndia's flank-checking (Ch9 B6, Ch14 B2) as a grief-in-action motif.
- **Ch3.** She is already the one who answers the carter (see 6). Make that exchange friendly, so the second read hurts.

## 10. Ch1 and Ch2: reach the mage sooner, and add the wit

**Problem.** Jaina appears on the last beat of Ch1. Scenes 1 and 2 are status quo plus a messenger. The story arc promises a "witty procedural" for Ch1 to Ch6, but the outlines hold almost no comic engine apart from Jaina misreading her audience in Ch2 B9.

**Fix:**
- **Ch1: merge Scenes 1 and 2.** During the hunt, Sylvanas checks a ward-stone herself and it reads wrong in her own hands. That gives her skin in the game, so when the runner says a human mage claims "the leylines are wrong", it lands as confirmation, not just a claim. The interrogation room then gets more space.
- **Give Ch1 to Ch6 a running translation joke.** Jaina talks in theory and Sylvanas demands the field version, every time. Each time Jaina's translation gets better, and Sylvanas's patience gets shorter.
- **Pay it off in Ch11 B6.** Alone in Arthas's tent, Sylvanas has to translate magic into logistics herself, and she uses one of Jaina's phrases word for word. It is a laugh line that is also a love line, and it costs nothing.

## Smaller notes

- **Ch21 B5.** Muradin's line that they "move like a machine built from two people" states the theme from outside. Show him watching them work instead, and let him make a dwarf joke rather than a diagnosis.
- **The formation-position motif** (forward-right half horse length, left-rear, empty right flank, Cyndia's sweep) appears in Ch9, 13, 14, 18 and 22. It is a good barometer, but it shouldn't drift every chapter. Use it at the break (Ch13), the repair (Ch18), and the inheritance (Ch22 B16). Elsewhere, leave the line steady.
- **Ch12 Beat 9–10.** Kel'Thuzad names the Lich King and the Burning Legion. That is a lot of cosmology for one sitting. Under the trade structure (see 3), the Legion should cost Jaina the most, so it comes last and is paid for.
- **Ch13's quick kill is right; keep it.** In Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor, Christ answers the Inquisitor's long, coherent case with a silent kiss, not a counter-argument. Sylvanas's arrow is that answer: Kel'Thuzad gets no last speech because the book's reply to elegant logic is action. Keep it that way, and don't let the prose explain it.
- **The survivor girl and Neral** leave in Ch3 and never return. The story arc says they "save no one who can testify". She is the one person they saved who could testify, and she is a witness to the "young grain-man". Either give her one reported line in Ch19 (through Vereesa), or leave her deliberately for Book 2.
- **Arthas leaves them alive in the cave (Ch21 B10).** Contempt works, but give him one look at Jaina before he turns away. That plants the first-name moment in the throne room (see 2).
- **Ch25 last image.** Don't end on "pattern locks". End on the count changing. Jaina, who has tracked the blade's pulse all night, feels it move: farther, then fainter. The blade is leaving the vault, with no wielder shown, which is consistent with "Do not add yet." It is a concrete closing image, and it turns the stinger into a cliffhanger.
