# The Plague Road: pacing and story-quality audit

Audit date: 2026-10-12. Method: `.claude/skills/planning-tension-pacing-audit/` (SKILL.md and references 1 to 6). No story file was changed.

## Material read

- **Outlines.** All 25 canonical outlines, as edited today. Line references use `chN:line` and point at the frozen copy in `research/input-audit/outlines-pre-structural/chapterN.md`, which matches `.afternoon/outlines/` as it stood before the live structural edits began.
- **Plans and cast.** `plans/story-arc.md`, all `characters/*.md`, and all `locations/*.md`.
- **Context only.** `continuity.md`, `fix-decisions.md`, `fix-log-malganis.md`, `plans-vs-outlines.md`, and the git word-diff of today's outline edits. Earlier continuity findings are not repeated here. Where a pacing finding builds on a gap those files already list, it is marked **(known: file §)**.
- **Pipeline state.** Prose already exists for Ch1 to Ch6, and Ch7 is in progress (`.afternoon/manifest.json`). A change to Ch1 to Ch6 therefore means rewriting prose as well as the outline. None of the top ten suggestions touches Ch1 to Ch6.

**Genre and phase standard.** The book is a competence thriller with an investigation spine that hardens into tragedy (`story-arc.md:3`). The audit uses two lanes from `2-genre-pressure-curves.md`:

- **Mystery / Investigation:** every chapter should narrow, widen or twist the case.
- **Thriller:** late acts need tight chapter jobs, short sequels and clock pressure, and the climax must play out in scene.

The phases are setup (Ch1 to 6), middle build with split threads (Ch7 to 14), atrocity (Ch15 to 18), endgame (Ch19 to 23) and aftermath/coda (Ch24 to 25).

**Shape in one paragraph.**
- **Ch1 to 7:** a slow, even ramp, sitting at 4 to 5 for five chapters.
- **Ch8 to 13:** the first real cost lands at Ch9, and the split-thread middle holds tension well.
- **Ch14:** a one-chapter sag.
- **Ch15 to 17:** a clean ascending ladder to the book's emotional peak.
- **Ch18 to 20:** a three-chapter trough after that peak.
- **Ch21 to 23:** a loss, an overloaded logistics chapter, then a strong climax.
- **Ch24:** a passive tribunal.
- **Ch25:** the coda.

The case logic of the investigation is essentially complete by Ch12, when the whole chain is known. After that, the only open case question is the farewell thread, and the outlines keep it off the board for long stretches. That is why the back half reads as thriller pursuit with no investigation tension under it.

## Strong zones (protect these)

1. **Ch8 to 9, hunters hunted, then the trap.**
   - Ch8 shows the enemy's internal defences, and the betrayal flows from the Ch6 directions.
   - Ch9 gives the book its first irreversible cost, and it comes from the protagonist's own flaw: "That mistake gets Lyriel killed" (ch9:46).
2. **Ch10 to 13, the split-thread middle.**
   - This matches worked example 2 almost exactly: entry (Ch10), external interrupt (Ch11), ideological duel (Ch12), arrival and misread (Ch13).
   - The intercut gives contrast, and Ch11's choice is one of the best in the book: "Sylvanas wins the room by giving Arthas a war he knows how to want" (ch11:132).
3. **Ch15 to 17, the Stratholme ladder.**
   - The chapters run plan, then local success and city failure, then atrocity. Each one turns harder than the last.
   - Jaina's honest answer at the gate, "not cleanly, not now" (ch16:142), is the book's best complicity beat.
4. **Ch21 to 23, the arrow callback.**
   - Ch21: "Sylvanas's best opening arrow proves ordinary lethal standards no longer apply" (ch21:174).
   - Ch23: "Arthas dies where the first shot failed" (ch23:165).
   - The first arrow is the warrior acting alone, and it fails. The second comes after the mage opens the seam, and it kills. This is the partnership thesis acted out, and it pays off Sylvanas's Ch9 lesson without a word of speech.

## Findings by area

### 1. The pressure curve

**1a. Early plateau, Ch3 to 7 (same-value-shift plateau, but in the relationship, not the case).**
- Following worked example 1, the case itself is not repeating. Each chapter answers a new question: border threat, then vector, then network, then authority failure, then mechanism.
- What repeats is the relationship value, which moves one notch warmer every chapter with no reversal:
  - Ch4: "Jaina is thinking of her as Sylvanas, not just the ranger" (ch4:58).
  - Ch5: "She stops thinking the mage and starts thinking Jaina" (ch5:59), and the scene line says Sylvanas "fully accepts Jaina as a working partner" (ch5:130).
  - Ch7: "Sylvanas can think Jaina's name plainly now" (ch7:60).
  - Ch8: "Their partnership is finally working at full speed" (ch8:65).
- Over the same run the leads pay no cost. The Ch2 wildlife fight, the Ch5 depot and the Ch8 escape cost them nothing. The only person who dies is the traitor.
- The genre promise for Ch1 to 6, "witty procedural", has no cue in the outlines (known: plans-vs-outlines b.1). So the opening act has neither contrast (humour) nor cost (wounds). Ch1 to 6 already have prose, so the realistic fix point is Ch7 to 8 (suggestion 14).

**1b. Ch14 sag (sequel overload).**
- Ch14 is all processing. It contains:
  - a full re-briefing of material the reader heard in Ch12 ("Jaina starts with scale. The plague is part of a larger war directed by the Lich King", ch14:118);
  - two relays (B7, B9);
  - an Antonidas call (B8);
  - a mirror realisation (B10).
- What only Ch14 does is narrower: Jaina chooses the omission, Sylvanas forces the bodies back in (B5), and the lich and the Nathrezim journal are planted.
- Ch15 B5 then re-briefs Arthas on the same chain, so the chain is told three times in four chapters (Ch12, Ch14, Ch15). By the skill's investigation rule, clue-processing repeated without a frame shift flattens.

**1c. Trough after the peak, Ch18 to 20.**
- One stillness chapter after the Culling is earned. Ch18 is the right one: the Alterac indictment and the model shift are unique work.
- Ch19 is strong drama (Vereesa).
- Ch20 then adds a second, lower-pitched stillness unit. Its promised value shift, "The sea gives them the honesty the road kept postponing" (ch20:140), delivers an abstract conversation about duty and certainty and a fourth pass of Jaina's mirror. That leaves three chapters at 5 to 6 between peaks of 9 and 8.
- The fix is to put real content into Ch20's honesty lane (suggestion 7), not to cut a chapter.

**1d. Late-act load in Ch22.**
- Ch22 has five scenes and sixteen beats. It concentrates the handoff, the return march, the crossing, the framework invention, the road, the institutional failure, the plan and the threshold.
- This is worked example 3's "return chapter that concentrates too many failure, planning, and threshold beats". The real surgery site is here, not Ch20 and not the coda.

**1e. "Too late" as a book-wide same-value plateau.**
- These beats resolve as lateness:
  - Ch5 depot: "already late";
  - Ch8: "the sick certainty of being late";
  - Ch13: arrives late to the room;
  - Ch16: "It is late";
  - Ch21: "too late before anyone crosses the threshold";
  - Ch22: four days late;
  - Ch23: "one beat too late";
  - Ch24: Uther twenty minutes late, Antonidas late, Lor'themar late.
- It is the thesis (Mal'Ganis "is the reason good investigation keeps arriving one step late"). But "we are later than we thought" is the skill's textbook example of a same-value plateau.
- The book has almost no on-time win that then costs something in a different direction. Ch16's single granary is the only one, and it is immediately swallowed. Suggestion 12 turns it into a second kind of loss.

### 2. Repetition of chapter and beat shapes

**2a. Witness dies before talking: four times in six chapters.**
- Ch4: "he has already swallowed poison meant for capture" (ch4:161).
- Ch5: "Sylvanas finds the overseer dead at his desk" (ch5:113).
- Ch8: "A necromantic death-curse kills the prisoner mid-sentence" (ch8:103).
- Ch9: "The courier dies without speaking" (ch9:133).
- On a second read these deaths are Mal'Ganis's sanitising, so the repetition has a purpose. The problem is that the protagonists never adapt to it. In Ch8 they take a prisoner as if Ch4 had not happened. One line in Ch8 showing them guard against poison (search the mouth, bind the hands) would make the curse an escalation they could not have predicted.

**2b. "Two halves of the answer join": four uses.**
- Ch4 B7: "Jaina has the body-side answer, Sylvanas has the road-side answer".
- Ch5 B7: "they finally fuse the paper trail with Jaina's magical map".
- Ch7 B6: "Neither half of the investigation would have been enough alone".
- Ch15 B5: "Sylvanas's road marks crossing Jaina's finer arcane notes".
- This is the partnership engine and should recur. But it always resolves into agreement. The book never has the two reads point in opposite directions. Havel in Ch7 is the obvious place for that (suggestion 14).

**2c. The institution calls and the protagonist refuses or ignores: the most repeated beat shape in the book.**

- The Antonidas line runs refuse (Ch14), then "not yet" (Ch18), then sever (Ch19). Ch18 is softer than Ch14, so the ladder de-escalates in the middle.

**2e. The Arthas briefing ladder (Ch6, Ch11, Ch15): repetition that works.**
- Three war-tent scenes, each with a different outcome: he ignores the warning, then he is armed, then he races.

### 3. The partnership arc

The plan's arc is threat, then asset, then partner, then necessary, then absolute trust under fire.

| Turn | Where | Earned? |
|---|---|---|
| Custody to escort | Ch2 | Yes. The practical admission (B5) and the map correction (B9). |
| Escort to partner | Ch3 to 8 | Earned by competence, but stated five times with no friction (1a). The stall. |
| Partner to necessary | Ch9 | Yes. Sylvanas's call kills Lyriel. The skipped "Jaina's mistake" half is known (plans-vs-outlines b.5, b.6). |
| Crack | Ch13 | Yes. Jandice's poison plus the tableau. The best-built turn in the book. |
| Omission | Ch14 | Yes, as a choice. **Never paid off** (3a). |
| Repair | Ch15 to 18 | Earned by action, but **claimed four times** (3b). |
| Chosen over institutions | Ch19 | Yes. The parallel severance. |
| Honesty | Ch20 | **Promised, empty** (3a). |
| Outside witness | Ch21 | Yes. Muradin: "a machine built from two people". |
| Structure | Ch22 to 23 | Yes. "Sylvanas says I" (ch22:224). The arrow callback. |
| Endurance | Ch24 | Shown only as suffering. The leads make no choice (7c). |
| Thesis | Ch25 | Stated in words. It should be enacted (5d). |

**3a. The Scholomance omission is set up hard and never delivered.**
- The setup:
  - "Jaina sees the misread land and lets it stand" (ch13:149).
  - "Jaina chooses omission on purpose and keeps riding with it" (ch14:53).
  - "The briefing is complete, but the worst omission is still there" (ch14:137).
- No later chapter has Jaina tell Sylvanas what the room did to her. A grep of Ch15 to 25 for confession or omission language finds only ch18:131, "through movement instead of confession".
- Sylvanas's wrong read of the sanctum (that Jaina was a willing student) is also never corrected aloud.
- "No spoken absolution" is a sound rule for the Stratholme repair. But this is a withheld fact, not a feeling. Leaving it unspoken spends the Ch13 to 14 setup for nothing, and it leaves Ch25's "never enough alone" standing on an unconfessed lie.
- Ch20's empty "honesty lane" is the natural home for it (suggestion 7).

**3b. The repair is claimed in four consecutive chapters.**
- Ch15: "This is not repair by speech. It is repair by who rides where" (ch15:64).
- Ch16: "The repair here comes through work" (ch16:252).
- Ch17: "The crack from Scholomance closes in practice through shared witness" (ch17:42).
- Ch18: "Shared witness shuts the crack" (ch18:214).
- (Ch17/18 duplication known: plans-vs-outlines b.8.) The pacing cost is wider than one duplicated line. The same relationship value inches forward through four chapters, and the crack never gets the chance to hurt in Ch15 to 16, where it should cost something.
- Keep it fully open through Ch16: Sylvanas verifies, and Jaina feels watched while she works. Then let Ch18's shoulder touch close it in one beat.

**3c. Ch9's lesson relapses in Ch13 without the outline noticing.**
- Ch9: Sylvanas "moves before the read is finished".
- Ch13: "Sylvanas refuses to give the room another second to keep talking" (ch13:145). This is the same flaw, acting on her own lens before the other half speaks, and it costs trust instead of a life.
- That is good structure, but nothing names it. One line in Ch13 makes the relapse legible, and then Ch23's second arrow (fired only after Jaina's seam opens) reads as the flaw finally mastered (suggestion 15).

### 4. The Arthas thread against the investigation

**On-page appearances.** Ch6, Ch11, Ch15, Ch16 (end), Ch17, Ch21, Ch23. That is seven, which is a healthy count for a never-POV antagonist.

**Gaps.** Ch7 to 10, Ch12 to 14, and Ch18 to 20. Ch22 has him by report only.

**4a. The pivot of his fall happens off the page.**
- He leaves Ch11 eager: "Arthas leaning forward when the threat becomes marchable".
- He arrives in Ch15 frayed: "His armor is damaged and unrepaired… he calls Jaina Lady Proudmoore" (ch15:118).
- Between the two is Hearthglen, his first fight against the dead. It is delivered by two relays (ch13:170, ch14:162) and one sentence from Uther: "His words about Arthas are not" (ch15:103).
- The reader sees before and after but not the hinge.
- Cheapest repair, using material already there: Uther's Hearthglen report should contain one concrete act that prefigures the Culling. For example, Arthas ordered the fever tents burned before the sick had died, and Uther stopped him. Ch17 then becomes the second time, and this time nobody can stop it. That is first-time-checked, second-time-unchecked escalation, built from a scene that already exists (suggestion 10).

**4b. The protagonists' complicity is the strongest Arthas material, and the ending does not cash it.**
- Sylvanas aimed him (Ch11). Jaina's honest "no" handed him the Culling, and Ch17 has her as "the witness whose honest answer made the next order possible without causing it" (ch17:56).
- The causal "Sylvanas lit this prince's road" line is absent from canonical (known: plans-vs-outlines b.8, b.9).
- The tribunal is where these true facts should come back as weapons (suggestion 6).

**4c. Is his fall felt?**
- At Stratholme, yes. The clean hands are a superb image.
- At the throne room, mostly by design: "Do not give him interior justification or tragedy language the scene has not earned."
- The weak spot is 4a. Without the hinge, Ch17 reads as a jump, not a slide.

### 5. The Mal'Ganis thread

**Spacing.** The sightings fall at Ch3, Ch6, Ch9, Ch15 and Ch17, then the reveal at Ch25. The gaps are 3, 3, 6, 2 and 8 chapters.
- The six-chapter gap covers the Scholomance block, where he is fully absent ("Mal'Ganis is steering both routes" is absent: known, plans-vs-outlines b.5).
- The eight-chapter gap (Ch18 to 24) is the real risk. For a third of the book the pivotal thread is off the board, and then it carries the final chapter.
- Do not add a sixth sighting; the five-face count is load-bearing. Keep the thread alive in the gap without a new face (suggestion 7 carries this in Ch20).

**5a. The first-read layer is over-cued, and the investigator never acts on it.**
- The cues:
  - Ch5: "The old carter clue has upgraded from a stray memory to a real pattern" (ch5:37). This comes with no new evidence; the second farewell is still a chapter away.
  - Ch6: "Sylvanas marks it at once as a pattern" (ch6:139).
  - Ch9: Cyndia hears it from a robed figure among fleeing cultists, and Sylvanas weighs "one being, one cult group, or coincidence" (ch9:134).
  - Ch15: "a pattern Sylvanas cannot dismiss as coincidence" (ch15:147).
  - Ch17: "cannot be dismissed as coincidence anymore" (ch17:119).
- After Ch9, Sylvanas knows a cultist uses the phrase. In Ch15 a quartermaster's aide in Arthas's camp says it while sending a sealed manifest to the Stratholme granaries, which are exactly Jaina's targets for the next chapter. The Ranger-General rides off without a question.
- In a competence thriller that reads as a lapse in competence, not as restraint. The reader is roughly ten chapters ahead of the protagonist.
- The second-read layer (the covert contacts) is subtle and well-chosen. The first-read layer is loud and inert.
- The fix is not to hide the farewell. It is to let Sylvanas pursue it once and have the trail evaporate (suggestion 2). That also gives the back half the investigation question it currently lacks.

**5b. Will the second read land? Contact by contact:**
- **Ch3 question about the grain-man.** It lands because it is small and specific.
- **Ch6 directions to Havel.** It lands hardest of all, because the consequence (the Ch8 trap) happens on the page.
- **Ch9 dispatch.** It is currently weak. The second-read note says only that "the courier carries his order" (ch9:129). That dispatch is what sends Sylvanas to Arthas, and Ch11 is where she arms him. If the figure *lets* Cyndia take the courier, then Mal'Ganis aimed Sylvanas at Arthas, and Sylvanas aimed Arthas at Hearthglen. That one line delivers the character file's "keep Arthas busy with the wrong war" and `story-arc.md:48`'s "steers Arthas", both currently absent from the page (known: plans-vs-outlines b.11 "curates real information: ABSENT"). It is the most valuable second-read line still available (suggestion 3).
- **Ch15 manifest.** It lands only if the manifest's grain shows up in Ch16. Right now the "half stripped" second store (ch16:129) and the manifest never meet.
- **Ch17 nod.** It lands as "pulling his people out". But "steers the trio to the one place where they will witness it" has no stated purpose. Why would he want witnesses? If the answer is that witnesses who can prove nothing are his preferred outcome, Ch25 should say so (5e).

**5c. Three tells are planted, and Ch25 uses only one.**
- **The farewell:** used.
- **Jaina's null read**, "Jaina gets no magical read from the man at all" (ch6:139): planted once, never repeated at Ch15 or Ch17 although Jaina is present at both, and not used in Ch25. The character file assigns it: "Jaina brings the ward logic."
- **The Nathrezim journal**, "demons who wear borrowed faces. She gives it a sentence and moves on" (ch14:134): seized at the arrest and entered as evidence in Ch24, then absent from Ch25.
- The Kaldorei root of the phrase is never set up at all. Ch25 needs it to "land as impossible before it lands as pattern key" (ch25:77). Nothing earlier lets Sylvanas, or the reader, hear the Common phrase as a translation.

**5d. Ch25's recognition is solo and recited.**
- Scene 1 states the thesis: "she was never enough alone" (ch25:97). Scene 2 then has Sylvanas solve the book's last mystery alone, by memory cascade, followed by a five-item recap list (ch25:141-142).
- The thesis should be enacted, not just stated. Ch25's hold-back rule ("No claim that Jaina sees the full five-face pattern before Sylvanas's memory cascade lands") still leaves room for Jaina to supply *what* the being is through the wall, once Sylvanas has the faces:
  - the null read: it was nothing until it chose to be felt;
  - the journal: borrowed faces.
- Dramatise two of the re-seen contacts, not five. The Havel directions and the Ch9 dispatch are the two that cost them most.

**5e. The visitor has no stated reason to come, and the condemnation has no author.**
- The character file allows "the late gloat".
- Mal'Ganis's Front 1 is "sanitize sites, remove witnesses". The tribunal has just filed away the only two witnesses to the truth without him lifting a hand.
- One line in Ch25 B5 makes the condemnation the last site he sanitised. It retroactively turns every "too late" in the book into his design, and it gives the ending its second-read payoff (suggestion 3).

### 6. The ending, Ch22 to 25

**6a. The throne-room kill: set up and paid off.**
- The pieces arrive in order: arrow fails (Ch21), seam heard (Ch21), three-school framework (Ch22), "Sylvanas says I" (Ch22), execution (Ch23). This is the best-built causal chain in the book.
- Ch23 plays out in scene: 3 beats of infiltration, 2 of murder, 4 of fight, 2 of arrest.

**6b. The four-day wait is a coincidence the climax depends on.**
- "He has been in Capital City for four days before the formation reaches the walls" (ch22:39), yet "Terenas is still alive when they reach the chamber" (ch23:123). Nothing says why Arthas waits, or why he kills on the hour they arrive.
- The answer is half present already: "his imported guard force settles inside" (ch22:196). Make it explicit: he is turning the palace post by post, and the murder is timed to a scheduled event, such as the king's formal audience for the returning prince. The celebrating village can announce it. The coincidence becomes a public clock the pair are racing (suggestion 5).

**6c. The condemnation: thinly set up.**
- The prosecution is abstract: "using real facts and reversing only the direction of cause" (ch24:123). The only concrete exhibits are the grimoire (added today) and the blade.
- The outlines already hold the true facts that would convict:
  - Sylvanas delivered Arthas to Hearthglen (Ch11).
  - Jaina told him the city could not be saved (Ch16).
  - Jaina spent days as Kel'Thuzad's guest and carried out his books (Ch12 to 13).
  - Sylvanas is a declared deserter (Ch19).
  - They killed two palace sentries in the corridor on the way in (ch23:100).
- That last fact is also an evidence hole as written. Ch23 defines the "undead royal guard" as "proof Arthas already colonized the building from the inside" (ch23:79), and Ch24 never mentions them. Either they are evidence for the defence, or, once the blade lets go, they are two dead guardsmen, and Ashcombe should count them as the defendants' first two murders.
- The cast sheet's arresting officer, Captain Gavren Marsh ("devastating because he simply reports what the room looked like"), is the eyewitness the prosecution needs. He is not in the outlines (suggestion 6).

**6d. Tribunal pacing: one value shift four times in a row.**

## Bottom line

The structure is sound. Its spine runs Ch9 Lyriel, Ch11 arming the prince, Ch13 the misread, Ch16 the honest no, Ch17 the Culling, Ch21 the failed arrow, Ch23 the second arrow, and it holds.

What the book most needs is for its investigation to stay an investigation after Ch12. The Mal'Ganis thread is the only case question left in the back half. Right now it is loud on first read, passive in the protagonist's hands, and solved by one woman reciting a list. Three cheap changes would make Ch25 the payoff of the whole book rather than a sequel hook:

1. Let Sylvanas chase the farewell once and lose it.
2. Let the Ch9 dispatch be the moment Mal'Ganis aimed her at Arthas.
3. Let the recognition need both women.

Around that:
- Fix Ch22's load and today's contradiction.
- Give the four-day wait a clock.
- Build the tribunal from the pair's own true acts.
- Spend the Scholomance confession where the outline already promises honesty.
