## Patterns
- **Sentence length.** He speaks in short declaratives, usually 5 to 12 words. When he has the stage he builds a measured second sentence that ends on a cadence ("until the flame of life has been snuffed out... forever"). He likes paired parallel sentences ("It is there that we shall settle the score between us. It is there that your true destiny will unfold."). An ellipsis marks a theatrical pause before the payoff word: "...forever", "Obviously...". He does not ramble.
- **Journey and road imagery (key).** In both Third War scenes he frames Arthas's life as a journey: "Your journey has just begun", "This is where your journey ends". The other images are destiny, a score to settle, the end of the road. This is the strongest canon support for "May the stars keep your road". The farewell sits inside his own canon vocabulary for other people's lives.
- **Darkness, sleep and possession.** "The night beckons!", "Death rages!", "I hunger!". He uses Sleep as a spell and dresses it as care ("You seem tired.", "Time out."). In WotLK he whispers to Abbendis in her dreams. A body worn as a disguise is a "shell". He names his master by title, "the Dark Lord" or "the Dark Lord of the Dead", never as "Ner'zhul".
- **Forms of address.** These shrink the listener in age or size: "young prince", "boy", "young human", "Prince Arthas" (mocking formality), "tiny mortal", "Miserable insect" (Anach kyree). The unit lines use "mortal". Other nathrezim do the same: "little human" (Tichondrius), "little banshee" (Detheroc), "little archer" (Varimathras), "my wayward pet" (Balnazzar).
- **Mock regret (verbal tic candidate).** He uses polite expressions of disappointment as insults: "Unfortunately for you", "How disappointing.", "Unfortunate, the best was yet to come.", "A pity...", "You're stronger than I thought." The courtesy is the knife. This is the most reusable tic. A clipped "Farewell." as a sign-off is canon, from *Doppelganger Duel*.
- **Humour and gloating.** The humour is dry and condescending, not jokey: "Haha, incompetence at its finest.", "You thought of that?". He states his ownership flatly: "your people are now mine". He gloats about a plan that is already in motion rather than one still to come.
- **How he refers to humans.** As property or as material: "your people", "household by household", "weak little shell". He calls them weak to their face only once his mask is off.
- **What he never says.** He never pleads, apologises or swears oaths. He never uses plain modern idiom. He never explains the whole scheme until he has won or is leaving. He never names his master's weakness. (Varimathras begging Sylvanas is the counterexample among his brothers.)
- **Under pressure.** When he loses control he shows it in one clipped exclamation: "Gah!", "WHAT?! No matter.", "ENOUGH! I waste my time here.". Then he retreats and promises revenge ("I'll have my revenge... on him AND you!"). At real betrayal his polish breaks off mid-sentence: "What? He can't possibly mean to--". The rule: the larger the threat, the shorter the sentence.

## Nathrezim disguise and infiltration evidence
- **Infiltration is their design purpose.** The wiki's Nathrezim article, citing the WoW Grimoire, says they "specialize in gaining the trust of the very forces they are sent to undermine". Their own name for themselves, *thal'kituun*, means "unseen guests" (the Ashbringer comic renders it "unseeing guest"). Disguised dreadlords pass each other written messages hidden by a cantrip. (Nathrezim article, citing *Cryptograms*.)
- **Mal'Ganis as Grand Admiral Westwind (WotLK).** He first whispered to Abbendis, who took his voice for the Light. Then he took Westwind's face, claimed the Light had sent him, and told her he did not mean to replace her while he quietly took her command. In disguise he sounds like a curt, sardonic officer ("How did you find me? Did Landgren tell?"). The demon shows only in the contempt.
- **Balnazzar as Saidan Dathrohan.** In disguise he is a warm, pious rhetorician who leads with questions: "Have you not felt it when you fight the Scourge? ... We fight hardest to protect what we love the most." (*A Cleansing Fire*, cited on the Saidan_Dathrohan page.) Unmasked in the Ashbringer comic he says: "Fleeting are the moments, these days, in which I may truly be myself..." This is disguise fatigue, the same note as Mal'Ganis's "weak little shell".
- **Possession versus illusion.** Balnazzar possesses Dathrohan's corpse ("I will take up residence in this weak shell your soul once called home"). The wiki says Mal'Ganis likewise possessed Westwind, and that both dreadlords disliked hiding inside a human "shell".
- **The brothers in Lordaeron (TFT).** Varimathras plays the courteous envoy: "Greetings, Lady Sylvanas... I've come to offer you a formal invitation to join our new order." Then comes the threat: "Careful, milady. It would be unwise to incur our wrath." Detheroc controlled Garithos by mind control rather than by disguise.
- **Later impersonations.** In Legion, a dreadlord poses as Medivh's spirit to Khadgar (*Harbingers*), and another poses as a Stormwind soldier (*Anduin: Son of the Wolf*). In Shadowlands, Mal'Ganis takes a Maw Walker's form. He also met Sylvanas as a "handsome blood elf".
- **Takeaway for this story.** Canon disguises are competent, plain and in character for the role. The tell is always a small slip of contempt or vocabulary. A ten-thousand-year-old blessing he no longer notices he uses fits that canon pattern.

No canon word for moon, night or road on its own was found. "Dor" occurs only inside compounds; the wiki speculates "dorei" = children. The night elf WC3 units speak almost entirely in English ("Goddess, light my path.", "Show me the path.", "By the light of the moon!"). Their Darnassian is limited to Anu'dora, Ash'thero danador, Tor falah-no dorah, Anu'dorini Talah and Bathel'daelune.

**Closest canon to "May the stars keep your road":** no canon equivalent exists. The nearest are "Al diel shala" (safe travels), "Ande'thoras-ethil" (the canon "May your..." farewell), "Elune-Adore" (a deity "be with you"), and "Ishnu alah terro shar" (a blessing that builds on Ishnu-alah to add stars).

**Inferred morphemes used below:**
- *anu* = let / may. It is shared by "Anu'dorini Talah" (Let my will be known) and "Anu belore dela'na" (The sun guides us).
- *dela* = guide / keep, from *dela'na* = "guide(s) us".
- *ana'diel* = your faring. "Doral ana'diel?" means How fare you?, and *diel* also appears in "Al diel shala" (safe travels).
- *irana* = shield, from Irana'ethil.
- *adore* = be with you, from Elune-Adore.

**Candidate renderings**
1. **Anu kal dela ana'diel** (AH-noo KAHL DEH-lah AH-nah-DEE-el). *Anu* = may/let (inferred), *kal* = stars (wiki speculation), *dela* = guide/keep (inferred), *ana'diel* = your road/faring (inferred). It copies the canon frame "Anu belore dela'na" (The sun guides us) and puts the night elves' stars where the Thalassian phrase has the sun. That suits a pre-Sundering ancestor of both languages. Four words, word for word. **Recommended.**
2. **Kal irana ana'diel** (KAHL ih-RAH-nah AH-nah-DEE-el). *kal* = stars (speculation), *irana* = shield/ward (inferred), *ana'diel* = your faring (inferred). Literally "stars ward your faring". It is terser and reads older because it has no optative particle. "Keep" in the sense of guard.
3. **Kal-adore ana'diel** (KAHL-ah-DOR-eh AH-nah-DEE-el). *kal* = stars, *adore* = be with (inferred from the canon greeting Elune-Adore), *ana'diel* = your faring. Literally "stars be with your road". A night elf listener would recognise it at once, because it echoes Elune-Adore. It is the least literal of the three.

None of the three phrases is canon. Each one is built only from canon-attested syllables.

## Later-era flags
- **Nathrezim origins.** That Denathrius created them, that they serve the Jailer, and the "exile" are all Shadowlands-era retcons (2020+). None of it is available to a Third War setting.
- **thal'kituun** first appears in the Ashbringer comic, set after the Third War. As an in-world term it is trait-only.
- **"Kirel narak", "Anach kyree".** Mal'Ganis speaks Eredun only in WotLK. In WC3 the dreadlords speak only Common on screen.
- **Westwind, Doppelganger Duel, Sylvanas novel, Revendreth lines.** All post-Third War. Use them for traits only, never for plot facts.
- **The Culling of Stratholme dungeon (2008)** is a retelling of the Third War scene. Its lines are compatible, but its boss banter ("Time out.") is game-flavoured.
- **AU invention.** No source I checked places Mal'Ganis among the Kaldorei 10,000 years ago. The phrase-origin premise is the story's own.
- **Recent-content warning.** The wiki's Darnassian page now lists 2026 entries ("Shen'dorei", skyborne, *Forever*). None of these is used above.
- **Thalassian.** Glosses taken from Thalassian (diel, dela'na, Shorel'aran) are attested in the daughter language, not in Darnassian. Flag this if a reader checks against Darnassian alone.
