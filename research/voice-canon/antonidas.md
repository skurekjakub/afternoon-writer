## Patterns

**Forms of address.**
- "Child" for Jaina, said warmly and with authority.
- Full title used as a blade ("Prince Arthas... your noble father"). His courtesy can be snide; Arthas calls it that ("There's no need to be snide").

**Syntax.** Complete and orderly. He states the institution's position in the first person plural: "We've prepared", "we will be forced".

**Humour.** Dry and scholarly, and it can turn to contempt ("That crazed fool's convinced...").

**Blind spot.** Canon shows him confident in procedure and dismissive of unorthodox warnings. The story builds on that: he believes Jaina, but the institution he speaks for moves too slowly.

**Under pressure.**
- The courtesy hardens into formal ultimatums ("Make your choice").
- Grief comes out as one plain sentence ("It pains me to even look at you").

**Never says.** No slang. No profanity. No hurry in his syntax, even when he is in a hurry.
