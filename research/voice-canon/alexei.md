## Patterns
- **"Do try to..."** This urbane imperative appears in both the Talking Skull line and the Hearthstone play line. It is the host's courtesy with a threat underneath: the guests are expected to survive, and it would be rude of them not to. This is the strongest canon anchor for the story's courteous host.
- **Understatement.** "Finally." is one word at the end of a long ordeal. "Here we go." He does not dramatise.
- **Passive agency and blaming the place.** "I and my family fell victim to this place". The house, the school and "this place" do the harm, never the bargain he made. "those who would meddle in necromancy" makes a general moral that excuses him. Even when he expresses regret, he does not say "I chose". For the story's bargain-maker this matters: he speaks of the arrangement as something that happened to the estate.
- **Speaks as the house.** "I and my family", "our fate". He uses the first person plural of a head of house. The family's identity is the estate (the deeds, the inheritance, "rightful heir").
- **Formal syntax.** "what seems like ages ago", "those who would meddle". The phrasing is mildly archaic and finished, with no slang (unlike his daughter's "real stones").
- **Family register (from the sons).** Money and property first: deeds, fortune, holdings, inheritance, "Barov Industries", a "summer retreat on the shores of Lordamere Lake". Rank is taken as self-evident ("I am a noble, after all"). Bargains are transactional and phrased as mirrors ("Ensure my future and I shall ensure yours"). Servants are a measure of comparison ("servant's quarters", "Servants, attack!!!"). Contempt is for cowardice and low birth. The "after all" and the casual estate-dropping are the old-money tells to carry into the father, made gentler and more courteous.
- **Euphemism for the deal.** The Hearthstone "supervision" (quoted as his own word) suggests he calls the Cult arrangement a lease and himself the overseeing landlord. Non-canon, but it fits.
- **Oaths.** None on record. The freed spirit says "Is that a Light I see?" (MoP gossip), which is Light-framed but late.
- **Humour.** Dry and drawling ("Do try to keep yourselves alive"). It never cackles.
- **What he never says (on record).** He never names Kel'Thuzad or the Cult in speech, never admits the bargain as his choice, and never raises his voice except possibly the uncertain "PREPARE TO DIE, VILLAIN!" (speaker not stated on the page).
- **Under pressure.** We have no canon for the living man. The undead spirit, once freed, breaks into repeated thanks ("Thank you, <name>. Thank you!"). That is relief that drops the polish, but it belongs to a different era.

## Later-era flags
- Every WoW line from the father is from his spirit (MoP, 2012), years after he was raised as a death knight. The remorseful, warning tone ("Let our fate serve as a warning") is hindsight the story's living lord does not have. Use it only to show that he deflects onto "this place".
- Hearthstone lines are non-canon but show him alive. They are the only living-era hints.
- The sons' lines come from Classic, after the Scourging, and from WoD. They show the family's register, not the father's voice.

## Thinness note
The canon is very thin. The father has no living dialogue in WoW, Warcraft III or any novel I could find. Everything recorded is either his MoP ghost (six short lines, some with inferred attribution) or a two-line Hearthstone card. The courteous old-money host in the story will be mostly invented. Anchor him on "Do try to...", understatement, the first person plural of the house, and talk of the estate that keeps his own choice out of the sentence.
