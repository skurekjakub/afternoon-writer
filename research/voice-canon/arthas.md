## Patterns

**Sentence length per stage**
- Prince: medium-length, conversational lines with contractions. He opens with a friendly connective: "Look, here's where we stand," "Well, I guess we should get underway," "Me? Well, of course!" Many short tag lines close a turn: "Let's get moving!" "Let's go." He asks real questions: "What were those creatures, Sergeant?" "Could the grain itself be plagued?"
- Hearthglen/Stratholme: the lines shorten into commands and exclamations, and "Damn it" opens the turn. There is one exception, and it is telling. When he loses the argument he shifts into a long, formal, legal register ("by my right of succession and the sovereignty of my crown, I hereby relieve..."). The longest sentence in the arc is the one where he removes Uther.
- Northrend: he speaks in absolutes, with "no one," "nothing," "any," "all": "No one goes home," "Nothing shall prevent me," "I would gladly bear any curse," "I will give anything or pay any price." He still exclaims.
- Post-blade: short, calm declaratives with no exclamation marks. The contractions loosen and then drop away. Compare "It's done!" in Stratholme with "It is finished." in Northrend. "I've taken care of everything" is soft and almost kind, which is what makes it cold.

**Imagery.** He has very little. He talks in tasks and logistics: camps, gold mines, granaries, warehouses, gates, ships. Abstractions appear only under pressure, and they are always possessive: "my homeland," "my people," "my crown," "my revenge." He calls enemies "beasts," "scum," and later "worm." His Light imagery is formula, not description (see oaths below).

**Forms of address**
- Uther: plain "Uther" in every friendly line ("Can the formalities, Uther," "Yes, Uther.", "I don't know, Uther."). He says "Lord Uther" in only two places: in the third person to Jaina ("tell Lord Uther what's happened") and in the speech that strips Uther of his command. He uses the title as a tool of formality, never as warmth. Uther calls him "lad" when warm ("I know you won't, lad") and "boy" when cutting ("Watch your tone with me, boy"; "You are not my king yet, boy!").
- Father: Arthas mentions him fondly in passing ("Father still hopes..."). He speaks to him as if in prayer, in advance, while Terenas is absent ("Father, forgive me for what I must do."). The last time he says it to his face is the murder: "Succeeding you, Father..." In the unit lines it is "my father the king."
- Muradin: "Muradin," and once the full "Muradin Bronzebeard, is that you?" He says "old friend" in a warm reunion line that was cut ("It's me, old friend") and in the break ("...old friend. Not even you."). The same address is turned against Muradin.
- Jaina: always "Jaina" in private. "Miss Jaina Proudmoore" is a showman's introduction made to his men. Her last appearance in his speech is a single questioning word: "Jaina?"
- His men: "To arms, my brothers!" (Strahnbrad), then "my warriors" (Dissension), then "Damn the men!" (Frostmourne). Captains are "Captain."

**Oaths and invocations of the Light**
- "Damn" runs through the whole arc, and its object moves. First it targets circumstance ("Damn! The attack has already begun!"), then exasperation ("Damn it, Uther!"), then people ("Damn Uther for forcing me to do this!", "Damn the men!"). That migration is the clearest single marker of his fall.
- The Light shows up as rote paladin phrasing: "Light, give me strength!", "We are the Chosen of the Light. We shall not fall!", "Light-blessed waters", and the unit lines "The Light is my strength" and "Light, guide my path." After Stratholme he stops invoking it. In Northrend he calls on "the spirits of this place" and then heeds "the voice of Frostmourne." In the dungeon retelling the Light is someone else's job: "May the Light have mercy... for I will not."
- His single sworn oath is a vow of vengeance, even in a cut prince-era line: "Jaina... I swear by the Light. You shall be avenged." (Cult of the Damned, unused).

**Humour.** As a prince he jokes, and the jokes are quick, situational, and a little cocky. He mocks his own princehood ("It isn't easy being a prince"), teases Jaina ("Jaina usually runs a little late"; "Let's study it after we kill it, okay?"), and enjoys trouble ("Never a dull moment"; "We may need to pay Andorhal a little visit", which was cut). The last flicker is sarcasm aimed at Uther in Hearthglen: "Then feel free to tag along, Uther." There are no jokes from the Prophet scene onward. The cut Northrend line "Me too, Muradin. Me too." is wistful, not funny. After the blade, what reads as humour is cold irony: "I've taken care of everything," "Succeeding you, Father..." The later quip "Doesn't anyone stay dead anymore?" (Frozen Throne) shows that the dry wit survives in a dead register.

**What he never says.** These come from the fetched lines, so they are inferences, not canon rules.
- He never apologizes to anyone living. Jaina apologizes to him in the Prophet scene, and he cuts her off ("Don't say it!"). The only forgiveness he asks for is from his absent father, and he asks before he acts.
- He never asks for advice after Blackrock. "I don't know, Uther." is his last spoken doubt. After that his questions become challenges ("Have I?") or the bare "Jaina?"
- He never says "retreat" or "home" as a choice for himself. "Flee?" is scorn, and "No one goes home" is a decree.
- He never calls the infected anything but "these people." In the dungeon version he is clinical about them: "these people are all infected with the plague and will die soon."
- He never calls Uther "my lord" or "sir" in the lines found.

**How pressure changes him**
1. "We" turns into "my" and "I." Early on he says "We'll get to the bottom of this" and "We'll find them and bring them home safe." Under pressure it becomes "my homeland," "my revenge," "I'm going. With or without you."
2. He reaches for rank the moment an argument goes against him, after first waving it away. "I'm not king yet" (Strahnbrad) becomes "As your future king, I order you" (Stratholme), then "by my right of succession," then "Succeeding you, Father." Uther throws his own phrase back at him: "You are not my king yet, boy!"
3. He gets louder before he goes quiet. Exclamation marks peak in Hearthglen, Stratholme and Dissension. After the blade he is level and soft-spoken. Stillness is the danger sign.
4. He frames atrocity as rescue: "Better that these people die by my hand than serve as your slaves in death!", "I can only help you with a clean death," "You no longer need to sacrifice for your people."

## Later-era flags
Use these only as evidence of an underlying trait, never as a register to imitate:
- "I've damned everyone and everything I've ever loved in his name, and I still feel no remorse. No shame. No pity." — RoC Scourge campaign, after the throne room: trait-only: later era. It shows the stripped, clipped closing fragments he will grow into.
- Death knight unit lines ("Frostmourne hungers.", "What is it now?", "No one orders me around.", "I was a fool to trust in the Light.", "I will be twice the king my father ever was.", "For the Lich King!") — RoC/TFT Scourge hero: trait-only: later era. They show the impatience and the father-rivalry beneath the prince's "I should be in command."
- Frozen Throne lines ("Muradin's dwarves? Doesn't anyone stay dead anymore?", "I have returned, lich, but you will now address me as King.") — trait-only: later era. They show the surviving dry wit and his fixation on title.
- Golden novel, merger with Ner'zhul: "No we. No one tells me what to do..." (fragment from the memorable-quotes section of the Arthas Menethil page) — Lich King, later: trait-only: later era. It shows the refusal to be commanded.
- Not used, but noted: "This kingdom shall fall, and from the ashes shall arise a new order that will shake the very foundations of the world." The wiki attributes it to the Arthas' Betrayal transcript, but I could not confirm it against the cinematic audio, and it may come from another scene. Treat it as [placement unverified] and do not build the throne room on it.
- All WotLK Lich King dialogue: trait-only: later era. None was collected here.
