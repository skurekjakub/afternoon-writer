## Patterns

**Sentence length.** Medium. One clause of observation, then one of inference, then a practical next step. Lines 2, 3 and 6 make a chain: what the sources say, what she believes, where to go next. She rarely builds long subordinate structures in canon. The "built sentences" in the story sheet are a story choice, and canon allows them only if each clause moves.

**Imagery.** Plain and physical, with a hitch before the word that frightens her: "the land around the granary is... dying", "sewn together from different corpses". She names horror in kitchen words, not scholar's words. "The currents of magic" is the canon idiom for what she senses.

**Forms of address.**
- First names for friends ("Arthas").
- Courtesy formulas for institutions and allies ("the generosity of the elves is greatly appreciated").
- She is "child" to Antonidas.
- She says "we" when she proposes action ("We should...").

**Oaths.** "I pray" and "Light" sit in the human idiom of WC3. There is no profanity in canon at this era.

**Humour.**
- Light and teasing with people she likes ("since a prince escorted me anywhere").
- Mild self-mockery about being a scholar in a war ("I'm no warrior", "All I ever wanted was to study").
- It is warmer than the sheet's "dry". Dry is the story's development, and canon's base is bright.

**Curiosity aloud.**
- She says when something interests her: "That's curious", "Sounds interesting".
- She tells people to be quiet so she can think.
- She is openly curious even when the thing is horrible.

**Under pressure.**
- She gets cut off when she argues on feeling ("You can't just--", "But--").
- When she does finish, the line is an apology followed by a flat limit: "I'm sorry, Arthas. I can't watch you do this." She doesn't argue the point again. She states what she will not do and leaves.

**Inference markers.** "Our sources believe", "I believe", "Obviously". She marks how sure she is, and "obviously" shows a bit of youthful confidence.

**What she never says in canon at this era.**
- No profanity.
- No cruelty.
- No grand speeches.
- No aphorisms. Those arrive after Theramore (line 16).

**Later lines as trait evidence.**
- Line 17 shows a refusal defined by a moral line drawn against another person's atrocity. That is the Stratholme refusal grown up.
- Line 19 shows a teacher's warmth and a joke about having been an apprentice.

Use the traits. Never use the cadence of a stateswoman.
