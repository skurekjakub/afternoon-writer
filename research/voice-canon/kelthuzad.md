## Patterns
- **Lecturer by default.** He defines terms ("Necromancy. It is called necromancy."), corrects how others name things, and treats horror as method ("a standard experimental technique", "the gauge by which I measure my progress"). "Why, ... of course" is a teacher answering an obvious question. He likes pedagogic and Latinate words: gauge, precepts, preparation, ritual, notwithstanding, obligation.
- **Sentence length.** When calm, he speaks in complete medium declaratives with one subordinate clause at most. He often uses two beats: a frank admission, then a turn ("Yes, I ordered... But the sole credit is not mine."). He confirms his guilt calmly. He does not deny things he is proud of.
- **Ellipsis as patience.** When he holds power (over Helcular, the dying moment, the spirit), he trails off: "In due time... All in due time...", "the scourging of this land... begins." The pause is his authority.
- **Imagery.** He uses abstractions, not physical detail: power, progress, knowledge, gift, cleanse, paradise of eternal darkness, the long run. He borrows religious words and turns them (bless, gift, heretics, church, paradise, eternal). He never gives sensory gore.
- **Forms of address.** Titles used sarcastically when speaking upward ("The venerable Antonidas"). Diminutives and plain names when speaking downward ("children", "Helcular" repeated, "Naive fool"). He gives his followers "my brothers" (per the Kel'Thuzad page Quotes section; mission not identified). He calls Arthas "Prince Arthas" only after his own death, as a spirit, then "young one", "young death knight" and "death knight" in later eras. He addressed Arthas and Jaina together as "children", and the "again" shows they had met before. No verified line names Jaina directly, and in RtD he only watches her. He bristles when he is diminished himself ("hardly qualifies me for boyhood").
- **Oaths.** "Merciful gods" (plural and secular, a mage's oath, never the Light), "damn" ("I do not give a damn"). He never invokes the Light.
- **Humour.** Dry and deflating, often at his own expense when an experiment fails ("I'm still working out some minor details..."). Mock-solicitous sarcasm ("I began to think you had fallen ill"). Told-you-so ("Told you my death would mean little").
- **What he never says.** No apology, and he never concedes the principle. His only concession is tactical, "I will be more discreet", said through gritted teeth (RtD). He never calls his work evil or cruel. He calls it necessary, progress, a gift, cleansing. He never shows fear to peers. In RtD his fear shows only in narration, and later in front of the Lich King.
- **How pressure changes him.** (1) Challenged by superiors, he moves from icy sarcasm to exclamation: "You lie!", "Because we are necessary!", "Merciful gods...". He is cut off mid-sentence twice in RtD, so in public he loses the floor before he loses the argument. (2) Frightened, he gets short: whispered imperatives ("Make her be quiet!"), small questions ("It would have killed me?", "Where is the master?"), and finally begging ("Please... Please!"). (3) At his death in WC3 he does not beg. He pulls back into contempt and the long view ("Naive fool... in the long run"). For this story's sanctum death, the WC3 register is the canon model: a measured line, a dramatic pause, and no plea.
- **Credentials as a weapon.** "As a former member of the Kirin Tor..." He still uses the rank he lost and measures others by it ("more magic... than you could probably imagine").
- **Contempt for the Kirin Tor as old men.** "archaic ways", "antiquated" (cut off mid-word in RtD), "fools". His complaint is that they are timid, not that they are wicked.

## Later-era flags
- **Spirit (WC3 Undead ch. 2-5).** "She is persistent. Reminds me of you, death knight." "Be wary. The elves likely wait in ambush." "Tell him nothing! Only you can hear me. The dreadlords cannot be trusted." He becomes a counsellor-schemer here. The cadence is still human, but he now addresses Arthas as an ally. Trait-only.
- **Lich (WC3 RoC late, TFT).** "That, young one, is a very complicated matter. Suffice it to say they no longer hold any favor with the Legion." "Don't be foolish. The Lich King told me how our encounter would end." "Be patient, young death knight." Also "I am reborn, as promised!" These show the deflecting-teacher trait. Trait-only: later era. Do not port "young death knight".
- **Naxxramas (WotLK).** "Your petty magics are no challenge to the might of the Scourge!", "I will freeze the blood in your veins!", "Pray for mercy!", "Do... not... rejoice! Your victory is a hollow one, for I shall return with powers beyond your imagining!" This is raid-boss bombast. "Petty magics" and "beyond your imagining" echo his living credential-boasting. Trait-only: later era. Do not use the volume.
- **WC3 unit responses** ("I always wanted to start my own religion... so I did!", "I need to print more brochures", "Fifty-thousand gold a year in childcare and they call it a cult?!"). These are fourth-wall jokes. Do not port.
- The often-quoted "As my lieutenant, you will gain knowledge and magic..." is the **Lich King** speaking (*Call of the Lich King* section), not Kel'Thuzad.
- Heroes of the Storm and Shadowlands lines ("Death's victory draws nigh") are later-era game banter. Not consulted beyond the wiki listing.

## Thinness note
Living-era canon is decent: about 40 spoken lines in *Road to Damnation*, the Helcular exchange and one WC3 mission. **Novel gap:** I could not verify any Kel'Thuzad dialogue from Christie Golden's *Arthas: Rise of the Lich King*. None is recorded here, and nothing from it should be cited as canon wording. Jaina never speaks with him on record before Andorhal, so their sanctum conversation has no canon template. Build it from the RtD lecture-under-challenge register.
