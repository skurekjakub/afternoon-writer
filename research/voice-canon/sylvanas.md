# Sylvanas Windrunner: canon voice research

Story era: living Ranger-General, before and during the Third War. Anything she says after her death is a later register. It is evidence of an underlying trait at most.

**Verification key**
- **Fetched:** the line was pulled this session from a Warcraft Wiki (warcraft.wiki.gg) page or transcript. The wiki's transcription was not checked against the game files.
- **Recalled:** the line comes from memory and was not confirmed this session.

The wiki blocks raw access, so every line came through a summarising fetch tool. The tool split some long lines to meet a length limit.

## Patterns

**Sentence length.** In combat she uses one-clause commands ("Fall back to the trees!"). Her defiance runs to one long finished sentence, usually split by a comma into a concession and a counter ("You've won through this gate, butcher, but you won't get through the second."). She concedes the fact and then takes it back.

**Forms of address.**
- She names enemies by what they have done, never by title: "butcher", "you wretches", "you monsters". She does not call Arthas "prince" once in these transcripts.
- She addresses her own people collectively and by instruction ("Fall back").

**Oaths and language.**
- "Damn" is in her mouth in canon.
- At the peak of a fight she switches to Thalassian: a curse at the enemy, an oath at her last stand.
- She keeps the switch for the moment of greatest pressure.

**Humour.**
- She takes the enemy's assumption and turns it back on him: a question that repeats his premise, then a dry correction built on elven pride ("Apparently you've never fought elves before.").
- The humour is contempt plus competence. She is never playful.

**Prediction as defiance.** She states the future as fact: "you won't get through the second", "it shall never be yours". She doesn't threaten. She tells you how it will go.

**Pride and entitlement.** Even dying, she states what she is owed: "I deserve... a clean death." She claims her due plainly.

**What she never says in canon.**
- She never pleads for help.
- She never explains her feelings.
- She never addresses an enemy by his honours.
- She does not hedge. Even "No! You wouldn't dare!" is an accusation, not a plea.

**Under pressure.**
- Canon contradicts "quieter, not louder" when she is on a battlefield. Under assault she shouts orders and taunts that carry across a field ("Damn you monsters!").
- Quiet pressure suits rooms and politics. Loud command suits the field.
- At the very end the sentences break up ("I deserve... a clean death"). That is the one place her finish fails.

**Later lines as trait evidence.** Lines 11 to 13 show traits that already exist in the living woman:
- she frames justice as an account to settle ("even the scales");
- she keeps her motives to herself ("my own reasons");
- she bargains with a formal conditional that names both sides ("You will aid us... or I shall cease to aid you").

Use the traits, not the gloom or the formality.
