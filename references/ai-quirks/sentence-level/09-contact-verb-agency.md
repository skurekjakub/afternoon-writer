# Contact Verbs with Agency

`found` and `settled` borrowed from physical contact and given to something that cannot do the thing: a hand, an ache, a discovery. The sentence sounds physical, but the verb belongs to the narrator's habit, not to what the character did.

AI defaults to these because they put a body or a sensation in the grammatical subject slot and sound precise.

**Hard limit:** zero uses where a body part is the subject of `found`, where `found` wraps a discovery, or where a sensation `settled`. Literal uses are fine: the mare settled into a walk, she found the first ring on the map.

**Hunting note (for editors):** this is a judgement call, not a regex. Read the subject of the verb. A person or animal doing the thing is fine. A body part or a sensation doing it is not.

---

## Body part finds

### 1.
**Bad:**
Sylvanas's fingers found the bowstring before she had decided to reach for it.

**Good:**
Her hand was on the bowstring before she decided to reach for it.

**Why:** The fingers are given the verb and the character is demoted to the owner. State where the hand is.

### 2.
**Bad:**
Her eyes found the door.

**Good:**
She looked at the door.

**Why:** Eyes do not find anything. She looked.

### 3.
**Bad:**
His hand found hers in the dark.

**Good:**
He took her hand in the dark.

**Why:** He is the actor. The hand is the instrument.

## Discovery wrapper

### 4.
**Bad:**
He tried to salute and found his hand still wrapped in the reins.

**Good:**
He tried to salute. His hand was still wrapped in the reins.

**Why:** `found` announces that he noticed a fact. State the fact.

### 5.
**Bad:**
She found herself staring at the map.

**Good:**
She stared at the map.

**Why:** `found herself` is a filter plus a hedge. If she did it, she did it.

### 6.
**Bad:**
He reached for the flask and found it empty.

**Good:**
He reached for the flask. It was empty.

**Why:** Two events and a fact. The wrapper adds nothing.

## Sensation settles

### 7.
**Bad:**
She rolled her shoulder until the old ache settled back into the joint.

**Good:**
She rolled her shoulder until the old ache was back in the joint.

**Why:** The ache does not land anywhere. Say where it is.

### 8.
**Bad:**
A heavy cold settled over the camp.

**Good:**
The camp went cold.

**Why:** `settled over` gives weather a body. State the change.

### 9.
**Bad:**
Dread settled in her stomach.

**Good:**
Her stomach tightened.

**Why:** Dread is a label. The body fact is the evidence.

## Literal uses (keep)

### 10.
**Keep:**
The mare settled into the long, rolling walk Thalassian horses could hold from dawn to dusk.

**Why:** A horse doing a physical thing. Nothing is laundered.

### 11.
**Keep:**
Sylvanas found the first ring on the map pinned beside the hearth.

**Why:** A person finds an object by looking for it.
