# Counting Devices

The model numbers things nobody in the scene is counting: strides, heartbeats, breaths, arrows, seconds, the order of events. The number sounds precise. It works as a rhythm device and reads as a narrator keeping a tally.

**Hard limit:** zero counting devices. A plain-fact number that does no rhythmic work is fine: two days' ride, a second horse, both hands, a count a character says aloud once.

---

## Ordinals and counts as rhythm

### 1.
**Bad:**
He took his first ten strides under the silverleaf, where she had no line. She let him have them. She let him have the eleventh too, when he jinked left around a boulder and looked back up the slope at nothing.

**Good:**
He made the silverleaf, where she had no line, and she held her shot. She held it again when he jinked left around a boulder and looked back up the slope at nothing.

**Why:** Nobody is counting his strides. The numbers fake precision and the `let him have` tags narrate restraint. State the cover and the held shot.

### 2.
**Bad:**
The first arrow went wide. The second took him in the thigh. The third found his throat.

**Good:**
Her first arrow went wide. The next took him in the thigh, and he went down still reaching for his knife. She shot him through the throat.

**Why:** An ordinal ladder sets a drumbeat in place of the fight. Keep one marker if needed and let each shot have its own consequence.

## Counted body time

### 3.
**Bad:**
She held her breath for three heartbeats, then four.

**Good:**
She held her breath until the patrol had passed the gate.

**Why:** Time passes by event, not by a pulse count.

### 4.
**Bad:**
He counted to ten before he opened the door.

**Good:**
He waited until the voices inside stopped, then opened the door.

**Why:** Counting is a stock suspense device. Use what he is waiting for.

## Clock time as measurement

### 5.
**Bad:**
For a full minute nobody spoke.

**Good:**
Nobody spoke. Out in the yard a bucket scraped on stone.

**Why:** A clock measurement where a sound or an event can carry the silence.

### 6.
**Bad:**
It took them eleven minutes to reach the ridge.

**Good:**
They reached the ridge with the light already going.

**Why:** Precise minutes are a narrator's stopwatch. Name the cost or the change.

## Distances and quantities for precision

### 7.
**Bad:**
Twelve paces of open ground lay between the tree line and the wall.

**Good:**
A short run of open ground lay between the tree line and the wall, too short to need a horse and too long to cross unseen.

**Why:** Say what the distance means to the people in it.

### 8.
**Bad:**
Nineteen dead lay in the square.

**Good:**
The dead lay in rows across the square, and the rows went on past the well.

**Why:** A total is a report. The image is the scene.

## Keep

### 9.
**Keep:**
"That's eight," Cyndia said, and dropped the last ear into the sack.

**Why:** A count a character makes aloud, once, as part of the action.

### 10.
**Keep:**
It was two days' ride to the coast.

**Why:** A plain fact with no rhythmic work.
