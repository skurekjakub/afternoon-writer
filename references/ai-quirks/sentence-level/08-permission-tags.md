# Permission Tags

`She let it.` The character is shown granting permission to a feeling, a sensation or an object. The tag looks like restraint or control. It is the narrator stating that she is controlled, instead of showing what she does.

AI defaults to it because it closes a sentence on a note of composure for free. Every instance reads as the same move: something happens, then she "lets" it.

**Hard limit:** zero uses of `she let it`, `let it settle`, `let it pass`, `let the [feeling] do its work`, `let [it/them] stay` as emotional or sensory permission.

**Not this pattern:** physical allowing with an object and a result. `She let the dog out.` `She let him in.` `She let the rope run through her hands.`

---

### 1.
**Bad:**
A bead of sweat crept from her hairline toward her left eye, and she let it.

**Good:**
Her shoulder burned, sweat creeping from her hairline toward her left eye.

**Why:** The tag adds a narrated decision to a plain event. The sweat is the event. If she does something (wipes, holds still), state that instead.

### 2.
**Bad:**
She leaned against the wall. She let it take her weight.

**Good:**
She leaned against the wall. Stone, cool through her shirt. She put her weight on it.

**Why:** `let it take her weight` gives the wall the verb and the character the permission. Make her the actor and name the contact.

### 3.
**Bad:**
The thought arrived with a pang, and she let it come and go.

**Good:**
The thought arrived with a pang, sharp and quick. Then it was gone.

**Why:** The thought arrives and leaves by itself. The tag claims credit for the character. State the two events.

### 4.
**Bad:**
She stood there and let the fear do its work.

**Good:**
She stood there. Her palms were slick. She did not run.

**Why:** The tag summarises. The replacement gives her body and the one decision she made.

### 5.
**Bad:**
She sat with that for a moment. Let it settle.

**Good:**
She sat with that for a moment.

**Why:** `Let it settle` is a second sentence that repeats the first and narrates the result.

### 6.
**Bad:**
She held still and let the thank-you settle next to the laugh.

**Good:**
She held still. The thank-you sat next to the laugh, two warm things in the same spot.

**Why:** Split the stillness from the sensation. Each gets its own subject and verb.

### 7.
**Bad:**
The tea went cold. She let it.

**Good:**
The tea went cold. She did not touch it.

**Why:** If the permission tag is the only record of her behaviour, replace it with the actual non-action in plain words.
