# Unplaceable, Then Placed

The narration says the character cannot identify something, then identifies it in the same breath. The disclaimer builds a false mystery, and the gloss answers it from outside her head.

**Hard limit:** zero uses. Either she knows it, or she doesn't, or the narrator is leaking.

---

### 1.
**Bad:**
The wind carried a sharp clean smell Sylvanas couldn't place, the air on the tops before snow.

**Good:**
The wind carried a sharp clean smell, the air on the high tops before snow.

**Why:** She can place it, because the sentence places it. Drop the disclaimer.

### 2.
**Bad:**
There was a sound she couldn't name, the low groan of a ship's timbers taking weight.

**Good:**
Somewhere below, a ship's timbers groaned as they took the weight.

**Why:** Name the source. If she knows ships, she knows the sound.

### 3.
**Bad:**
He gave her a look she couldn't read, half warning and half apology.

**Good:**
He gave her a look she couldn't read.

**Why:** If she can't read it, the prose can't either. In limited POV the gloss is a leak.

### 4.
**Bad:**
Something she couldn't name moved in her chest, something close to grief.

**Good:**
Her chest went tight.

**Why:** A disclaimed feeling plus a label is two tells. Give the body fact.

### 5.
**Bad:**
A taste she couldn't place, sweet and wrong, like bread gone over.

**Keep (when the mystery is the clue):**
A sweet, wrong taste at the back of her throat that she couldn't place.

**Why:** In a mystery the unknown can be the point. Then keep it unknown and give only the sensation. Do not answer it in the same sentence.
