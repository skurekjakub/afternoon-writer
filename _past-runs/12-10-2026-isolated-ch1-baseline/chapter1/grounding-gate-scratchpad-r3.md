## KEEP decisions

### G4: "The rangers moved without being told, the one in the corner stepping back along the wall" / "Around her the rangers held still" (line 325)
- Correction to the r2 reasoning: the r2 scratchpad placed "the ranger working the door" inside the storeroom. He is in the outer room (keg ranger, 255/267/269/295). Only one ranger is established inside the storeroom (273).
- Defense: once the door is pushed wide (325), the keg ranger and the four outer-room rangers (227) are a doorway's width behind her, and the corner ranger is in front of her. "The rangers" reads as the post's rangers reacting across the open threshold. "The one in the corner" singles out the only movement that matters to the line.
- Why it survives: no position contradicts another, and the reader can stage the walk-in (door, circle, corner, chair, ring-bolt). The plural is a loose collective, not a positionless conflict.

### G4: Cyndia "tipped her head toward the mage's right hand" with the door eased to a hand's width (297)
- Defense: the gesture's direction (through the door, toward the prisoner) is legible. Both women saw the inked hand moments earlier (285). The broken-off line ("Same ink as—") carries the meaning, and the gesture only points.
- Why it survives: it is subtext protection (D6). Making the gesture explicit would flatten the beat.

### G4: "Sylvanas set a hand on the doorframe" and wind "through the slit" (303)
- Defense: she is just outside the nearly shut door after stepping back. The outer room has its own south slit and wind-whine (227).
- Why it survives: it fits the door chain.

### G2: "smaller than the one by the door" (271)
- Defense: it refers back to the outer-room lamp "Beside the door" (227).
- Why it survives: the comparison is clear, and nothing staged depends on it.
