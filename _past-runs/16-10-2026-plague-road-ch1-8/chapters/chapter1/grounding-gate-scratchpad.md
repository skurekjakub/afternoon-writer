## KEEP decisions

### K1. G11 / G9: "sun-warmed Eversong blossom" (L17)
- Defense: geographic binding is needed, and the cost is one word.
- Why it survived: this is the chapter's only naming of Eversong, which D4 needs at the opening. It goes into an existing smell list without a new clause, and the location file asks that Windrunner ground be "seen, not indulged." It sits near the edge of label territory but doesn't become a tour note.

### K2. G9 / G12: "who tossed her fine-boned head at the delay" (L51)
- Defense: the detail is sourced (thalassian-horses.md) and is a single adjective.
- Why it survived: a horsewoman plausibly registers her mare's lines. The beat also carries pressure, since the mare is impatient with the stalling Lor'themar is needling her about. One adjective is not a pile-up. Borderline, noted for the human reviewer.

### K3. G11: "Convocation chamber at Sunfury Spire" (L35)
- Defense: it binds the avoided institution to a place, and the source is the location file.
- Why it survived: it is Sylvanas's resentment, and the named seat makes the bench she is dodging concrete. Silvermoon is still not shown directly, so the plan constraint holds.

### K4. G3: stall-and-stone exchange (L25-L53) and stone exchange (L73-L89)
- Defense: embodiment recurs inside the runs.
- Why it survived: the knife thumbed and sheathed, the arrow pulled and cleaned, the horse cropping a fern, the scar scratch, glove off and on, Lor'themar's own palm on the stone, the bit chiming. The runs fail the featureless-room test; they couldn't move to a bare room without real loss.

### K5. G3: budget needling on the ride (L95-L115)
- Defense: the run is short (5 spoken lines before the interior turn), and the beats are meaningful.
- Why it survived: "drawing level," "counting off fingers" on the rein hand, and "the same half-length back he always rode" bracket and interrupt it. The interior paragraph (L105-L107) is the deliberate pause, not a float.

### K6. G3: runner report and split (L125-L173)
- Defense: physical interruption recurs.
- Why it survived: wrist across the mouth, the horse blowing, the closed fist with cold in it, the bow lowered across his knees with his eyes on her hand, the bow slid back into its loops, two fingers lifted off the reins, the runner's horse still splayed. The embodiment is distributed through the run, not left at its edges.

### K7. G3 / G4: Cyndia's arrest report (L197-L217)
- Defense: the report is a monologue paragraph, staged in a room that has already been established.
- Why it survived: the guardroom is set up just before it (sweating walls, ward-lamps, rangers on the far wall, skin on the stew). Cyndia "falling in at her shoulder" and the "left hand near her quiver" place her body. The closing five short lines are under the six-line threshold and lead straight into the rack alcove.

### K8. G8: rack and satchel inventory (L219-L221)
- Defense: it is POV-bound evidence reading, not lore.
- Why it survived: Sylvanas is reading a prisoner through her gear, so a list is the honest form. Every item carries character: darned stockings, bread hard as bark, the left boot retied. Nothing explains the world to the reader.

### K9. G12: "Kirin Tor ink ... dries violet at the edges" (L239)
- Defense: the plan requires that "Kirin Tor ink and staff make match the Farstrider file," so there has to be some distinguishing mark. Violet is the established Kirin Tor colour.
- Why it survived: it is a minor invented specific in service of a plan-mandated fact, and Cyndia says it as field observation. Flagged as an inference, not a direct source quote.

### K10. G12: "A lord admiral's daughter" (L243, L269)
- Defense: it is canon outside the story files (Daelin Proudmoore) and consistent with the Proudmoore surname in the plan.
- Why it survived: it doesn't conflict with any source. Not in the plan or the character files, so it is flagged as inferred from canon.

### K11. G12: "the one with the split cap" (L197)
- Defense: it is a local identifying mark, the kind of label rangers give individual stones.
- Why it survived: it carries no lore and makes no claim about the world; it just shows a Farstrider way of naming stones. Harmless invention.

### K12. G12: "the way her own people bred speed into horses" (L207)
- Defense: it is an analogy and doesn't contradict the source.
- Why it survived: thalassian-horses.md stresses endurance and responsiveness but doesn't rule out speed. The simile is about inbred traits, not horse doctrine. Noted as slight drift from the source emphasis.

### K13. G5 / G1: interior deliberation before the step (L281-L287)
- Defense: each paragraph carries a concrete callback (rangers holding the doorway, the map's ink ring, the lens and clerk, a rider north, ink in the creases of her fingers).

### K14. G10: final pressure beat (L289-L295)

- Why it survived: boots scraping, the stone humming in the wall, a cup set down in the guardroom, then the fixed line. It is concrete and clipped with no bloat.
