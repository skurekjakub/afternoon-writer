# The young man with the cart (the newer grain seller)

Never seen on page. Millhaven's survivor girl said a new grain seller, cheaper than Mister Hollis, called at her house and set her parents quarrelling (Ch3). Here the granary keeper Joss describes a young, civil man with a good horse and a cart, brown or fair haired, who three months ago came through from the west road selling barley and flour cheaper than the valley farms by two coppers on the measure. He sold Joss four loads, the last a fortnight ago, bought him a jug at the alehouse and asked after his wife's leg. Sylvanas: 'I asked him for a face. He gave me a young man with a cart.' Jaina: the same man as Millhaven's, 'Cheaper than Mister Hollis.'

The sacks in the loft (and a jar under the floorboard) carry the stencil ANDORHAL; Brask: 'That's the big granaries.' Jaina: 'Or where he wanted us to think it came from.' Sylvanas: 'Either way, it's the road he drove.' The party turns onto the grain road after him. The old carter of Ch3 (who asked about a grain man) is not mentioned in this chapter, and no link is stated.

## Chapter 5
Still never on page. Sylvanas's belief that she was following one man collapses at Corley's (thirty-one places; 47; 53 slips; 'somebody with yards and books and boys'): the cart man is one delivery hand of many. Traces: the 'own cart' short-rate loads from midsummer (Millhaven first; twelve sacks, two coppers under); the miller past Coldwell says he came back 'yesterday' to buy back his last four sacks at more than he had sold them for ('the damp had got into them'); at Wick one free carter left south before light that morning without breakfast ('He always paid'). Sylvanas reads these as being a day ahead of her, with someone shutting doors or sending word on. Not confirmed that all three are the same young man. Corley never saw any name ('It's the load I book, not the man').
