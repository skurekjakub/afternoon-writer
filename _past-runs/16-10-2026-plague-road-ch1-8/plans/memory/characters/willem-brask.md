# Willem Brask

Militia captain of the sick village two days south of Millhaven. Big, heavy-shouldered, quilted jack too warm for the day, leather cap, grey-flecked fair stubble, knuckles cracked, right thumbnail split and black. He bars the main lane with a boar spear held level 'the way a farmer holds a gate shut against his own cattle' and tells the party 'Nobody comes in armed, and nobody comes in I don't know.' He wants them to turn back and clings to 'It's fever. Tobin says it's fever.'

## The turn
Sylvanas names Millhaven to him in objects: laid tables, a spoon in every bowl, a cradle with the blanket tucked, no one in any house, sixty prints, a child alive in a cellar saying 'everybody coughing at the well'. His thumb stops. He looks at his own street, the pump, the child coughing into a blue apron. He grounds the spear, gives his name, and puts the mage in the care of Dickon. He then walks the village with Sylvanas so his people see him with her.

## The night
He comes up the loft stair with a torch, names the boy at once ('That's Tam. He works the hoist for Joss.'), asks 'What've you done?' in a father's voice. 'He sat next to my youngest and cut her meat for her.' He locks Tam in the cage by the pound, hesitates ('In the morning. I'll have the whole of it in the morning.'), and when Sylvanas gives him until cockcrow he says 'Tell me what to shut.' Orders the granary and bakehouse shut and every sack of the man's flour burned in Joss's yard: 'I'll answer for the flour.' He stands at the yard gate marking sacks.

## The morning
Grey, sooty, with a ledger under his arm. He recovered the cart at the ford and the Hallams' flour; the alehouse had two sacks. He had the loft boards (with Jaina's ice) burned. He recognises the ANDORHAL stencil: the big granaries, three days by cart; 'My barley goes through Andorhal.' He writes all four names in the ledger and says each aloud as he writes it ('The mage. Jaina. Proudmoore.'). He does not return on page (Chapter 23: a sworn letter for the defence).
