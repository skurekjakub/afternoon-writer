# Joss, the granary keeper

Stout, flour-grey to the elbows, cap pushed back. Has kept the stone granary at the bottom of the village for years and bought from the valley farms for twenty. Three months ago a young man with a cart came from the west road with barley and flour cheaper by two coppers on the measure. Joss bought four loads, the last a fortnight ago; the man bought him a jug at the alehouse and asked after his wife's leg. 'The keeper liked him.' Asked for a face, he gave Sylvanas 'a young man with a cart. Brown hair, he thinks. Or fair.'

He hired Tam at the back end of summer and dropped the bar on the outside of the granary at supper before going home. On page he is only seen at the doorway and heard about; Brask means to question him in the morning. Whether he is a dupe or knowing is left open; Brask's phrasing ('what he knows about this lad') and the keeper's pleasure at the price lean toward dupe.
