# Jaina Proudmoore

Dalaran apprentice to Archmage Antonidas, lord admiral's daughter, and, in Ch3, 'the mage' to Sylvanas every time it is not 'Proudmoore' shouted at a well. Chapter 3 is seen entirely from Sylvanas's side, so everything Jaina does reaches the reader as something said, held, torn or shown.

## What the reader sees
She is out of the saddle at the crossing with the stirrup leather in her good hand, face tallow-coloured, begging to put her hand to the ground now ('It's strongest *here*') and obeying 'Houses first'. Her right hand is still dead, tucked up in her armpit. Her left does the work and pays for it: at the well she goes under until Sylvanas shouts her name three times and pulls the hand up, leaving four fingertips and the heel of a palm on the frost; at the storehouse the frost runs further and she leaves skin on the boards again. By the night the fingers curl halfway 'stiff as a dead bird's foot' and Cyndia wraps both hands in strips of somebody's linen.

## How she argues
The chapter's defining act: the storehouse is the loudest place in Millhaven and she will not call it the origin. 'A store is where things end up... If I tell you it started here and it came from a day's ride off, you'll ride the wrong way, and so will I.' She says 'I don't know' at the well and means it ('I told you on the road I would. I'm sorry.'). Her reading of the crossing is deliberately mixed (north lines lag, east does not, west is drowned out), so source-or-sink stays open.

## What she can do now
Listen with the heel of the staff set in a print, a floor, a horse's forefeet, and sort marks into 'Heavy.' and 'Light.' as fast as Cyndia can lay a hazel switch. That sorting turns a rain-ruined drove way into 'sixty, at least'. She is also the one who checks the cellar for anything carrying it, checks the horses (clean, and she does not understand why), and at noon the next day reports the sickness 'wider, spread out... a stain in a cloth that's been wet through' ahead of them.

## With the child
She is low-voiced, slow and very short with her questions ('Before she was ill, the sweetness? Or after?'), and keeps the turnip heap between herself and the girl. She asks the question that matters ('How did you know?') and says 'Thank you' for the shaving glass.

## Where she ends
Asleep on the mare, chin on her chest, wrapped hands slack on the pommel; waking to a staff that points south. Prisoner in the middle of the line, and the only one of the four who can say which way to ride.

## Chapter 4 (her own POV): The Sick Village

**Where she starts.** Cheerful in the saddle (she slept, she made Lyriel laugh, she has a clear head for the first time since Dalaran) until the copper taste and the ticking crystal arrive before the village does. Under the hammer and the egg-sellers she hears the drag moving among warm bodies, 'a blanket over a cough', quieter than Millhaven because 'it still had somewhere warm to go'. Right hand dead and carried 'for someone else', left hand wrapped and throbbing; she mounts and dismounts badly every time and Lyriel lifts her without comment.

**What she learns about Sylvanas.** She has the Kirin Tor speech ready for Captain Brask ('necromantic in character') and shuts her mouth when she sees the egg stall, the flushed child, every face turned to the spear. Sylvanas hands her bow to Cyndia, walks up empty-handed, concedes his point, tells him about the cradle and the spoon in the bowl, and the spear comes down. Jaina's ears go hot: she had Windrunner down as maps and had written her off on people. 'Could it be learned?' Antonidas never taught it; men lowered their spears for him 'because of the hat'. She relapses once (the window scene) and then, at dusk, gives Sylvanas the real-register report in order instead of 'small words'. From the window scene on her narration calls her Sylvanas, not Windrunner.

**The work.** In Tobin's chapel infirmary she reads patients with the staff (Lyriel holding the crystal to the body, Jaina's cheek on the ash, the plain light word). It starts in the gut. Garran, the oldest case, is everywhere. She wards them with the holding ward for flasks (blue, 'the shadow side of snow') and then goes round and round the room feeding and trimming wards. She finds the answer by interview: Tobin writes a slate, she asks everyone what they eat. Bread, one flour, dose by dose; Mother Haw (her own oats) is well, Col (oatcake all summer, two nights of bread) has a walnut. Garran lives alone and eats sops of bread in milk twice a day.

**The knot.** She cannot tie a rag on an old man's arm; Lyriel puts her hands over Jaina's, teaches the one-handed Farstrider bandage knot (loop over thumb, wrist under, tail through, set with the teeth) and calls her 'Jaina' mid-instruction for the first time. 'Again.' She ties the cooper's boy's dressing ('Did you just *bite* my bandage?'), cannot yet tie one on her own hand, and at dawn ties Garran's anew unaided. It is a silly thing to want and she wants it more than anything since Dalaran.

**The granary.** Insists on going in. Pins Tam's legs with frost when he nearly escapes, throws a shell ward over the spilled jar (a tear in the left palm, the arm ice to the shoulder) and reads it through the shell against Lyriel's 'leave it': the same stuff as in Garran, but whole and built, a stack of at least three spells, 'one part to keep, one part to wait', a third part she cannot follow, written in the death-currents grammar the Kirin Tor keep behind the grille. It is beautiful, and she wants to take it apart, and Garran has been eating it in his sops. Tam, kneeling in her frost, says he is not to speak to her. A minute later he bites a poison from his sewn-in collar and dies convulsing. She kneels beside him and reads his gut: clean.

**What it costs.** The guilt (if she had frozen his jaw; she hated doing it to a wolf; he'd have thawed and waited); the burned boards of the loft that took the third part with them; the left hand split to the heel; wards that thinned while she was down in the dark. She and Sylvanas cannot ward a village, so she asks Sylvanas to make Brask shut the baking before cockcrow. They do. At dawn Elsie's forehead is cool.

**Where she ends.** Shaking on the mare with her bandaged hand flat on its neck, in the middle of the line with two empty places at the back, the staff leaning toward the ruts of the grain road to Andorhal. 'Or where he wanted us to think it came from.'

## Chapter 5 (seen from Sylvanas's POV): The Mile-Post

Grey with fatigue, staff in the crook of the dead right arm, the wrapped left hand with two bare fingertips doing the work. She reads roads, not paper: from a mile-post at Corley's crossing she says north, west and south are heavy and old, east light and only starting, and the book's east-road counts bear her out. She will not claim more ('I can't hear paper, Ranger-General.'), draws her marks in blots with a pen in two fingers, and moves them without pride when the book corrects her (Hollow Green barred, Brent's marked; Ashford made heavy). There is no lecture.

She supplies one piece of political fact: Lord Barov's family holds the land and most of the granaries round Andorhal, and her father once pretended to listen to him talk about wheat at dinner in Boralus. At the fork she reads fifty-odd slips as one-word reads and steps outside, sets the staff between the roads, and the crystal's light leans toward the south bend while her teeth chatter and frost rises on her fingers. 'As sure as Brent's.' When Sylvanas will not take a re-read ('No.') she accepts it, and when told the army will get in the way she says 'Then I'll have to show it to them.' She laughs aloud at Lyriel's joke about a loop on the seven. Lyriel calls her 'Jaina'; Sylvanas, privately, begins to.

## Chapter 6 (her own POV, absolute): The Pattern

**The click.** On the south road under the hills, the same late hitch comes up through the mare and the staff on every bend, and at the culvert it matches what she felt at Millhaven with the staff heel in a dead man's footprint. Corley's book, the slips, Garran's bowl, the jar and the walking dead lock into one reading: not luck, not a fever, not one clever man with a cart. 'It's one thing... It comes in on the wagons and walks out on its own feet.' She tells Sylvanas plainly and admits the gap: she cannot say who built it or how a knot under the breastbone becomes feet walking after death.

**The fear.** The work is built the way Dalaran taught her to lay a ward, every seam closed, and it uses the craft kept behind the library grille. She runs down faces of masters, senior students and the ones who left and never wrote, stops at one, and makes herself look at the road. It stays unnamed. She also wants to see the keeping part work just once; she calls it study, knows what it is, and is sick over the side of the mare.

**Strahnbrad.** She goes into the camp certain a clean case will move Arthas, and that Sylvanas has been too hard on a prince she never met. She is lifted down by Lyriel, takes the sheet from Sylvanas in two bare fingertips, and meets Arthas with her old teasing voice. He believes her ('I don't doubt you', 'I believe you') and sends her to write it down for his father, because the Garrow farm is burning and he cannot see her grain. He never takes the paper. The internment-camp night (the gap under the watchtower, the female humming to her infant) runs in her head; she asks if she was unfair to him for years, answers 'No.', and keeps it from Sylvanas. She tells Sylvanas aloud 'You were right. At the fork.' No thanks, no warmth.

**The road in.** She answers Sylvanas's question about the tally-man with 'Nothing but a tired clerk', then wonders whether she tried. The drag collapses on her between the walls of the approach: teeth chattering in the heat, frost on the bare fingertips, a nosebleed, the crystal sinking to a stone grain. She refuses to get down, because stopping is waiting for a letter and a council again, and rides on. At the crest, Andorhal is working, bread and hoists and a man singing, and under it the drag beating so hard she feels it in her teeth. 'The market gate.' Nobody behind her is coming.


## Chapter 7 (seen from Sylvanas's POV)

At the market gate she answers the clerk flatly, one word at a time, holding the Farstrider pass up in two bare fingertips with her jaw shut, blood dried in her nose, and cuts off the clerk's question about the Ranger-General with 'My escort.' At the militia post she names the tally-man, tells Corin 'Nobody sent me', asks who he wrote to ('Did anyone answer?' 'No.'; her fingertips go white on the staff) and asks to put her hand on the flagged samples. She goes off with Corin and Lyriel to the sample room (offstage) and reads every shelf.

She returns grey to the lips, the crystal dead, frost on her linen on a warm night. She tried to write the results and got blots, so she dictated and Corin wrote: bags on copied marks are heavy, on true marks clean. The poison is a coat too thin to see, worked through the grain by hand after dressing and before sewing; on its own it does little, and what it carries wakes inside whoever eats the bread. Whoever mixed it knows alchemy and necromancy both. With Sylvanas's roof account they have the hands. She asks Havel what is left in the loft when his men close the row, steps up beside Sylvanas before being asked and sets the staff down at her shoulder, says the loft is wet tonight and she can still read it, and offers to freeze one man's jaw before he can bite his collar: her fingers on his skin, once tonight.
