# Cyndia

Post officer from the Thalassian Pass, now Sylvanas's left-hand escort and her best tracker. Auburn braid, even weight, an arrow on the string. She calls Sylvanas 'Ranger-General' every time and is called 'Cyndia', curt.

## Chapter 3
She reports in flat lists and will not hand over a number she may have to take back. In the dark lane behind Millhaven she reads boots, clogs and bare feet by a hooded lantern, finds one man who dragged his right toe, and still says 'I'd rather not give you a number and then take it back.' At the cut at first light she opens skeptically ('It's a drove way... Half of this will be cattle') and then, with two hazel switches laid a man's step apart and the mage calling heavy and light, puts her name to sixty, four and five abreast, 'closer than men would'. Her voice drops and slows as the count turns to alarm.

She gives the girl her water skin, wraps the mage's hands, and keeps her bow up on the carter after the others lower theirs. She turns her head after the wagon when he says 'May the stars keep your road' and holds it there a moment. She says nothing; she is the second witness to the grain-man exchange and the farewell.

## Chapter 4
Left-hand escort again. She notices the three shuttered houses before Jaina does ('Middle of the day.'), takes Sylvanas's bow when she gets down to face Brask's spear, and works the granary and bakehouse with Sylvanas: the report she delivers at dusk is the supply chain in order (new man, three months, west road, cheaper by two coppers on the measure, four loads, the last a fortnight ago; the bakehouse takes everything from the granary). She notes the keeper dropped the bar from outside, so the light in the loft means someone who came in another way; opens the unbarred side door; holds the stair head; cuts Tam's sleeve free. At night she turns up with the cold on her cloak and oatcakes from Mother Haw's own field for Garran, 'She wants the cloth back.' She says nothing about the carter.

## Chapter 5
Left flank still, and the party's counter. She counts carts at Corley's gate, the short-rate lines by road (north nine and three, west seven and four, south eleven and six, east one and nine), the places (thirty-one, then forty-seven, nineteen in more than one book) and the burned slips (fifty-three readable, nine with the end burned away; 'Barov on thirty-one'). She reads the river depot's raked yard, the rope-marks on the crane posts and 'on purpose'; crouches into the clay ruts on the Brill road and reports no heavy wagon since the rain; reads the overseer's trap tracks south; walks the south road to the first bridge in the dark and returns with the army-wagon shoe (wide flat, heavy heel: the King's post horses she has seen at the Pass). She watches the left-hand trees while Jaina laughs.

## Chapter 6 (seen from Jaina's POV)
Left flank, silent. She watches an empty army wagon's team's feet, 'the wide flat shoes heavy at the heel', until it passes; takes Sylvanas's mare's rein at the Strahnbrad hedge and draws the horses in, watching the soldiers' hands; speaks into her horse's ear when it will not settle near Andorhal. On the road she goes by the tally-man on his left and gives no sign on the page of the farewell (her Ch3 knowledge is held for Ch9).


## Chapter 7

Sylvanas's counter and Havel-minder in Andorhal. At the gate she counts five watchers, four of them on the wagons (the man on the wall has not looked at the road since they joined the line). At the post she asks Havel where the loaders sleep. In Fenner's yard she gives Sylvanas the count of eight, with positions, and stays with Havel (asking which end of the rope-walk they unlock first) while Sylvanas tails the runner. At the post she reports that the crew went in at the far door, the pair from the trough last, and that three from the gate went up Hurdle Lane toward Tenter Row. She is given the roofs across from Kett's loft with the order not to loose until Sylvanas calls. No farewell reaction anywhere; the Ch9 hold stands.
