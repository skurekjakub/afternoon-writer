# Garran

Old man, a widower since the spring, living alone in a cottage on Cooper's Lane, three doors from the cooper's house. No teeth: Tobin took the last at Midsummer, so he lived on 'sops', bread broken small in milk, morning and night, sent up from the bakehouse by Ellen. He fainted first of anyone in the village, 'since before market'. Tobin bled him the day before and 'it did him no good at all'.

In the chapel he lies on a child's truckle bed with his feet over the end in grey stockings, face like tallow, belly hard under the sheepskin. Jaina's light finds him everywhere: liver dark and swollen, lungs, the great vessels of the neck, both arms to the fingers, every brown thread running back to the gut. His ward takes the most holding and is the first to thin. He is the 'late' case that proves the dose is cumulative; he is also the case that disproves contact (he lived alone). Because of him Jaina asks what they eat.

At dawn he is the same: 'His breath still caught at the top, and caught, and caught, his skin dry and hot as a stove', the brown no further than in the deep of the night. His bandage slips again and Jaina re-ties it unaided. Mother Haw's oatcakes in milk are his new sops.
