# Sylvanas Windrunner

Ranger-General of Quel'Thalas. Chapter 3 is the first time the reader is inside her head since the Ch1 cell, and the interior voice is exactly what the voice sheet promises: blunt, procedural, self-interrogating, swearing privately ('Sun's sake', 'Damn her, then'), checking herself with short imperatives ('Don't. Find out.', 'Leave it.').

## Body and kit
Tall, sleepless, wet at the knees from the ditch, the doe's blood dried brown in her knuckles. Eyes 'full of sand'; by the morning ride the road goes 'very flat and very bright' for her. Right hand bare for stones and, now, for ground: she pulls the glove off with her teeth to read the drove-way grooves by fingertip, but she reads edges and depth, never magic. Seals with a leaf-and-arrow signet ring (the wax slid and blurred on one side). Carries a belt knife and a worn stub of lead that fails on oiled paper.

## How she thinks
She works by drill, and when a place has no drill she says so to herself ('What drill did she have for a drawknife set down tidy?'). A sacked steading she can read from the gate; Millhaven, where people got up from breakfast, supper and a sickbed at different hours and walked out, makes her count what is missing instead of what is there. She keeps catching herself reaching for the doe as an explanation ('Don't make them into the doe because it's late and you want a story to put on it') and refuses to guess aloud ('I don't know. Not on this ground. Not in the dark.'), even when she badly wants to say north.

She does not trust a child's word through a grating, so she holds the warning overnight, sits the inn gallery past her watch, and goes to the cut at first light. Her dry contempt for the Convocation ('Let him count it. She had sixty in a cow way.') is the closest she comes to feeling the weight.

## With the mage
Aloud: 'Mage.' Twice at the well, in alarm, 'Proudmoore.' In her head always 'the mage' (and 'the prisoner' when she is thinking about custody). She expected a scholar to grab the loudest answer; the mage's refusal ('a store is where things end up') irritates her and she accepts it ('Damn her, then. The hand.'). She forbids more hand-reading that night, takes the staff once and feels only a tremor in a stick, and by noon on the ride south she is telling herself 'No. On the trail. The read was the trail.' That sentence is the chapter's self-deception.

## With the child
Awkward. One flat question ('Which is the north road?') makes the girl flinch. She gives the room to Neral, stands half in the door, turns away from the tenderness, and sends the child north with a sneer at her own motives ('Was she sending the child north to safety, or north to be somebody else's problem? Both.').

## Where she ends
Four horses south instead of seven ('She had sent them back up the road herself'). Duty done by any measure the chamber would seal, and she is watering her horse in Lordaeron, facing south anyway. Exhausted, uneasy about the gaps in her line, with the carter's 'May the stars keep your road' already filed under 'Leave it'.

## Chapter 4 (seen from Jaina's POV)

**At the spear.** Brask bars the lane with a boar spear. Sylvanas swings down without hurry, hands her bow to Cyndia and walks up with nothing in her hands. 'You're right to stop us.' Pitched for his ear alone, she tells him Millhaven in objects (the laid tables, a spoon in every bowl, the cradle with the blanket tucked, sixty prints, a child in a cellar) and lets the thumb on the shaft stop. 'Your people are watching you, Captain. Keep your spear. Walk with me.' She never raises her voice. Jaina watches it and asks 'Could it be learned?'.

**Her road.** While Jaina reads the sick, Sylvanas goes straight to the long stone granary and the bakehouse beside it: price, supplier, since when, who else buys. She takes a pinch of flour and writes. By dusk her left glove is grey with it. She has the new man's description (young, civil, good horse, brown or fair; the keeper 'gave me a young man with a cart') and the loads; Cyndia reports, she asks 'How sure?' of Jaina. When Jaina half-says 'Somebody is selling it to them on--' she finishes 'On purpose.'

**The granary.** She leaves nothing to chance: nobody takes another sack out of the loft, 'Then we're quick.' On the stair she goes first, arrow on the string, planks making no sound. When Tam goes for the lamp she pins his sleeve to a post with a single arrow, takes the lamp, winds the wick to a bead and grinds out the burning oil. At the cage, when he convulses, she takes the key from Brask and kneels on the stone with her bare fingers in his mouth until there is nothing more to stay for (two of them end with deep teeth marks). She finds the waxed paper sewn into his collar: 'Somebody taught him that. They sewed it in for him and told him when.'

**Shutting the grain.** Jaina's warning ('I can ward a room. I can't ward a village.') is passed to Brask in the same low voice: 'Then you have until cockcrow.' She goes after him without waiting to see what he does. Very late she stands on the chapel threshold: 'It's burned. All of the granary. All of the bakehouse.' In the morning she shows Jaina and Brask a square of sacking she cut from the loft before the fire: a sheaf in a ring, ANDORHAL. 'Either way, it's the road he drove.' She turns onto the grain road south and west without a word.

**What changes.** Jaina's narration starts calling her 'Sylvanas' mid-chapter, which Sylvanas never sees. She still says only 'Mage.' Her first act of trust is the open one: 'Let my mage see your sick.'

## Chapter 5 (her own POV again): The Paper Road

**Where she starts.** Three days without proper sleep, knees set to the saddle, two bitten fingers purple and slow, a square of ANDORHAL sacking against her ribs that her hand keeps finding. At Corley's depot she splits the party (Cyndia at the gate, Lyriel with her, the mage to the crossing) and gets the out-book out of a frightened clerk by laying her evidence beside his rule. She finds 'own cart' short-rate loads since midsummer and, with the mage's roads on one side and her names on the other, builds a joint sheet that does not agree with itself: the book says thirty-one places, the mage says the east road is only starting, and the book's east road has nine short-rate loads in the month.

**What she learns.** She thought she was following one man with a cart. 'One man with a cart didn't sell grain short at forty-odd villages on four roads in one season. That was somebody with yards and books and boys.' Someone is a day ahead at every door (the miller's buy-back, the ostler's southbound carter), and in the dark wood she decides not to stop at the Plough.

**The mistake.** At the swept river depot she lets a pleasant, terrified overseer send her north on the Brill road with a Tolliver-and-Sons story because 'every breath was a furlong'. She reads the live stove as Corley's kettle. The slumped ruts and the toll-keeper tell her there were never any wagons. She names it the same excuse she once flayed a ranger for, and rides back through a second sleepless night. The stove ash, the shawl on the stair, the wagon-board bench at the fork finish the lesson.

**Jaina.** She waits for the mage's lecture and does not get one. At the fork she sits Jaina on the bench, notices her picking frost off a finger and reading the map upside down, and thinks 'Jaina, then.' This is the first time Sylvanas's mind uses the name; aloud she still says 'Mage' and 'Proudmoore'. She pins Strahnbrad into the bench with her belt knife, refuses to let Jaina re-read the road ('No.'), and has Jaina's word and nothing else for the south road.

**Open items for her.** The Barov name on the paper (she wants it to be him; she holds back). The old carter's empty wagon, resurfaced by the grain-dust bench: 'An empty wagon, going the wrong way', unmatched. Who tells the doors ahead of her.

## Chapter 6 (seen from Jaina's POV only)
**At the camp.** She tests Jaina's plain statement ('Somebody.') and presses on its one weak joint: what do you say to a man who cannot feel any of it? She tells Jaina to make it short because Arthas has orcs. At the crest she reads Strahnbrad at a soldier's speed (the big tent his, riders out and back east, pickets doubled east, 'on this side, one sergeant and a hedge'). She gives her full rank and name to the sergeant, hands Jaina the sheet at the stirrup, and stands behind her. While Arthas can still prove her wrong she says what he will do: 'He's going to write to his father, Proudmoore, and then he's going back up that hill.' He studies her and says 'Yes. I am.' Afterwards: 'Give it here, Mage.' and the verdict, 'He's a good commander, with an enemy he can see and men who'd follow him into it. He'll fight that war, and likely win it.' 'And the other one?' 'Is ours.' She does not ask about the history and does not look smug.

**On the road.** She stops a tally-man on foot and asks who runs the granaries. When he sends them off with 'May the stars keep your road', she turns right round in the saddle, one hand on her horse's rump, and asks Jaina what she read off him. Jaina says nothing but a tired clerk. By the time she looks back he is gone. 'I had the same one at Millhaven, from an old carter going north over the bridge... Once, it's a farewell. Twice, it's wrong. That's as far as it goes yet.' She calms her shying horse in Thalassian and, at the crest, turns to Jaina and waits. Aloud she says 'Mage' to Jaina and 'Proudmoore' in front of Arthas or the sergeant.


## Chapter 7 (own POV, Andorhal)

**The gate.** She waits in the queue on foot, horses left in a carter's yard outside the wall. She wrote the pass herself on the lid of an ostler's feed bin, under her own name, rank and leaf-and-arrow seal (no other name would get a human mage and three bows through a gate). She reads the gate as ground: a chest-high field-stone wall with no ward-stones, because 'every cart still had to stop at the man with the book'. The clerk's nib chatters; she cannot tell a tired man from a paid one. The ground is made of paper. A man on a barrel chalks a count of every wagon that turns toward the granaries; she walks on without looking back.

**The post.** She runs the questioning in short questions that work as orders. Havel's street truth and Corin's paper truth arrive in two halves; she notes that Havel has watched drilled men for a summer and written it down, where she would have shut the rope-walk the first night. She sees Havel steer her down Hurdle Lane from behind, 'so it's covered'. She splits the work by skill: 'Proudmoore. The sample room. Take him.' She sends Jaina with Corin and Lyriel and takes Cyndia and Havel; it had been easier at Corley's with a shout between them.

**The district.** She reads Fenner's yard: eight men placed as she would have placed them, the trained waiting she last saw in Tam. She lets the dead drop sit in the wall (empty crack, eight gone by morning) and follows the runner alone: Tenter Row, a wagon's wheels for cover, the roofs, a chimney stack across from Kett's loft. She sees five masked men work a black crock into open sacks with their arms, wash to the elbow, drink with faces turned away. A grit slide, a man at the window looking for a long time, and her flat stillness on the slates. She leaves on the Exchange bell.

**The table.** She says 'I have the hands' and sketches Kett's beside Corin's column of *Heavy*. She holds her tongue about Havel (a documented bias: she is sick of slow men; the Convocation would love him; and the Dalaran boy has been right every time) and lets the cart-timing argument carry the room. 'Tonight.' Jaina steps up beside her before she can ask. She assigns Cyndia the roofs and Lyriel the Tenter Row bend, and notes she is spreading what is left of the detail so thin she can see light through it. She tells Corin to stay behind Proudmoore, pinches out the candle, and the chapter ends minutes before the capture.
