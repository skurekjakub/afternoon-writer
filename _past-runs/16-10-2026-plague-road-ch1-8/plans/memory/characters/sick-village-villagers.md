# Sick-village villagers

The working morning when the party rides in: a hammer on iron, a woman calling a price for eggs and another calling it lower, geese in a yard, bread smoke lying low and brown, two boys after a hoop. Under it, coughs: a man in a doorway, someone upstairs behind a shutter, two women at the pump. Three houses with shutters up in the middle of the day, a horseshoe over a lintel with its points down, a stool with a cup left on the step.

Patients in the chapel (every bed in the village crowded round a brazier): Garran (see his entry), Elsie (see hers), the cooper's boy of ten and his mother (a fist and more; three more in the house, a bed kept for each), Col the smith's striker (a walnut; oatcake all summer then two nights of bread), Wenna Dunmore of the top farm (carried in by her husband Hew and another man on a sheep hurdle, fighting, talking about the hens; barley black in the ear, store flour since, eats the heel of each loaf standing up) and a little girl in a man's coat who sat by the font. Elsewhere: Mag, about five or six, flushed with a small wet cough, hanging on her blue-aproned mother at the egg stall; Piers, the Aldon boys and the Hallams. At dawn Col is perfectly well and starving, the cooper's boy wants bread, Elsie is cool, Wenna's forehead is a sleeper's warmth, Garran is unchanged. Mag is never read on page.
