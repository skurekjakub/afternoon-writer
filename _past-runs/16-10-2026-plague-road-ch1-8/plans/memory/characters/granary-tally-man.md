# The granary tally-man (unnamed)

Chapter 6, afternoon on the Andorhal road. A man in his middle years, going grey above the ears, with ink on one cuff and a ledger satchel whose strap has worn a long pale stripe across his coat. He walks out of Andorhal on foot while every cart goes in, and Sylvanas stops him by putting her horse across his path. He half steps back and covers the buckle of his satchel, then eases when Jaina asks politely.

He is helpful, wry and exact. Who runs the granaries? Depends who you ask, and all three will sit you on a bench till the lamps are lit. Whom should they see? Captain Havel, militia, at the post just inside the market gate on the left with a bench outside the door; he knows the granary district better than the factors, 'and they'd sooner not hear it'. Directions: keep straight on at the bottom of the hill where the carts bear left for the yards. He looks back once toward the granaries as he talks.

As Jaina passes he says 'May the stars keep your road' and walks on. Sylvanas turns round in the saddle at it, asks Jaina whether she read anything off him, and gets 'Nothing but a tired clerk' (Jaina had not turned the staff on him; his residue was the road's, like the carters'). By the time Sylvanas looks back he is lost in the traffic. She tells Jaina it is the same farewell she had from an old carter at Millhaven: once it is a farewell, twice it is wrong, and that is as far as it goes yet.

**Writing note.** He must stay forgettable on the surface and must not resemble the Ch3 carter. His face is never described beyond age, grey, ink. Any later appearance should keep the satchel and pale strap as the only anchor.
