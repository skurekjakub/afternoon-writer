# Prince Arthas

## Chapter 5: referenced only
Road talk at a south-road inn and at a toll-bar: he has his army at Strahnbrad, burning out orcs from the camps, with soldiers on every hill road, remounts and oats taken at the King's price. Sylvanas thinks of him as 'Terenas's boy' and expects his army to rank grain below orcs and the quartermaster's supper. Never on page; the chapter ends with the party entering the road that runs toward his war.

## Chapter 6: on the page (seen only from Jaina's POV)
**Look and voice.** Broad now, a man with a hammer where there was a practice sword; sweat-dark hair at the temples, gold elsewhere, armour polished by someone else and greaves plastered brown. He crosses the camp at a long stride with officers hurrying behind and laughs out loud at the sight of her: 'Jaina! Light's sake. Jaina Proudmoore, on my hill. Did Antonidas finally throw you out?' He teases, listens with his arms folded and his weight set, and is courteous to Sylvanas ('Ranger-General. You're a long way from home.' with a small correct bow from the neck).

**What he does with the warning.** He hears all of it. He puts one gloved finger on the knife slit in the map ('That's us.'), turns a Barov seal and jokes about the family, leaves the sheet in her hand and says 'Jaina. I don't doubt you.' Each time she presses, his eyes go past her shoulder to the smoke on the eastern hills. When Sylvanas calls it behind her ('He's going to write to his father, Proudmoore, and then he's going back up that hill') he studies her long and steady, does not snap, and says 'Yes. I am.' He turns Jaina by the shoulder to face the valley: the burning Garrow farm, old Garrow, his daughter, her two boys, who wanted to hold his hammer; he helped carry them out yesterday. 'They came out of the camps, Jaina... I can see that from my tent door. I can't see your grain.' 'I believe you.' He tells her to write it down for his father, he will send it tonight with his own rider, and he will be in Andorhal when the hills are quiet. He says he is sorry, squeezes her dead arm, orders food and water, and goes up the lane to a rider with the helmet on. He never takes the paper.

**How to write him.** Warm, quick, sure. Never cartoon cruelty. His reasons are real and visible, and the orc dead are real. He turns the conversation with a gesture, not a speech, and uses kindness the way a shield is used. Jaina's memory of the internment camp (the crawl through the bracken, 'beasts, Jaina, you'll see, that's why there are fences', the female humming to the infant with its fist in her tusk) shows he was as sure then as now.
