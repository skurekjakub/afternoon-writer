# Lyriel

The ranger who talks. Right flank on the ride, one hand never far from the mage's mare. Quiet, dry, courteous; 'Lady Proudmoore' to the mage in Ch2 and not addressed to her on page in Ch3.

## Chapter 3
She steadies the mage's panicking mare without a word, sweeps the east side with Cyndia and reports one detail that lands hardest because it is small: 'a cradle... with a blanket in it and no baby... The blanket's been tucked.' She holds the inn gallery and then the bridge; she takes the first watch.

She is also the one who answers the old carter. Warm, easy, and true: 'We passed nobody, friend... The little one there spoke of a newer grain seller, though, so we'll keep an eye out for him going south, and if we see him we'll tell him you're looking. You'll get a better price going home full than going home empty, mind.' Cyndia's bow stays up, but Lyriel's reads as simple road courtesy; nothing on the page suggests carelessness or suspicion on her part.

On the ride south she moves up to the mage's knee without being told and hooks two fingers through the cheek-strap while the mage sleeps; at noon she holds the stirrup.

## Chapter 4
Right flank still, and now Jaina's hands. She steadies the mare ('Steady,' to the horse or to her), takes Jaina's weight at dismount, and in the chapel holds the staff's crystal to every patient, braced for it to bite ('If it bites me, Lady Proudmoore, I'm letting go.'). When Jaina cannot tie a rag on an old man's arm Lyriel crosses the bed without a sound, puts her hands over Jaina's, and teaches the one-handed Farstrider knot: loop over the thumb, wrist under, tail through, set it with the teeth. 'Go on, Jaina. Bite it. It won't bite you back.' That is the first 'Jaina' any ranger says; Jaina notices at once. 'All of us. In the first year. You do it with the left until you can, and then the right... so we can patch our own mistakes.'

She pushes Jaina up the loft stair when Sylvanas would have left her at the water butt, tells her 'Jaina, leave it' when she keeps reading the shell, covers the dead boy's face with her spare cloak, runs her up Chapel Lane to the wards, and in the deep of the night cuts off the black-red linen on Jaina's torn palm and rebinds it with the knot ('Don't let Tobin see that. He'll want to bleed you as well.'). At dawn she grins at the knot Jaina ties on Garran's arm; in Brask's ledger she asks that one of them be written down as tying a good knot, glancing at Cyndia.

## Chapter 5
The party's talker. At Corley's she gets the Ranger-General's name onto the table and a clerk to open his book by joking about his stove ('It's like standing in a boot.'); she admires a miller's slate, a hamlet woman's notch-stick and a toll-keeper's beans and gets answers out of all three. She rides at Jaina's knee in the dark, and after the mare goes down on the causeway rides with her fist in the back of Jaina's coat. Her joke about the clerk's looped g's ('paid by the loop, Jaina') makes Jaina laugh aloud for the first time on the road. She says 'Jaina' aloud, as in Ch4. At the dawn start she saves Jaina the burnt end of the bread.

## Chapter 6 (seen from Jaina's POV)
Quiet and practical. She turns round at Jaina's knee when Jaina says the plague is one made thing; lifts Jaina down and up at every stop ('as she had at every stop since the sick village'); hands over half an apple at the flat below Strahnbrad ('half for you and half for her... she's walked through more mud today than a Silvermoon gardener sees in his life', meaning the mare). On the Andorhal approach she says 'Jaina. Your nose.', offers to hold the mare while Jaina sits on the wall, and when refused puts her hand over Jaina's on the reins until the mare stops fighting the bit. She does not press, and she shows no reaction at all to the tally-man's farewell.


## Chapter 7

Quiet and working. Lyriel lifts Jaina down in a carter's yard outside the wall, stands behind her at the gate watching the man on the wall, slides the pass inside Jaina's coat when she might fumble it, and stays in the post doorway facing out. She reports the lamplighter and the chalker packing up. She goes with Jaina and Corin to the sample room, a hand under the elbow, stopping every time Jaina stops. Back at the post she holds the shutter gap and counts: seven loaded carts down the lane and one empty coming back with a sleeping carter. Sylvanas gives her the bend in Tenter Row; she answers 'Mine.' Alive; no jokes; no foreshadowing.
