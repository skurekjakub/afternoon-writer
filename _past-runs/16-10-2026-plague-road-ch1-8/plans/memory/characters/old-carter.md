# The old carter (unnamed)

Comes up over the hump of the Millhaven bridge at a walk on a flat plank-sided farm wagon behind a mud-caked brown cob. Weathered, a nose broken once and set crooked, a hat he takes off at the sight of the empty street. His wagon is empty, with old grain dust packed grey into the cracks between the planks ('Empty going up, full coming down').

## What the page shows
He says 'Morning' to the bows, then looks past them, and the shock arrives slowly ('mouth went slack first, and then the rest of him caught up'). 'Where's everybody?' 'Millhaven? I was through here on market day. They were well enough then.' He gives Sylvanas a plain, useful picture of the south (market at the ford busy yesterday, smoke in every chimney, barley up a copper). He comes up for the mill every fortnight. He asks whether they passed a young fellow with a grain cart, a new man; Lyriel answers warmly and truly; he laughs once through his nose ('That's the truth'), takes in the line of horses once from front to back, notes the mage's mare sitting 'in the gap', and drives on, saying 'May the stars keep your road.' Sylvanas marks the blessing as odd (not Light, not 'Safe road') and files it under 'Leave it'; Cyndia turns her head after the wagon.

## Chapter 5 (off page, in Sylvanas's memory)
At the fork the wagon-board bench, grain dust packed hard in its cracks, makes Sylvanas recall him: the empty plank-sided wagon, 'Nothing for me here, then', and that he did not turn round at the dead mill but drove straight on north, as if he had business further up. He asked after the new man. She tries him beside the river depot and beside the young man with the cart and he fits neither 'except that he had asked after him.' 'An empty wagon, going the wrong way.' Filed unmatched. (Prose still gives no proof; she says 'One odd old man on a road.')

## Caution for later chapters
The prose frames him as ordinary and forgettable. Do not retroactively add suspicion on Sylvanas's side. Project notes (not the prose) make him the Mal'Ganis plant; keep the plant observable-only. Note the one detail a later chapter may want: his statement that Millhaven was 'well enough' on market day is not reconciled on the page with Sylvanas's 'Dead. Five days.'

## Chapter 6 (referenced only)
Sylvanas names him to Jaina after the Andorhal tally-man's farewell: 'I had the same one at Millhaven, from an old carter going north over the bridge.' Jaina does not remember what was said at Millhaven. No resemblance between the two men is drawn on the page.
