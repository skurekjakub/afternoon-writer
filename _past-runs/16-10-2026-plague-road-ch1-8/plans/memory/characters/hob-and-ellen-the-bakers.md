# Hob, Nan and Ellen (the bakehouse)

Hob is the village baker, a big soft man white to the eyebrows, who lights his long brick oven 'by cockcrow, him and Nan'. His bakehouse takes all its flour from the granary next door. Ellen (fifteen or sixteen, round-faced, flour to the elbows) is the girl with the basket: she brings warm loaves to Tobin's chapel and, for three months, sent a loaf up to Garran's cottage every day. 'Mam says how are they, and is there anything else.' She backs off the chapel step at the sight of the lit staff and the elf, leaving a pale flour print on the doorframe that Jaina notices when she wipes her hand. (It is inferred but not stated that Nan is Ellen's mother and Hob's wife.)

They are the chain's last link: innocent. Jaina's warning is that Hob's oven mustn't be lit again; the bakehouse chimney stands 'black and cold against the glow' at dawn, 'for the first time all day', and Hob stands in the yard next door with a fork, watching the heap burn.
