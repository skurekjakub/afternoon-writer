# The flour supply chain and Andorhal

A young, civil man with a cart came from the west road three months ago and sold the granary keeper flour two coppers cheaper on the measure; four loads, the last a fortnight ago. The bakehouse bakes it, the village eats it. The loft held most of the last load in new sacks stencilled with a sheaf in a ring and ANDORHAL. Sylvanas cut a square of it and keeps it. Brask: Andorhal is the big southern granaries, three days with a cart; everything in the south goes through it. Jaina doubts it ('Or where he wanted us to think it came from'). Sylvanas: 'Either way, it's the road he drove.'

The chapter ends with the party turning onto the grain road after him. No face, name or plan for the seller yet.

Chapter 5: the chain turns into a network. Corley's out-book: 'own cart' short-rate loads since midsummer (31 places; by road before/since: north 9/3, west 7/4, south 11/6, east 1/9). A table across two days: 47 places, 19 in more than one book. The river depot's burned slips: 53 readable, all with Andorhal at one end, Barov on 31. Jaina's marks cluster on the south road, older toward the bottom. Sylvanas: not one man but 'somebody with yards and books and boys'. Status: advanced.

## Chapter 6
Jaina lays the chain before Arthas (grain, mills, bread, Millhaven, the depot slips, Andorhal at one end, the road worst) and asks for the Andorhal granaries to be shut; he does not. The south road below Strahnbrad fills with grain carts and each farm lane adds to the drag. Andorhal is a working granary city. The tally-man says granary authority is split between factors, corn guild and town, and that Captain Havel of the militia knows the district best.

Chapter 7: the stencilled ANDORHAL sacks are real and in use at Kett's loft; the new factor's grain went to Fenner's and the Exchange (same week at high summer), briefly Garside's, Kett's and the weir-end malt-house, and leaves on other yards' copied marks (forty-one bills). The factor is a name on contracts nobody at the Exchange has met.
