# Andorhal

**First sight (Chapter 6).** From the crest of the rise the city fills the shallow valley from one side to the other: a long low grey wall, roofs packed close behind it, chimney smoke going up from every one. Above the roofs stand the granaries, tall square timber buildings on stone feet, each with a hoist beam jutting out under the gable. On the nearest a sack goes up on its rope while a man leans out of the loft door to take it. There are more beyond, a whole quarter of them, and a haze of grain dust lies over that quarter with the low sun turning it gold. Wagons stand in lines on the roads in. The one on this road runs down to the smaller (market) gate. A longer one on a broad straight road to the right runs to a bigger gate: the King's road. The noise comes up the hill: iron tyres on cobbles, a bell, a dog, a mill wheel, a man singing a rude song badly. Beneath the noise the drag beats slow and late, so strong Jaina feels it in her teeth.

**On the ground (Chapter 7).** The city is working and it is made of paper. The wall at the market gate is chest-high field stone with no ward-stone in it; the defence is a clerk with a book under the arch, a board of wax seals with each yard's name inked under it, and guards who spike every sack since 'the Exchange said'. Grain trickles off the tailboards into cobbles already pale with it, every crack packed full. The whole gate smells of warm grain, dry and sweet, like bread before it goes in the oven. A second count sits on top of the gate's: a man on a barrel at the head of the granary lane chalking every wagon that turns down it.

Inside, the carts bear left down a broad lane to the granaries; the militia post is a squat stone house on the left, lamp lit, a bench by the door. Behind the yards run blind loading lanes and Hurdle Lane; beyond them the dyers' Tenter Row. The Exchange bell rules the shifts. The mage quarter, the inspection offices and the sample room are across the city, behind doors that open to a magister's ring (offstage in Chapter 7). The dust is thick and shows prints at the edges of the cobbles; the night is warm and still, with no wet streets.

Local authority is the Exchange, the town council, the magistrate (who frees Havel's arrests by dinner), the factors and the guild. Havel's twelve militiamen walk between the gate and Mill Lane. Sickness runs along Mill Lane (Holt's, Fenner's, the Exchange granary, Garside's) and the streets that buy bread from the bakers at its foot.
