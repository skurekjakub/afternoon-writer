# The big depot on the river road

A town's worth of grain store on the bank of a river: three tile-roofed barns on stone footings, a wharf and timber crane, a weigh-house beam over the gate that could lift a loaded wagon, stables, an office, a yard for twenty carts. This is where Andorhal loads are broken for the whole side of the river.

**The first visit (grey dawn).** Gate open, nobody about. The barns have been swept to the corners and the cobwebs brushed off the tie-beams; pale rectangles on the floorboards show where sack-stacks stood for years; the yard is raked, the stables mucked to the stone, the wharf swept. The only warm room is the little brick office, where a stove burns with its ash pan half out and a red curl of ash still glowing. The overseer behind the tidy desk (five pens trimmed to one length) answers every question before it is finished: books gone to the regional office, six wagons of grain sold whole to Tolliver and Sons of Brill, two days ago, north.

**The second visit (the next dawn).** Gate chained and padlocked. The house is empty and run from in a hurry: back door knocking, drawers upside down, a woman's shawl on the stair, the washing still on the line. The stove is cold, and in its ash pan is the proof: burned waybills, a wad of crammed paper, broken red seals. Cyndia fetches the ash pit's contents in a bucket.

Sensory keys: grain-dust swept clean, the river under the floorboards, the lavender smell of the press upstairs, honey from the beeswax candle Sylvanas takes from the kitchen floor.
