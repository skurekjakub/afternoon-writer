# Chapel infirmary

A squat grey Light-chapel with a bell-cote and no bell, benches upside down in the yard under a yew like dead beetles. Inside it is dim, close and too warm, every shutter barred but one: a round of greenish bubbled glass above the altar with a single pane wiped clear, looking down the lane at the granary. A brazier smokes where the benches were, and round it, sides touching, stands every bed in the village that could be carried. Blankets of every colour. Vinegar, sweat, tallow, the slop bucket, and under them 'pears gone brown'. Someone says 'Mam, Mam, Mam' in a small flat voice. A cracked slate hangs on a nail inside the door with Tobin's list of teeth and debts; it becomes the epidemiological slate.

Through the night the blue wards over each bed thin and are re-fed. By dawn after rain the early cases are 'more than holding', one is cool, and the oldest is unchanged.
