# Fenner's yard and the rope-walk

A beaten-earth yard behind Fenner's granary at the foot of Hurdle Lane. Along one side stands the old rope-walk, long and low and windowless, a door at each end with a hasp and padlock on each: the sleeping shed of the loaders hired in at high summer. There is a trough, a hoist platform with an outside ladder, a gate, a cooper's door across the yard (one of Havel's lads), and a boy who goes round lighting lanterns on their hooks at dusk.

The day crew comes off: most of the men sit on sacks with caps off or stand arching their backs with fists in them. Eight do not. Two at the near corner of the shed, a clear foot off the wall; a pair at the trough drinking one at a time; a man on the hoist who stops coiling; three by the gate, sitting but with their feet under them. They hold the yard, both shed ends and the mouth of the lane. At the Exchange bell the crew lock themselves into the rope-walk. Yard-reeve Coll Simmons is frightened and will not say so.
