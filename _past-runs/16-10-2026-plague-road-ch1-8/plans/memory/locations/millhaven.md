# Millhaven

First human village of any size south of the Pass, in a long shallow fold of land where the road from the Pass meets an east-west road. A few dozen low thatched houses, gardens behind them, lanes between the garden walls. A stream runs along the bottom of the fold under a stone bridge; a mill and a stone storehouse sit by the bridge; the inn stands at the crossing, with a yard, an outside stair and a gallery above its door. At the dead middle of the crossing is the well, and four roads run away from it into the dark.

## What it looks and sounds like
Chapter 2 gave the callbacks (the still wheel, the water in the race, the inn sign, no smoke, no dogs) and Chapter 3 spends them once: the inn sign's 'dry complaint and a pause and the same complaint again', the inn door knocking in the east wind, hooves ringing off the walls, the race 'white even now, the same pitch hour after hour'. Cold chimneys. A horseshoe nailed over the inn lintel with the points down. Smells are ash, wet thatch and sour milk from one kitchen: no rot.

## What the houses say
Everyone got up from whatever they were doing at different hours and went out and shut the door: a drawknife laid down tidy on a half-shaved spoke in the wheelwright's; a laid table (four bowls, a spoon in each, a hard loaf, a sweated cheese); porridge pot and scraped bowl; a sickbed with a basin, a folded rag and a cup of water; a cradle with a tucked blanket and no baby. Coats on the pegs, boots and people gone. No blood, no broken doors, no dogs, no chains. One brindled barn cat, healthy and indifferent.

## What the ground says
The lane behind the east gardens is churned to ridges and holes, a gate lies flat with its post splintered outward and no latch lifted; a man's clog stands upright in the mud with the print after it bare. The oak field gate at the top of the lane is open with a drover's loop of rope holding it, and beyond is the drove way, where sixty or more dead went north and a little east.

## Timing
The party arrives at last light. The wheelwright stopped in the middle of the day; Sylvanas judges 'Five days at the most' from the mould; the girl says the feet went by 'every night'. The old carter claims he was through on a market day and the village was well enough then; nothing reconciles that with 'five days'.
