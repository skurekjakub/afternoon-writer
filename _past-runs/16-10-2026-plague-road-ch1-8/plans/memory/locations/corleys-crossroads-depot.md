# Corley's depot (the crossroads yard)

A small grain depot where the road from the sick village meets another: long shed, weigh-house, beaten yard, green trough, beam scale at the gate swinging in no wind, three carts with their shafts down. The clerk Corley keeps a fire lit in a lean-to office on a warm day ('The ink thickens. Of a morning.'). The one west window lets the afternoon sun straight across the counter; a wasp tests an open pane.

The out-book on the back shelf is the chapter's first hard evidence: lines ruled in red, 'And.' in the margin of nearly every one, 'own cart' for free carters, short-rate loads from midsummer. Cyndia counts them by road. Jaina reads the crossing outside because 'everything in a yard gets walked over.' The party sleeps in the loft of the carters' inn across the road.

Use it for: the ordinary, rule-bound face of the grain trade; Corley's red ears and runny nose against the overseer's white face later (a deliberate contrast: Corley 'had gone red to the ears and run at the nose; this one went white').
