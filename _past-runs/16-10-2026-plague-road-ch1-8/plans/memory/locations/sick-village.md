# The sick village (unnamed)

South of Millhaven, loud and working. The lane runs cobbled between cottages with a hammer on iron somewhere ahead, 'quick and slow and quick again', an egg stall under an awning (a woman calling a price, another calling it lower, laughing), a salt-herring barrel, a pump, geese, two boys under a mare's nose after a hoop, bread smoke lying low and brown over everything. Three cottages have their shutters barred in the middle of the day; one has a horseshoe nailed over the lintel with its points down and a stool on the step with a cup on it. At the top of the lane a squat grey chapel of the Light with a bell-cote and no bell; at the bottom the granary, the bakehouse, the pound and its cage, and the brook. Wells: Cooper's Lane, the top farm's own, and the brook for Ashby's. A tannery upstream.

Under all the noise Jaina hears the drag 'thin and spread and moving'; the village is Millhaven 'a fortnight ago or a week or however long it had been, Millhaven with smoke in its chimneys and a market in its street'. It is saved in a single night: the granary and bakehouse shut, every sack of the new man's flour in every house burned in Joss's yard.

At dawn after a little rain it is 'smaller without the brown haze, and barer'; the mares are saddled under the yew; people stand at their doors; the egg woman stops setting out her trestle, the woman in the blue apron holds up her hand flat. At the bottom of the village, past the brook, the road splits: north to Millhaven, south and west between hedges, rutted by every loaded cart, the grain road.
