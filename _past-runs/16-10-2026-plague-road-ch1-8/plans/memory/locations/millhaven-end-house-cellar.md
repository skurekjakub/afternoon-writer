# Millhaven end house and cellar

The last house on the back lane, its back wall right on the lane. One room, a box bed, a hearth gone grey, a table with two bowls furred soft and grey and a spoon standing upright. The bed has been dragged across the floor to cover a square hatch with an iron ring; the bolt is on the underside. Cyndia called the house empty on the first sweep.

Down the ladder: a clay-walled cellar, cold earth and piss and turnips, a spilled bin, and a barred slot a hand high in the back wall just under the beams, a crust of dirt on its sill. A child on the turnip bin sees the lane at the height of a dog's nose. That is how the girl watched feet go past, toes pointing away from the mill, up toward Pell's, and how she learned which way the dead went.

Neral came through the cracks of the floor with her voice and water; Sylvanas stood above the hatch with an arrow on the string; Jaina went down on her elbows and the heel of her good hand and read it clean of carriers.
