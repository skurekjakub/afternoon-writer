# Jaina Proudmoore and Sylvanas Windrunner

## State at the end of Chapter 3
Still custodian and prisoner on paper; in practice a working pair that neither will call one. Sylvanas never says 'Jaina' even in her head: 'the mage' and, when she is thinking about custody, 'the prisoner'. She says 'Mage' aloud and 'Proudmoore' only when the mage will not come up out of a read. Jaina says 'Ranger-General' every time, whether she is pushing ('Ranger-General, it's right under us') or arguing ('a store is where things end up') or apologising.

## How it moved
Sylvanas first overrides ('Houses first.'), then pays off 'Then I pick the ground' by choosing and covering the well, and hauls the bucket herself. When the mage goes under she shouts her name and tears the hand from the frost. The mage's answer is a straight 'I don't know. I told you on the road I would. I'm sorry.' Sylvanas thinks that is more than she has had from a mage in her life. At the storehouse she expects the mage to grab the loudest answer and sign it, as any Silvermoon magister would; the mage declines, and Sylvanas thinks 'Damn her, then. The hand.' She takes the staff once on the cellar ladder: a cold stick that buzzes in her bones and means nothing to her. She cannot feel any of it, so the mage's words are all she has, and she acts on them (the lane prints, the horses, the heavy-and-light count in the cut).

## What Sylvanas will not do
She will not praise. She will not trust. She keeps the mage because the mage is a prisoner and 'the only one of the four who could say which way to ride'. By the noon water she tells herself she is following the trail, not the mage: 'No. On the trail. The read was the trail.'

## Pressure points for later
- Sylvanas still thinks of the mage as a prisoner when it suits her; do not give her warmth or the name 'Jaina' before the partnership arc earns it.
- The mage's hands are the visible cost: left raw and wrapped, right dead.
- Jaina's unspoken Ch2 question (was anyone alive?) was answered by the cellar; Sylvanas never heard it.

## State at the end of Chapter 4 (their one clean win)
They enter the village as two specialists with different methods. At the spear, Sylvanas gives up her bow, speaks low, concedes, tells the story in objects (the cradle, the spoon, sixty prints) and offers 'let my mage see your sick, and we'll know by dark whether I'm a liar'. Jaina has the Kirin Tor speech ready, shuts her mouth, and thinks she had Windrunner down as maps: 'Could it be learned?' She relapses at the chapel window, then at dusk brings the human problem straight to Sylvanas in the real register ('It starts in the gut... What sorts them is bread') while Sylvanas, from the granary side, has the supplier; Jaina starts a sentence, Sylvanas finishes it: 'On purpose.' Jaina's narration begins calling her Sylvanas; aloud it stays 'Ranger-General'; Sylvanas still says only 'Mage'.

In the granary the two methods are one action: Sylvanas pins the boy with an arrow, Jaina with frost, Sylvanas takes the lamp, Jaina clamps the spill under a shell. At the cage Tam dies, Sylvanas on her knees with her fingers in his teeth, Jaina on hers reading a clean gut. Jaina picks Sylvanas, not Brask, to ask for the night shutdown ('I can ward a room. I can't ward a village.'), and Sylvanas puts it to Brask in the same low voice: 'Then you have until cockcrow.' The village lives. In the morning Jaina doubts the ANDORHAL sacking; Sylvanas folds it away: 'Either way, it's the road he drove.'

## Chapter 5: two maps, one road

Back in Sylvanas's head. She sends Jaina to a crossing to read the roads and herself to the clerk's book. The mage gives one-word reads (north heavy and old, east light and starting) and no lecture; Sylvanas, waiting for it, notices. They draw two pictures on one sheet that do not sit together until Cyndia counts the book's east road (nine short-rate loads in the last month): the mage was right. Jaina moves her own marks (Hollow Green, Ashford) without pride; Sylvanas had expected a magister's vanity ('the wasn't that something'). At the overseer, 'Proudmoore. Read him.' finds nothing on him and Sylvanas lets him go on a polite lie: a day lost. At the stove, 'Proudmoore. Barov.' gets the house and the granaries. At the fork Jaina's staff light leans to the south bend; 'How sure?' 'As sure as Brent's.' And Sylvanas, privately: 'Jaina, then.' She pins Strahnbrad to the bench with her knife, refuses Jaina's offered re-read ('No.'), warns 'Expect them to get in the way', and gets 'Then I'll have to show it to them.' / 'You'll have to. I can't see it either.' It is the first admitted dependency.

## Chapter 6 (Jaina's POV): two women reading the same failure
Sylvanas tests Jaina's plain line ('Somebody.') and tells her to make it short because Arthas has orcs. At Strahnbrad she reads the camp faster than Jaina, hands Jaina the sheet at the stirrup, stays behind her, and calls the prince's decision aloud before Jaina has finished. Jaina had been sure proof would show Sylvanas too hard on a prince she never met; afterwards she says 'You were right. At the fork.' Sylvanas offers the verdict (a good commander with the wrong war for this; 'the other one is ours'), asks nothing about the history, and Jaina offers nothing. No thanks, no warmth, and the old prejudice has less room to hide. On the road Sylvanas asks Jaina to read the tally-man, accepts 'Nothing but a tired clerk', and says only 'Once, it's a farewell. Twice, it's wrong.' At the crest she waits for Jaina to name the destination. Aloud: 'Ranger-General' and 'Mage' (or 'Proudmoore' in company).

### Pressure points for later
- No open trust or warmth shown; Jaina's 'thank you' is formal; Sylvanas never replies in words.
- Sylvanas still does not say 'Jaina' ALOUD (she thinks it from Chapter 5 on; Chapter 4 text said 'no Jaina' and Ch5 changes only the interior).
- She now bets four horses on a read she cannot check; the first outside test is the army on the south road.
- Jaina keeps swinging between 'maps' and 'Sylvanas'; the sentence-finishing ('On purpose.') is a first sign of shared reasoning.


## Chapter 7 (Sylvanas's POV): the split holds, and the halves fit

At the gate Jaina answers the clerk flat and cuts off his question about the Ranger-General ('My escort.'). At the post Sylvanas decides at the bench: Jaina, Corin and Lyriel go to the sample room (a bow in a corridor would only slow it; Jaina can read bags and cannot tell a drilled man's walk from a tired one); Sylvanas takes the district. Jaina says what her half cannot do ('I won't know who put it there'), and Sylvanas answers 'That's mine.' Privately she remembers that it had been easier at Corley's, with never more than a shout between them.

When they meet again the pictures fit at once: Jaina's coat laid on the grain by hand, Sylvanas's masked men and crock; 'I have the hands.' Jaina steps up beside her before being asked and sets the staff down at her shoulder facing Havel; Sylvanas had been about to ask for the jaw. Sylvanas thinks 'Jaina' now and worries she will be white by the bell; aloud she says 'Proudmoore'. Nothing is said about trust. The raid exists only because both halves exist.

Pressure points for later: Jaina has offered to spend her last working hand once tonight; Sylvanas has spread Cyndia and Lyriel thin; the Dalaran fear Jaina carries is not touched by Corin's unanswered letters on the page.
