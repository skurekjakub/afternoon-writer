# Magic

Ward-stones hum low and warm from the inside; Sylvanas reads them with her bare palm, wardens with lenses. Dampers (a slate plate, a gold-runed wedge) suppress a mage's staff. Jaina hears the leylines as sound through the floor and the staff, and reads the contamination as a late drag in each pulse and an old-coin taste. Elves cannot feel it; horses and the mage tell them.

## The contamination (through chapter 3)
In clay, groundwater, roots, crops, living animals; in Millhaven's well water, soil, cellar, storehouse, footprints and the footprints of the dead. Cattle, dogs and hares read light; the dead's marks read heavy. The crossing read gave no source-or-sink verdict; the storehouse is loudest but the mage will not call it the origin. Horses remain clean and nobody knows why. South of Millhaven it reads wider and more spread out. Sweet breath ('pears gone brown') is a symptom reported by the girl; the dead rise after death.

## Costs
Deep reads cost Jaina her hands: frost, torn skin, loss of fine control. By Ch3 the left hand is raw and wrapped, the right dead.

## Chapter 4
Spells shown: the plain light word (diagnostic, pale gold through the body), the holding ward (the first Dalaran ward, for flasks; blue, takes the drag, thins, must be fed in rounds), a frost cast on a person, and a shell ward over a spill. The contamination is swallowed in flour and cumulative; early cases are a fist under the breastbone and a thread along the gut; late cases are everywhere. Elsewhere in the same loft: a clay jar of the built form, a stack of at least three spells, one to keep, one to wait, one unreadable, in the death-currents grammar the Kirin Tor keep behind a grille. Wards hold but do not cure. Every spell now costs Jaina: cold in the chest and teeth, a torn left palm, an arm iced to the shoulder.

## Chapter 5
Jaina reads contamination off a road from a mile-post or crossing and grades it heavy/light and old/starting: north, west and south roads old and heavy, east light and starting, the King's road thin and clean, the south road layered (thin new on old, older toward the bottom). She cannot read paper, books or people, and cannot hear places she has not visited. Reading costs: dimming gold in the crystal, a cold draught in rooms, chattering teeth, frost on bare fingertips; at the fork the crystal's light visibly 'leaned' toward the south bend. Right arm dead, left hand wrapped with two usable fingertips.

**Chapter 6.** The drag in the road has a late hitch behind every pulse and rises at running water, at each joining farm lane, and enormously at Andorhal. Jaina's cost ladder adds ear pressure and a nosebleed, with the crystal sinking to dark. Her model: one distributed system, with a keeping part carried in bread, building meal after meal, killing, then walking, with the last step unexplained. Design signature: Dalaran-style closed seams; the death-currents craft is behind the library grille. She cannot read people.

## Chapter 7
Jaina reads sealed sample bags as heavy or clean; every bag on a copied mark is heavy. The coat is applied by hand after dressing and before sewing, worked through every grain; alone it does little, and what it carries wakes inside whoever eats the bread. Alchemy and necromancy both. The loft residue is wet tonight. She offers a once-tonight jaw freeze by fingertip touch. The day's reads cost her a grey face, a dead staff crystal and frost on her linen.
