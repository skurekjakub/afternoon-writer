# Economy

Millhaven's grain trade centres on its mill and stone storehouse (sacks chalk-tallied, a tally board). The village had an established seller (Mister Hollis) and a newer, cheaper one. The old carter's fortnightly run to the mill: up empty, home full. In the south the market at the ford is busy; barley is up a copper the measure, and people are buying anyway. [Chapter 3 caution, now superseded.]

## Chapter 4
Grain IS the carrier. In the sick village: valley farms -> granary keeper Joss -> bakehouse and houses. Three months ago a new young man with a cart from the west road undercut the farms by two coppers on the measure; four loads, the last a fortnight ago; sacks stencilled with a sheaf and ANDORHAL. Bread is the staple (the cooper's house of five takes two loaves a day; Ashby's buys bread and cheese for the hands). Andorhal's big granaries supply the south; Brask's barley goes through it. The village burns its flour, its granary and its bakehouse in one night.

## Chapter 5
Depots log load, carter, destination, sacks, weight and rate; free 'own cart' carters are booked by load, not man; weights are taken on the carter's word and settled at Andorhal. Short-rate loads (twelve sacks, two coppers under) began at midsummer, Millhaven first; by road before/since last month north 9/3, west 7/4, south 11/6, east 1/9; 31 places in Corley's book, 47 across all books (19 in more than one). The big river depot breaks the Andorhal loads for the whole side of the river. Burned waybills: 53 readable, all Andorhal at one end, Barov on 31, 9 more burned through. A free carter bought back his spoiled flour at a loss.

**Chapter 6.** Andorhal is in full trade: granary hoists, queued wagons, ovens and a mill wheel running. The south road below Strahnbrad carries ordinary farm carts into the city; army wagons run empty down and loaded up. Cheap-flour loads recalled by Jaina as part of one organised system.

## Chapter 7
Bills, seals and yard marks run the trade; the inspection office keeps copies; forty-one marks copied since midsummer. A factor nobody has met supplies Fenner's and the Exchange (same week, high summer), Garside's briefly (Lammas), Kett's and the malt-house. Loaders hired from outside sleep behind Fenner's. The sickness follows the Mill Lane bread.
