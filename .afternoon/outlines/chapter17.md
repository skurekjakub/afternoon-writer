# Chapter 17: The Gate

**POV:** Sylvanas Windrunner (limited third, absolute)
**When:** Directly follows Chapter 16 at Stratholme's gates. Opens the moment after Jaina answers Arthas honestly and closes after the Culling, with Arthas emerging below the wall with clean hands and wrong eyes.
**Where:** Stratholme's main gate, service lane, and inner wall.
**Travel:** On foot at the gates and through the service lane, a short climb to the inner wall, then a static witness position once the Culling begins.
**Characters:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas, Uther, Falric, gate soldiers.
**Goal:** Survive Arthas's answer to too late, get a clear read on the city once the source plan fails, and keep Jaina moving inside a decision neither of them controls.

**Sylvanas's starting position:**
- Jaina's source plan was real, practical, and too late at city scale.
- At least one granary could be broken, which proves the method. The problem now is time and mouths rather than theory.
- The city is both seeded and partly steered: the crude choir can self-muster, but living cult handlers are still directing parts of the dead.
- Arthas came to the gate needing a yes-or-no answer and got no. Sylvanas started to answer first, and Jaina spoke over her.
- In Chapter 11 Sylvanas gave Arthas a war he knows how to want and sent him to Hearthglen.
- At Hearthglen, Uther stopped Arthas from burning the fever tents with the sick inside. Uther heard the same answer at the gate.
- Cyndia now keeps a record of the farewell sightings.

**Still in the dark about:**
- How far Arthas will carry too late.
- Whether Uther can stop him a second time.
- How long Jaina can stay upright once the prince turns the answer into policy.
- What face the farewell will wear next if it comes again.
- Where she and Jaina can stand that keeps them clear enough to witness and alive enough to leave.

**Hold back:**
- No clean emotional reconciliation.
- No explicit Mal'Ganis reveal.
- No interiority for Arthas, Jaina, or Uther.
- No framing that makes the Culling easy just because it is tactically legible.
- No Northrend pivot yet.
- No spoken confession of guilt from either woman. Each knows her own part. Neither says it.

**End of chapter state:**
- Arthas answers Jaina's too late with the Culling.
- Uther breaks with him at the gate and cannot stop him this time.
- Jaina refuses the order and stands outside the command chain with Sylvanas.
- The fifth farewell comes from a fleeing grain carter in the service lane who also gives them the route to the wall. Sylvanas hears it herself and has his face in front of her. Jaina gets nothing off him at all.
- Sylvanas starts down the lane after the carter, stops when the city breaks and she sees Jaina frozen at the foot of the wall stair, and chooses Jaina and the wall over the chase.
- From the wall, Sylvanas reads the Culling as deliberate, methodical, and effective at preventing a full city-wide turn while still remaining an atrocity.
- Each woman knows she had a hand in it: Sylvanas aimed Arthas in Chapter 11, and Jaina answered him alone at the gate. The crack from Scholomance closes in practice through shared witness and shared guilt before either woman says anything.
- Arthas emerges from the gate afterward with clean hands and the look of someone already moving on to the next necessity.

## Character Arcs

### Sylvanas
- She opens still hoping Arthas may stay inside hard containment instead of crossing into something worse.
- The gate proves he will turn necessity into license the moment he thinks the cleaner answer is gone, and that Uther can no longer stop him.
- In the service lane she finally has the farewell face in front of her, and chooses Jaina at the stair foot and the wall above over chasing him.
- She has to watch a methodical atrocity unfold block by block, knowing she pointed the man running it.
- By the end, Arthas no longer looks like a hard ally. He looks like a man who walked past his last check and washed his hands before coming back into view.

### Jaina through Sylvanas's POV
- Jaina is the failed rescuer and the witness whose honest answer, given over Sylvanas, made the next order possible without causing it.
- The source attempt gives Sylvanas the field proof she needed: whatever happened in Kel'Thuzad's room, Jaina spent what she learned trying to save strangers.
- Keep Jaina exterior. Show her through silence, posture, flour-dusted knuckles, the tremor in her hands, and where she fixes her eyes once the gate order lands.

### Partnership
- The pair opens damaged but synchronized.
- Shared witness and shared guilt finish what the source attempt started. Each of them put a hand on this, and each knows it.
- The crack is not talked through. It closes in placement, timing, and Sylvanas turning back from the carter's wagon the instant she sees Jaina stopped dead at the bottom of the wall stair.
- Do not give them spoken absolution.

## Scene 1: The Gate

**The action:** Jaina's honest answer becomes Arthas's order, and the moral split breaks into the open.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas, Uther, Falric, gate soldiers.
**Starting point:** The source plan failed because it was late, not because it was wrong.

### Beat 1
- Arthas does not ask for nuance twice. If the city cannot be saved cleanly, he treats that as the decision already made and orders the city purged before the dead can claim it.
- Arthas turns too late straight into the Culling, moves faster than anyone else at the gate can follow, and Falric and the officers around him start snapping into formation before the last word drops.
- They do not know whether Uther will refuse in words only or with his body, or where Sylvanas and Jaina can stand that keeps them clear enough to witness and alive enough to leave.
- Ground the order: Arthas's voice flattening instead of rising, gate guards stiffening before the last words land, Jaina's flour-gray hands still dirty from the failed stores while the prince gives a clean command.
- Arthas orders the city purged, and his officers start drawing steel before anyone at the gate can answer him.

### Beat 2
- Uther refuses the order and puts himself in the gateway, the way he stood in the tent mouth at Hearthglen. This time Arthas rides around him. Jaina refuses the order too, though she does not pretend the city can still be saved by the plan that already failed. Arthas does not bend.
- Uther's break is immediate and public, and it does not stop anything. Jaina stands outside Arthas's order beside Sylvanas, ending whatever bond held her to him. Sylvanas watches a man step past his last check without slowing his horse.
- They do not know whether the city can still be watched from anywhere outside the purge lines, or what the prince will look like once the city is done.
- Ground the split: Uther's hammer held low in the gateway, the scrape of armored men shifting as loyalties split into two clusters of steel, Arthas's horse passing close enough to brush Uther's shoulder.
- Arthas rides past Uther into the city, and the main gate collapses into steel and panic.

## Scene 2: The Service Lane

**The action:** The three women leave the main gate, the farewell comes again from a face Sylvanas can follow, and she chooses Jaina and the wall over the carter as the city breaks.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, fleeing civilians, grain carter, sick-looking acolyte, scattered soldiers.
**Starting point:** The Culling is underway. Witness now matters as much as survival.

### Beat 3
- The main approach clogs with panicking townsfolk, moving troops, and people who still do not understand what the prince has ordered. Sylvanas pulls Jaina and Cyndia out through the edge of the crush before the gate seals.
- Sylvanas chooses high ground and witness over futile fighting in the street, Jaina keeps moving only because walking is easier than standing at the gate and listening, and the side service lane leads toward the inner wall clear of Arthas's command line.
- They do not know how much of the city they can see from the wall, or whether height will make the purge clearer or harder to bear.
- Ground the escape: a narrow service lane choked with overturned handcarts and split grain sacks, Cyndia angling her horse across the mouth long enough to clear the turn, Jaina stumbling once on cobblestones and catching herself without speaking.
- They clear the gate crowd for the side street, leaving the purge line moving behind them.

### Beat 4
- A grain carter with an empty wagon is working his horses out before the lane seals up. He knows which wall stair is still open, which service yard has already gone sick, and which route puts them above the inner district instead of inside it. He should not be this useful. A sick-looking acolyte waits by a yard gate behind him. The carter nods once, and the acolyte slips away down the lane. The carter speaks the farewell to Sylvanas, and while he says it he looks past her at the wall. Sylvanas glances at Jaina. Jaina shakes her head: there is nothing on him to read.
- The service route makes the wall reachable without reentering the main crush, an ordinary carter holds precise timing and street knowledge, and for the first time Sylvanas has the farewell and the face in front of her together. The nod looks like one frightened local telling another to run. Jaina's read comes back empty, the way it did in Chapter 6.
- They do not know whether the pattern ties to the plague, the route, or only to these women being watched.
- Ground the clue: an empty wagon bed dusted with flour, a man talking too fast as if fear just caught up with him, the farewell spoken like habit with its Common words set in a slightly stiff order.
- Second read (plant only; the prose must not explain it): he is pulling his people out before the Culling, and he steers the trio to the one place where they will witness it. He looks at the wall because the wall is where his work will be watched. Jaina reads nothing because he is showing her nothing.
- The carter slaps the reins and his wagon rattles off down the lane.

### Beat 5
- Sylvanas turns and goes after the wagon. She gets a few strides down the lane—close enough to overtake him before the bend. Behind her the alarm bells start, the first fires go up, and troop lines begin moving in step through the streets. She glances back once: Jaina is standing frozen at the foot of the wall stair, one hand locked white on the stone rail, not moving. Sylvanas stops dead, lets the wagon vanish around the bend, and walks back to Jaina and takes the stair beside her. They climb while the city breaks below them, and from halfway up Sylvanas can tell this is method rather than riot.
- This is the one time Sylvanas chooses not to chase the farewell, and the pull back to Jaina at the stair foot is as immediate and personal as the need to see the city. The carter disappears around the corner before she reaches the bottom step. Arthas's purge is moving district by district, and military order does not make it less monstrous.
- They do not know whether the carter's face will ever come within reach again, or how long Arthas can keep the troops moving in step.
- Ground the choice: iron-rimmed wagon wheels rattling toward the bend, one church bell answered by another across the rooftops, Jaina's flour-gray knuckles white around the stair rail, torch lines cutting across the street grid as they climb.
- They reach the parapet as the first torch lines lock around the inner streets below.
- Inside: the face, at last, and six more strides puts her hand on his collar; Jaina standing frozen at the stair foot with the bells starting; she stops before she finishes weighing the city against the wagon, and resents how fast she turned back; the wagon clears the bend, and she climbs beside Jaina and will not look down the lane again

## Scene 3: The Wall

**The action:** Sylvanas reads the methodical grid of the Culling, faces her own hand in it, and sees the one image below the gate that leaves no room left for argument.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas's forces below.
**Starting point:** The source attempt failed, the Culling is underway, and the city is being cut apart below them.

### Beat 6
- From the wall, Arthas's pattern is horrible and plain. He is cutting the city into manageable blocks, denying the plague one mass activation by getting to the living first. Sylvanas knows whose war he is running. In Arthas's tent she gave him a war he knows how to want and sent him to Hearthglen. Beside her, Jaina is staring down at the gate where she answered him, not at the burning roofs.
- Arthas prevents a full city-wide turn by killing the city in controlled sections, tactical neatness does nothing to lessen the slaughter, and each woman put a hand on this. Jaina stays at the parapet beside Sylvanas instead of pulling away. Neither speaks a word about her own share.
- They do not know what this leaves inside Arthas when it finishes, or whether Sylvanas can ever put what she is watching into report language again.
- Ground the witness: district fires stepping outward in planned sequence, soldiers rotating lines with siege discipline, Jaina's shoulders rigid beside Sylvanas and her eyes fixed on the gate arch below.
- From the parapet Sylvanas sees the entire grid lock into place, and hates how cleanly the soldiers work.
- Inside: gave him this war in a tent and called it strategy; good work down there, and she can see it is good work, and that is the worst of it; would she have done it differently, with his army; Jaina beside her watching the gate instead of the fires; wants to touch her sleeve or say something and has nothing that isn't a report

### Beat 7
- When the screaming stops and the last organized column marches back toward the gate, Arthas emerges below them. He looks up at the wall once. His hands are clean. That is worse than blood would have been.
- Arthas had time, or made time, to wash before stepping back into view, he knows the wall holds witnesses and meets their eyes anyway, and Sylvanas is left with an image cleaner and colder than battlefield gore.
- They do not know what Jaina is making of those washed hands from inside her own history with him, or where the pursuit runs once Stratholme is behind them.
- Ground the image: pale hands catching firelight with no blood left on the skin, Arthas's face emptied out, the city below gone quiet except for crackling roof beams.
- Arthas looks up once with washed hands and an empty face, then turns toward his camp beyond the gate.
