# Chapter 17: The Gate

**POV:** Sylvanas Windrunner (limited third, absolute)
**When:** Directly follows Chapter 16 at Stratholme's gates. Opens the moment after Jaina answers Arthas honestly and closes after the Culling, with Arthas emerging below the wall with clean hands and wrong eyes.
**Where:** Stratholme's main gate, service lane, and inner wall.
**Travel:** On foot at the gates and through the service lane, a short climb to the inner wall, then a static witness position once the Culling begins.
**Characters:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas, Uther, Falric, gate soldiers.
**Goal:** Survive Arthas's answer to too late, get a clear read on the city once the source plan fails, and keep Jaina moving inside a decision neither of them controls.

- Jaina's source plan was real, practical, and too late at city scale.
- At least one granary could be broken, which proves the method. The problem now is time and mouths rather than theory.
- The city is both seeded and partly steered: the crude choir can self-muster, but living cult handlers are still directing parts of the dead.
- Arthas came to the gate needing a yes-or-no answer and got no. Sylvanas started to answer first, and Jaina spoke over her.
- In Chapter 11 Sylvanas gave Arthas a war he knows how to want and sent him to Hearthglen.
- At Hearthglen, Uther stopped Arthas from burning the fever tents with the sick inside. Uther heard the same answer at the gate.

**Still in the dark about:**

- Whether Uther can stop him a second time.
- How long Jaina can stay upright once the prince turns the answer into policy.
- What face the farewell will wear next if it comes again.

**Hold back:**
- No clean emotional reconciliation.
- No explicit Mal'Ganis reveal.
- No interiority for Arthas, Jaina, or Uther.
- No framing that makes the Culling easy just because it is tactically legible.
- No Northrend pivot yet.

**End of chapter state:**
- Arthas answers Jaina's too late with the Culling.
- Uther breaks with him at the gate and cannot stop him this time.
- Jaina refuses the order and stands outside the command chain with Sylvanas.

## Character Arcs

### Sylvanas
- She opens still hoping Arthas may stay inside hard containment instead of crossing into something worse.
- The gate proves he will turn necessity into license the moment he thinks the cleaner answer is gone, and that Uther can no longer stop him.
- In the service lane she has the farewell face in front of her for once, and the city outranks it.
- She has to watch a tactically coherent atrocity, knowing she pointed the man running it.
- By the end, Arthas no longer reads as a hard ally. He reads as a man who crossed a line so fast only the aftermath is left to track.

### Jaina through Sylvanas's POV
- Jaina is the failed rescuer and the witness whose honest answer, given over Sylvanas, made the next order possible without causing it.
- The source attempt gives Sylvanas the field proof she needed: whatever happened in Kel'Thuzad's room, Jaina spent the knowledge here trying to save strangers.
- Keep Jaina exterior. Show her through silence, posture, spell residue, and where she fixes her eyes once the gate order lands.

### Partnership
- The pair opens damaged but synchronized.
- Shared witness and shared guilt finish what the source attempt started. Each of them put a hand on this, and each knows it.
- The crack is not talked through. It closes in placement, timing, and who stays beside whom when leaving would be easier.
- Do not give them spoken absolution.

## Scene 1: The Gate

**The action:** Jaina's honest answer becomes Arthas's order, and the moral split breaks into the open.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas, Uther, Falric, gate soldiers.
**Starting point:** The source plan failed because it was late, not because it was wrong.

### Beat 1
- Arthas does not ask for nuance twice. If the city cannot be saved cleanly, he treats that as the decision already made and orders the city purged before the dead can claim it.
- Arthas converts too late straight into the Culling, he is now moving faster than anyone else at the gate can morally follow, and Falric and the command edge are already orienting around execution rather than debate.
- They do not know whether Uther will refuse in words only or in action, or where Sylvanas and Jaina can stand that keeps them clear enough to witness and alive enough to leave.
- Ground the order: Arthas's voice flattening instead of rising, gate guards stiffening before the last words land, Jaina's flour-gray hands still dirty from the failed stores while the prince gives a clean command.
- The uncertainty ends in the worst clean answer the gate could have produced.

### Beat 2
- Uther refuses the order and puts himself in the gateway, the way he stood in the tent mouth at Hearthglen. This time Arthas rides around him. Jaina refuses the order too, though she does not pretend the city can still be saved by the plan that already failed. Arthas does not bend.
- Uther's break is immediate and public, and it does not stop anything. Jaina stands outside Arthas's order and ends that bond in the only way that matters now. Sylvanas is watching a man step past his last check.
- They do not know whether the city can still be read from anywhere but inside the purge line, or what the prince will look like once the city is done.
- Ground the split: Uther's hammer held low in the gateway, the scrape of armored men shifting when loyalties become physical, Arthas's horse passing close enough to brush Uther's shoulder.
- The main gate stops being a place to argue and becomes a place to survive.

## Scene 2: The Service Lane

**The action:** The three women leave the main gate, the farewell comes again from a face Sylvanas can follow, and she chooses the wall over the face as the city starts breaking.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, fleeing civilians, grain carter, sick-looking acolyte, scattered soldiers.
**Starting point:** The Culling is underway. Witness now matters as much as survival.

### Beat 3
- The main approach is collapsing into panic, soldiers, and people who still do not understand what the prince has ordered. Sylvanas gets Jaina and Cyndia out through the edge of that crush before it seals.
- Sylvanas chooses witness over futile participation, Jaina keeps moving only because motion is easier than standing at the gate and listening, and the service route offers access to the wall without putting them under Arthas's immediate command.
- They do not know how much of the city they can still see from the wall, or whether the wall will make the Culling feel cleaner or worse.
- Ground the escape: a side lane clogged with handcarts and dropped sacks, Cyndia using her horse as moving cover long enough to clear the turn, Jaina stumbling once and correcting without speaking.
- They leave the gate line knowing they are not stopping what is coming.

### Beat 4
- A grain carter with an empty wagon is trying to get out before the lane seals up. He knows which wall stair is still open, which service yard has already gone sick, and which route will put them above the inner district instead of inside it. He should not be this useful. A sick-looking acolyte waits by a yard gate behind him. The carter nods once, and the acolyte moves off down the lane. The carter speaks the farewell to Sylvanas, and while he says it he looks past her at the wall. Sylvanas glances at Jaina. Jaina shakes her head: there is nothing on him to read.
- The service routes make the wall reachable without reentering the main crush, an ordinary grain carter has precise and timely knowledge, and for once Sylvanas has the farewell and the face in front of her together. The nod reads as one frightened local telling another to run. Jaina's read comes back empty, the way it did in Chapter 6.
- They do not know whether the pattern ties to the plague, the route, or only to these women being watched.
- Ground the clue: an empty wagon bed dusted with flour, a man talking too fast because fear is finally catching him, the farewell spoken like habit with the words in a slightly stiff order for Common.
- Second read (plant only; the prose must not explain it): he is pulling his people out before the Culling, and he steers the trio to the one place where they will witness it. He looks at the wall because the wall is where his work will be watched. Jaina reads nothing because he is showing her nothing.
- The carter slaps the reins and his wagon starts down the lane.

### Beat 5
- Sylvanas turns and goes after the wagon. She gets a few strides down the lane. Behind her the bells start, the first fires go up, and troop lines begin moving in step through the streets. Jaina is at the foot of the wall stair, not moving. Sylvanas stops, turns back, and takes the stair. They climb while the city breaks below them, and from halfway up Sylvanas can tell this is method rather than riot.
- This is the one time she chooses not to chase the farewell, and it is the right choice. The carter is gone around the bend before she reaches the stair. Arthas's purge is being run district by district, and tactical coherence does not make it less monstrous.
- They do not know whether the carter's face will ever be in front of her again, or how long Arthas can keep the pattern controlled.
- Ground the choice: wagon wheels rattling away down the lane, one bell answered by another from across the city, Jaina's hand on the stair rail, torch lines cutting across the street grid as they climb.
- They reach the wall in time to watch the machine start working exactly as intended.
- Inside: the face, at last, and the wheels already going; a few more strides and she has him; Jaina at the foot of the stair, not moving; a phrase or a city, and she knows the answer and resents being asked; the bend in the lane, and she will not think about it now

## Scene 3: The Wall

**The action:** Sylvanas gets the operational read on the Culling, knows her own hand in it, and sees the one image that turns the read into something personal and irreversible.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Jaina, Cyndia, Arthas's forces below.
**Starting point:** The source attempt failed, the Culling is underway, and the city is now a process they cannot stop.

### Beat 6
- From the wall, Arthas's pattern is horrible and plain. He is cutting the city into manageable pieces, denying the plague one mass activation by getting to the living first. Sylvanas knows whose war he is running. In Arthas's tent she gave him a war he knows how to want and sent him to Hearthglen. Beside her, Jaina is looking at the gate where she answered him.
- Arthas is preventing a full city-wide turn by killing the city in controlled sections, efficiency does not reduce the atrocity, and each woman put a hand on this. Jaina stays on the wall beside Sylvanas instead of breaking away. Neither says anything about her part.
- They do not know what this will leave in Arthas when it is over, or whether Sylvanas can ever reduce what she is seeing back into report language.
- Ground the witness: district fires stepping outward in sequence, soldiers rotating lines with the discipline of a siege, Jaina's eyes fixed on the gate below and not on the fires.
- Sylvanas gets the full military read and hates what the clarity proves.
- Inside: gave him this war in a tent and called it strategy; good work down there, and she can see it is good work, and that is the worst of it; would she have done it differently, with his army; Jaina beside her watching the gate instead of the fires; wants to say something to her and has nothing that isn't a report

### Beat 7
- When the screaming stops and the last organized movement comes back toward the gate, Arthas emerges below them. He looks up once. His hands are clean. That is worse than blood would have been.
- Arthas had time, or made time, to wash before stepping back into view, he knows the wall holds witnesses and prepares for that fact, and Sylvanas now has an image she can carry south that is cleaner and more damning than battlefield gore.
- They do not know what Jaina is making of the same image from inside her own history with him, or what direction the pursuit takes once the city is behind them.
- Ground the image: pale hands catching firelight with no blood on them, Arthas's face emptied out, the city below gone quiet except for burning timber.
- Arthas stops reading as prince, ally, or necessary force and becomes something worse.
