# Chapter 11: The Fuse

**POV:** Sylvanas Windrunner (limited third, absolute)
**When:** Runs parallel to Chapter 12. While Jaina is already inside Scholomance after Chapter 10's descent, Sylvanas and Cyndia ride south from the Chapter 9 split to brief Arthas, then turn north again when Jaina's trail goes dark.
**Where:** Eastweald road south toward Arthas's field camp. Hearthglen, his next target, lies northwest of the Eastweald.
**Travel:** Mounted on Thalassian horses south through the Eastweald to Arthas's camp, then hard north again toward Caer Darrow.
**Characters:** Sylvanas, Cyndia.
**Goal:** Force proof of the plague through human military command fast enough to move an army, then reckon with whether Arthas is rescue or a loaded weapon.

## Character Arcs

### Sylvanas
- She opens hoping one hard handoff to a prince and an army can put Lordaeron's war back into Lordaeron's hands.
- Instead she gets ground through everything she despises: checkpoints, salutes, defensive quartermasters, and commanders who only hear danger once it sounds like a siege.
- Without Jaina beside her, she fights the tent with routes, ledgers, and one sentence stolen from Jaina's mouth.
- She wins in the ugliest way possible: by handing Arthas a war he knows how to want.
- Twin-sin wiring (structural note; the prose never names it): this is Sylvanas's second act of being right alone, after Chapter 9 B3. She is right about the grain and right about Arthas, and she hands him the target without the half of the case only Jaina could make. Jaina's matching act is Chapter 16 B8.
- When the lake watch rides in, all calculation vanishes in one heartbeat: she drops a kingdom's war and rides north for Jaina.

### Jaina through Sylvanas's POV
- Jaina is missing as magical proof, as the person who could have made the war tent see the real horror, and as the quiet heat at Sylvanas's shoulder whose knot still holds Sylvanas's bandage tight.
- Without her, Sylvanas can sell supply lines, granaries, and dead villages, not the curse inside them.
- Jaina stays off-page and simultaneous—a risk Sylvanas cannot put down even while briefing a prince.

### Partnership
- Splitting the pack traps each woman inside the arena she hates most: Jaina alone inside dark scholarship, Sylvanas trapped inside human command tents.
- Absence shows up in borrowed phrasing, Lyriel's empty flank, and the speed with which Sylvanas abandons the entire campaign the moment Jaina goes missing.

## Scene 1: The Road Alone

**The action:** The two surviving Farstriders ride south through dying country toward the human army Sylvanas hates needing.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Cyndia.
**Starting point:** Jaina is alone on the Barov line, and the southbound ride has to put steel in motion fast enough to matter.

### Beat 1
- Missing Jaina and Lyriel bites first as simple danger: Sylvanas must choose between crawling forward to check every treeline or riding hard blind without Jaina's ward-read. She spurs for speed, Lyriel's knot pulling tight across her healing shoulder under her coat.
- The formation is halved, Sylvanas has no magical sweep ahead of the horses, and Lor'themar's earlier warnings ride heavy in her saddlebag.
- They do not know how much of the southern road is already dead or whether Arthas's camp still sits where the torn dispatch placed it.
- Ground the ride: bow leather creaking against Sylvanas's bandaged shoulder, hoofbeats too thin without a four-rider cadence, cold wind cutting into raw skin, folded dispatch parchment warming inside her coat.
- The countryside soon shows them what kind of war has already started.

### Beat 2
- They push hard through country already rotting under quiet cult control. Farmhouses stand shuttered at midday, wheat blackens on the stalk, and distant cult watchers on the ridges do not even bother to loose an arrow because the poisoned grain is already doing their work.
- Ordinary cavalry patrols would ride right past this and see bad weather; the cult does not need pitched battles when tainted wagons already rolled through every crossroads.
- They do not know how far south the sickness has already crept or whether Arthas's supply train is already eating out of poisoned sacks.
- Ground the country: black-spotted grain drooping in unharvested fields, a gaunt farm dog too sick to lift its head or bark, the sweet chemical stench of spoiled storage drifting off a closed barn.
- Sylvanas faces the blunt math: two scouts cannot hold a dying kingdom.

### Beat 3
- Sylvanas turns grief into cold aim. If this must become a human war, she will decide where the human sword falls. Lyriel's death strips away the last patience for border containment; the torn Hearthglen dispatch is the lever to move a prince.
- The dispatch is the excuse, not the goal—command authority is. Arthas may be a sharp blade or a ruinous one, and he is the only blade within reach.
- They do not know which version of Arthas waits in the tents ahead or whether his column can pivot fast enough to reach Hearthglen in time.
- Ground the decision: dark mud flying under hard Thalassian hooves, Cyndia riding tighter at Sylvanas's elbow than a full squad would allow, the flat quiet of a commander choosing a dangerous weapon.
- The road drops them onto the picket line of the prince's camp.
- Inside: if it has to be a human war, then she chooses where the human sword lands; picking a prince because he is the nearest blade, and knowing what that costs; strategy, or only wanting something big enough to hit back; Lyriel would have had a dry line for this road, and nobody does

## Scene 2: The Prince's Camp

**The action:** Sylvanas breaks past Falric's checkpoint and wins Arthas's war tent using supply-line facts and a sentence borrowed from Jaina.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Cyndia, Arthas, Uther, Captain Falric, Lordaeron troops, Silver Hand healers, quartermasters.
**Starting point:** The plague trail is clear enough to lay before the prince, but the camp is braced for raiders and orc remnants, not poisoned grain.

### Beat 4
- Riding into camp, Sylvanas sees the mismatch in ten seconds: strong steel pointed at the wrong war. Earthworks, cavalry pickets, and banner tents face outward while the enemy is arriving inside flour sacks and quartermaster ledgers.
- Silver Hand healers are already fighting fevers the Light refuses to clear cleanly, supply officers bristle at scrutiny, and Jaina's absence grates at every turn because Sylvanas has to translate dark magic into wagon weight and rations.
- They do not know whether the camp guards will let an elf ranger past rank and ceremony in time or how many tents are already hiding sick men.
- Ground the camp: smiths hammering fresh horse-shoes, healers' bitter incense layered over horse sweat and mud, tall blue-and-gold banners snapping above supply wagons nobody has inspected.
- Before she can reach Arthas, she has to crack the checkpoint guarding his tent.

### Beat 5
- Captain Falric blocks the inner line with steel, rank, and cold patience, backed by defensive supply officers and suspicious priests. Sylvanas cuts straight through ceremony by refusing to talk magic at all—she hits them with granary tallies, wagon schedules, dead villages along the Eastweald road, and the blood-stained Hearthglen dispatch.
- Naming poisoned grain is the blow that freezes the tent mouth. Falric drops his stiff posture when a quartermaster goes pale at the route numbers; nobody yields until the threat sounds like sabotage of the army's belly.
- They do not know what Arthas has already heard from the northern shires or whether he will hear an elf out.
- Ground the blockade: Falric's gauntleted hand resting on his pommel at the rope line, crowded healer tents steaming behind him, a quartermaster going rigid when Hearthglen's wagon seal hits the table, Sylvanas clamping her jaw on the urge to end the delay with an arrow.
- Falric steps aside, and the real fight opens inside the command tent.

### Beat 6
- Inside, Sylvanas strips the plague down to maps, roads, poisoned stores, and dead towns—everything she can prove without Jaina beside her. When a Silver Hand healer asks how a fever turns into a walking corpse, Sylvanas hears herself answer with Jaina's line from the road to Strahnbrad: `"It comes in on the wagons and walks out on its own feet."` One quiet callback; she knows whose voice she just borrowed.
- Arthas hears a target and a march. Uther hears danger and missing pieces. The staff officers hear ruined harvests and panicked nobles. Sylvanas holds back Kel'Thuzad's name and Jaina's trail to Darrowmere—she cannot prove one in this tent and will not put a human prince anywhere near the other.
- They do not know whether Arthas will strike with discipline or whether Uther can check him if he does not.
- Ground the briefing: charcoal plague marks slashing across battle lines on the map table, Arthas leaning hard over the table at Jaina's sentence, Uther going quiet and watchful at his shoulder.
- Sylvanas wins the tent by giving Arthas a war he knows how to want.
- Inside: Jaina's line, borrowed without leave, and it cut deeper than anything of her own; keeping the mage's name and the lake out of his mouth, prudence or pure refusal to hand her over to this prince; he leaned in at the wrong part; won the room, and hates the taste of it

## Scene 3: The Conversion

**The action:** Arthas turns certainty into an immediate march, and Uther names the flaw Sylvanas just armed.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Arthas, Uther, Cyndia, Lordaeron command staff.
**Starting point:** Arthas now has Hearthglen on his map, with none of the patience or magical read behind the warning.

### Beat 7
- Once the pattern clicks, Arthas needs no prodding—he snaps orders before the map pins stop trembling, turning suspicion straight into a forced march on Hearthglen. Outside the tent flap, Uther catches Sylvanas's arm for one plain warning: Arthas cannot stomach a fight he believes he must win, and he will smash straight through any caution that tries to slow him down.
- Arthas is already moving faster than judgment, Uther sees the cliff ahead clearly, and Sylvanas realizes she just lit a fuse she cannot hold.
- They do not know whether the march will save Hearthglen or turn it into a slaughterhouse, or whether Arthas will hear another word once his boots are in the stirrups.
- Ground the shift: Arthas's gauntlet sweeping the map off the pegs, Uther's tired, scarred face in the cold air outside the canvas, warhorses churning camp mud as horns call the column to form.
- The march rolls out—and Silvermoon's knife arrives before Arthas's dust even settles.

## Scene 4: The Call North

**The action:** Silvermoon's recall slams down from behind, then the lake watch rides in and erases every order except one.
**Cast:** Sylvanas, Cyndia, Farstrider messengers, the lake watch.
**Starting point:** Arthas's column is moving north toward Hearthglen, and the wider plague fight has been handed off to human steel.

### Beat 8
- A Farstrider courier delivers Lor'themar's latest dispatch like a slap in the face. Cult sites back east have been scrubbed clean, proof is evaporating behind them, and Silvermoon's irritation is hardening into formal recall.
- Political backing is collapsing under Sylvanas's feet, and the erased sites are no accident—someone is wiping the trail behind them. Staying with Arthas keeps a hand on the prince, yet loses her backing at home.
- They do not know how many days remain before Silvermoon sends a direct writ of recall or whether staying near Arthas would check him at all.
- Ground the pressure: Lor'themar's tight, angry hand across heavy parchment, wax broken in mud, the cold fury of being bled by silver seals and recall letters instead of blades.
- Then a lathered Thalassian horse bursts past the camp pickets—the lake watch from Darrowmere—and politics dies on the spot.

### Beat 9
- The watch slides off his blown mount with one sentence: Jaina left her horse on the shore, took a boat to Caer Darrow, and never came back. The prince's march is live war, Silvermoon's recall is in Sylvanas's coat, and none of it weighs a feather against that empty shore.
- Sylvanas drops a kingdom's war without a second look. Her fingers whiten on the leather rein as she swings her horse's head north; Cyndia reads the snap in Sylvanas's shoulders and wheels beside her before the order leaves her mouth. Chapter 13 opens where this ride ends.
- They do not know whether Jaina is chained in a cell, bleeding on a floor, or locked inside a room with something older than a noble house.
- Ground the turn: knuckles gone bloodless on the rein, the familiar heft of the bow settling across Sylvanas's back as she turns north, Thalassian hooves tearing away while Arthas's banners still hang in dust to the south, Lyriel's knot pulling tight across her shoulder as she spurs into a gallop.
- Chapter 13 opens at the shore of Caer Darrow.
- Inside: drops a kingdom's war for one mage and doesn't even bother calling it tactics; Silvermoon's recall can wait, or rot; the runner did exactly what she ordered, and she wants to hit him for the news; is she dead; no, not that, ride
