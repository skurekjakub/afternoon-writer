# Chapter 6: The Pattern

**POV:** Jaina Proudmoore (limited third, absolute)
**When:** Directly after Chapter 5's route choice, with the party already on the southern feeder road near Strahnbrad before the last approach to Andorhal.
**Where:** Southern feeder road into Andorhal's network, with the burning outer farms and military camp of Strahnbrad ahead.
**Travel:** Mounted on Thalassian horses.
**Characters:** Jaina, Sylvanas, Cyndia, Lyriel.
**Goal:** Confirm how large the grain-plague plot really is and force someone in Lordaeron's command to take the warning seriously.

**Jaina's starting position:**
- Grain is carrying the poison.
- The cult is organized, watched, and disciplined enough to lay decoy tracks and ambush pursuers.
- Barov seals and paperwork keep turning up in the trail of rot.
- Their chosen road runs through the military zone near Strahnbrad before Andorhal.
- Her sensitivity has put them ahead of most of Lordaeron, yet they are still chasing poison that has already spread.
- The old carter from the Millhaven road is still sitting in the back of her mind as an unresolved road clue.
- They lost a day to a depot overseer's decoy and fought his cleanup crew in the yard at dusk.
- She can see necromancy in the design, yet she still cannot explain how sickness becomes undeath.

**Still in the dark about:**
- Whether Arthas will listen.
- Whether the Barovs are fully complicit.
- Whether the old carter matters.
- What Andorhal looks like from inside the city.

**Hold back:**
- No Mal'Ganis naming.
- No certainty that the Barovs designed the plague.

**End of chapter state:**
- Arthas heard the warning in the smoking aftermath of a live Blackrock raid and still chose the visible war first.
- Sylvanas has heard the farewell twice. She calls it wrong, not yet a pattern.
- Andorhal sits ahead on the horizon—alive, busy, and rotten underneath.

## Character Arcs

### Jaina
- She opens convinced that clear, terrifying proof will make Arthas listen before Andorhal locks down.
- Watching a live Blackrock raid hit Strahnbrad's pickets forces her to see why his visible war feels urgent—which makes his polite, rigid refusal cut ten times harder.
- Standing in the ash holding notes he never opened, she feels the gulf between Arthas's unbending certainty and the sharp, immediate way Sylvanas steps up beside her.
- By the end, Jaina stops waiting on Lordaeron's command and sets her face squarely toward Andorhal.

### Sylvanas through Jaina's POV

### Partnership
- The live flank fight outside Strahnbrad confirms what the depot yard started: they cover each other on instinct now.
- Arthas's dismissal kills the last argument between them over whether Lordaeron will rescue them.
- They are still taut and unsentimental, yet when Jaina turns away from Arthas, Sylvanas is the person standing squarely at her shoulder.

## Scene 1: The Pattern on the Road

**The action:** Jaina finally sees the plague as deliberate high craft instead of a string of unlucky villages.
**Cast:** Jaina, Sylvanas, Cyndia, Lyriel.
**Starting point:** They are on the southern feeder road with the Barov seals, the depot ambush behind them, and Strahnbrad ahead.

### Beat 1
- Before Strahnbrad's smoke rises over the trees, the scattered pieces lock into one deliberate design. Jaina stops seeing unlucky villages and sees a built weapon: trade routes, dark alchemy, necromancy, and the living body all stitched into one sequence.
- The plague is far too tuned to be an accident or some hedge-mage's stunt. Whoever built it had serious formal training, and the human body finishes the work instead of merely dying from it. When Jaina puts it into words for the others, she says it plainly: "It comes in on the wagons and walks out on its own feet."
- They do not know the architect's name or how high the cult ladder goes.
- Ground the recognition: Jaina tracing route lines in the saddle, dull pain climbing behind her eyes, horses cutting through damp morning mist.
- Whoever built this knows grain ledgers and high necromancy equally well, and Jaina rides toward Strahnbrad knowing she is hunting a peer.

### Beat 2
- Jaina has to face how good the spellwork is. The recognition makes her stomach turn, yet she cannot unsee it: someone engineered this with the kind of mastery she would admire in any lecture hall in Dalaran.
- The plague brushes against forbidden Kirin Tor theory about keeping bodies usable after death. Half of Jaina's revulsion comes from recognizing real brilliance inside a slaughter.
- They do not know whether the work belongs to someone she once studied beside.
- Ground the reaction: arcane pressure buzzing harder behind her temples, knuckles white on the reins, nausea rising from understanding too much.
- Ahead of them, dark woodsmoke climbs above the ridge—pulling them straight from hidden proof into a live war.
- Inside: the elegance of it, and wanting to take it apart to see how; that wanting, while villages die of it; the forbidden shelves in Dalaran, and who else read them; not admiration, assessment, and she knows that is a lie

## Scene 2: Strahnbrad

**The action:** They ride straight into a live Blackrock raid, fight a sharp flank clash beside Arthas's perimeter, and watch him dismiss the plague in the smoking aftermath.
**Cast:** Jaina, Sylvanas, Cyndia, Lyriel, Arthas, Lordaeron troops, Silver Hand presence, Blackrock raiders.
**Starting point:** The party understands the plague's design. Strahnbrad's outer farms are burning right now.

### Beat 3
- There is no quiet entry into camp. Black smoke pours off burned outer farmhouses, brass alarm horns blare across the ridge, and bleeding scouts stagger past the palisade as a Blackrock raiding wedge slams into the east perimeter right as the party clears the road.
- Arthas charges straight into the center of the raid in blinding golden Light—warhammer swinging with absolute, radiant certainty. A second orc knot tries to turn the camp's baggage flank right across their road. Sylvanas does not ask leave or wait on human banners; she spurs level with Jaina, dropping the lead raiders with rapid shots while Jaina freezes the churned mud under orc boots and Cyndia and Lyriel seal the ditch. They break the flank cleanly in half a minute without losing a horse.
- They do not know whether fighting beside his pickets will buy Jaina a real hearing once the horn stops.
- Ground the clash: woodsmoke stinging eyes, alarm horns over shouting footmen, Arthas's golden Light flashing off bloody plate, Sylvanas's bowstring singing two feet from Jaina's ear as frost cracks hard across trampled mud.
- The raiders pull back into the burning brush, leaving corpses at the fence and Arthas striding across the mud toward them.

### Beat 4
- In the smoking yard—medics tying off gashes, horses coughing ash, Arthas wiping orc blood from his gauntlets—Jaina presses her folded plague notes into his view and lays out the truth cleanly.
- Arthas hears every word with old affection and unshakable courtesy, then gestures at the burning barns and bleeding footmen behind him: Blackrock steel is killing his people right now, and he will not divide his command over rumors of spoiled flour. The refusal hits ten times harder because the blood on the mud proves his war is real—and because Sylvanas stands half a step off Jaina's left shoulder, hand quiet on her bow, reading the prince's refusal before he finishes the sentence.
- They do not know how many towns will die while his army chases raiders across the hills.
- Ground the refusal: ash drifting onto Jaina's folded pages, Arthas's gauntleted hand still warm with Light and soot, good manners laid down like an iron wall, Sylvanas's lethal stillness at Jaina's shoulder.
- Arthas turns back to his shouting officers, leaving Jaina's folded pages untouched in her hand.

### Beat 5
- Standing in the trampled mud while officers close around him, Jaina stares down at the folded notes he never took.
- She remembers a night years ago on the road to Dalaran, when Arthas talked her into slipping past the watchtowers of an orc internment camp to look. He was sure the camps were necessary before they reached the fence, and he was still sure after she had seen the females and the infants. Today he was bloody, polite, and just as sure.
- They do not know whether anyone in Lordaeron will listen before whole cities begin to empty.
- Ground the aftermath: sour metal at the back of Jaina's throat, ash smeared across ink on the folded notes, Arthas's broad golden back disappearing into a knot of captains.
- Jaina stops waiting on permission from anyone wearing a crown.
- Inside: sure before the fence, sure after the infants, and today bloody, courteous, and still sure; was she unfair then, or is she now; angry at him, and sharper-angry at herself for thinking old history would buy one second look; Sylvanas called it before she finished, and she will have to say so out loud

### Beat 6
- Sylvanas does not rub salt in the cut or mock Jaina's history with the prince. She steps right up to Jaina's shoulder, names the military blind spot in two flat sentences, and asks for Jaina's map—fierce, immediate alignment with zero pity and zero distance.
- Jaina feels the contrast like cold air after woodsmoke: Arthas stood three feet away and looked through her; Sylvanas meets her eyes dead-on, trusts her read without blinking, and asks where they ride next. Their last quarrel over Lordaeron's command dies on the spot.
- They do not know whether Andorhal will hand them proof loud enough to break through anyone's wall.
- Ground the shift: Sylvanas stepping close enough that Jaina catches bow-wax and cold wind off her cloak, eyes steady on Jaina's face, horses wheeling together back onto the Andorhal road.
- Andorhal becomes their only remaining target.

## Scene 3: The Road to Andorhal

**The action:** The old carter clue resurfaces on the road, and Andorhal finally rises on the horizon.
**Cast:** Jaina, Sylvanas, Cyndia, Lyriel, granary tally-man, Andorhal approach traffic.
**Starting point:** Arthas has chosen the orc war, and Andorhal is their only remaining lead.

### Beat 7
- A granary tally-man is walking out of Andorhal with a ledger satchel slung on his shoulder, and they stop him for the last directions into the market gate. While he answers, his eyes flick back up the road toward Andorhal's granary roofs. He tells them to ask at the militia post by the market gate for Captain Havel, who knows the granary district better than anyone. He reads as helpful, tired, and utterly forgettable until he sends them off with the farewell from Millhaven: "May the stars keep your road."
- Sylvanas hears it a second time and twists hard in her saddle. She asks Jaina sharply whether she read anything off him. Jaina felt nothing off him but a worn-out clerk. When Sylvanas looks back up the road, he has already melted into the wagon traffic. Twice is wrong, not yet a pattern—Sylvanas says so in a few clipped words and urges her horse forward. His directions still sound like plain civic courtesy.
- They do not know why the phrase keeps returning or that it is the same being wearing another face.
- Ground the encounter: practiced clerk's courtesy, the sudden chill of hearing those exact words again, Sylvanas twisted back in the saddle staring into a crowded road, grain carts closing around them as they ride on.
- Second read (plant only; the prose must not explain it): he has just come from checking the granaries where his work is spreading, and he routes the party to his own man. Havel is cult-paid (Ch7–8). While he answers, he looks the four riders over again: who rides where, and how far apart.
- The farewell stays lodged under Sylvanas's ribs as a wrong note she cannot act on yet.
- Inside: nothing off him but a tired clerk, and she looked properly, didn't she; Sylvanas twisted in the saddle over six words; a farewell is a farewell; but Sylvanas asked her straight, and she had nothing, and hates having nothing

### Beat 8
- The closer they come to Andorhal, the less Jaina can wall off the city's magic. The rot stops feeling like a map line and starts pounding behind her eyes.
- The taint presses thick enough to make the horses balk and toss their heads before the walls even show. Sylvanas notices Jaina's knuckles go white on the reins and drops the pace half a stride beside her without making a speech of it; Jaina keeps riding anyway.
- They do not know whether Andorhal is already hollow behind its gates.
- Ground the approach: dull throbbing behind Jaina's eyes, heavy sluggish air, Sylvanas matching her horse's stride shoulder-to-shoulder beside Jaina's without looking away from the road.
- They press over the last rise until the city breaks the skyline.

### Beat 9
- Andorhal appears at last, and the sight is worse than Millhaven's silence. It is working. Granary hoists turn, chimneys smoke, wagons crowd the road, and the whole city hums with trade while every poisoned route converges on its gates.
- Because the city is still alive and shipping, the plague is moving through a living mill hub rather than a dead ruin. Whoever runs the shipments is still inside those walls.
- They do not know who inside Andorhal is steering the work.
- Ground the reveal: broad timber roofs and active granary towers against the pale sky, market roar carrying across the fields, rot pulsing dull and steady beneath the noise.
- Chapter 7 opens at Andorhal's gate as they slip inside on foot.
