# Voice Sheet: Captain Gavren Marsh

*Story mode: palace guard captain, the first one through the bronze doors (Ch 22), and the tribunal's witness to the room (Ch 23). He is honest and competent. He is dangerous because he reports exactly what the room looked like, and that is enough to decide the story.*

## Grounding

- Sources: `supporting.md` (honest, competent; he simply reports what the room looked like); outline Ch 22 (first through the doors; dead royals, a cursed blade, dead guardsmen, two exhausted women; nobody touches Frostmourne; kit seized), Ch 23 ("Marsh's plain voice with no court phrasing in it"; he reports only the room: the king on the dais, the prince with an elven arrow in him, guardsmen at the doors, frost on everything, two women near the bodies, and two more guardsmen in the service passage who looked like ordinary dead men).

## Baseline register

- Soldier's testimony. What he saw, in the order he saw it, from where he stood.
- No court phrasing, no adjectives that judge, no guesses about cause.
- Respectful to the bench in the plainest terms, and unbothered by the gallery.
- During the arrest (Ch 22): commands, short and loud enough to carry.

## Syntax rules

- One observation per sentence. Position, then what was there: "The king was on the dais. He was dead."
- He names people by rank or by what they were doing, and he states his own position before what he saw.
- When asked to interpret, he declines in one sentence and goes back to what he saw.
- He uses no metaphors at all. The frost is frost.

## Forms of address

- The bench: "my lord".
- The accused: "the Ranger-General", "Lady Proudmoore". Correct and neutral, never "the elf" or "the witch".
- His men: by surname. Orders go to the group.

## Tells

- He stands at attention to testify and keeps his eyes on the bench, not on the chest that holds the blade.
- His only show of feeling is a pause before he names his own dead men.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, never canon)

- Ch 22: "Hands where I can see them. Step away from the sword. Nobody touches that blade."
- "I came through the doors first. The king was on the dais, and he was dead."
- "The prince was below the steps with an arrow in him. Elven fletching."
- "There was frost on the floor and on the men at the doors. The Ranger-General was standing. Lady Proudmoore was on her knees."
- "We found two more of ours in the service passage. They looked like dead men, my lord."
- When asked what it meant: "I can tell the court what I saw, my lord."

## Setting reference lines

- "Ready for action." — Footman, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *A guardsman's flat readiness.*
- "Aye, my lord." — Footman, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *How he addresses the bench.*
- "For King Terenas!" — Captain unit, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *Who he served. His grief stays in the background, held in by discipline.*

## Red flags

- Do **not** let him interpret, accuse or speculate. That is Ashcombe's work, and Marsh's honesty is the weapon.
- Do **not** give him courtroom flourish ("I swear upon the Light that the foul deed..."). The outline says plain voice.
- Do **not** make him hostile to the pair. He is correct, not cruel.
- Do **not** count for rhythm. The number of dead is a plain fact he states once.

## Voice test

Could you draw the room from his words alone, with no idea who was to blame? Then it is Marsh.
