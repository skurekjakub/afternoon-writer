# Voice Sheet: Captain Aldric Havel

*Story mode: an Andorhal militia captain at the post by the market gate. On a first read he is a harried, honest local officer who knows his streets and wants things done properly. On a second read, every offer he makes steers the party onto routes, into shelters and toward timings that he controls. He is on cult coin. He appears in Ch 7–8 and dies by Sylvanas's arrow when he turns the trap.*

## Grounding

- Sources: `supporting.md` (harried, honest, solving the wrong-sized problem). That entry describes his **cover**. The outline Ch 6–8 gives the truth: the tally-man sends the party to him; he gives them the street-level truth (names, shifts, the supplier change three months ago, workers who move like soldiers); he keeps redrawing the same safer lanes, pushing dawn timing and militia control so the arrest "looks proper"; he looks worse after the curse; and his debts vanished when the supplier routes changed.
- **The rule for the writer:** his information is real, and his help is real until the moment it isn't. The tell is never in how he speaks. It is only in **where his advice always points**. Ch 6 says the prose must not explain the plant.

## Baseline register

- A capable, tired officer who is glad someone has finally come. He sounds warm and practical, and a little proud of how well he knows his city.
- He talks about his streets like a man who walks them: lanes, yards, shift bells, who drinks where.
- He says "we" early and often, and treats the party as his to look after.
- He is procedural because he has been burned by magistrates before. He wants arrests that hold up.
- He never argues hard. He offers again, more reasonably each time.

## Syntax rules

- He gives a concrete fact first (a name, a lane, a shift), then an offer ("I've men on both ends of it").
- His steering is always phrased as care for them or for doing things properly, never as preference: "safer", "proper", "my lads can close it".
- When he is overruled he agrees out loud, then redraws the same lane a few lines later.
- After the curse he speaks faster, says "we" more, and reaches for shelters: "a cellar I trust", "a yard that's mine".
- He asks one helpful-sounding question about the party's formation ("Who's on your back on a raid like this?"). That is a second-read plant. Use it once and never underline it.

## Forms of address

- Sylvanas: "Ranger-General", correct on the first try. He is the one human who gets it right without being told. Do not comment on it.
- Jaina: "Lady Proudmoore", or "my lady".
- Corin: "lad", or "the Dalaran boy". Affectionate, dismissive.
- His men: "lads".

## Tells

- Scarred hands, a well-used truncheon. He points with two fingers held flat like a knife.
- He spreads district notes against a wall and redraws the same lanes.
- After the curse: sweat at his collar and a smile held too long.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, never canon)

- "Half the granary men send people to me. I'm the one who answers."
- "It started on the mill lanes. The new supplier came in after the thaw. His loaders don't drink, don't dice, and they go home in pairs."
- "Take the river lane. I've men on both ends of it, so nobody gets behind you there."
- "Wait for dawn. The mill changes shift and my lads can close the street. You want this done so a magistrate can't throw it out."
- After the curse: "We need you off the street. I've a cellar off Cooper's Yard. It's dry, and it's mine."
- At the break: "This way! The yard's clear—"

## Setting reference lines

- "How may I serve?" — Captain unit, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *An officer's readiness to serve.*
- "Lousy pay, constant danger." — Captain unit, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *An ordinary officer's grumble. It makes him human and gives him a motive.*
- "It must be something in the water, I tell you." — Stratholme Citizen, WoW: Wrath of the Lich King (set in the Third War). *A local's confident guess.*

## Red flags

- Do **not** give him any villain tell in his wording: no sly asides, no double meanings, no oily flattery, no eyes that "glint".
- Do **not** make him unhelpful. His names and shifts are true and useful.
- Do **not** let the narration or another character name his pattern before Ch 8.
- Do **not** give him a road-blessing farewell. That tell belongs to Mal'Ganis alone, and Havel is only his hired man.
- Do **not** turn his last moment into a confession speech. He breaks for his route with a shout, and the arrow ends it.

## Voice test

On a first read, would the reader want him on their side? On a second read, does every offer lead to a lane he chose? It needs both to be Havel.
