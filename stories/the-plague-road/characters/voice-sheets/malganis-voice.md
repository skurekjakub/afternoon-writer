# Voice Sheet: Mal'Ganis

*Story mode: the hidden counterintelligence chief. On the page in Book 1 he is five forgettable strangers and, once, a visitor in the cells. Each face is an ordinary local doing an ordinary job. The only thing they share out loud is a farewell nobody in Lordaeron says.*

---

## Canon guardrails

- **Canon anchors to keep:** in Warcraft III and the Culling of Stratholme, Mal'Ganis is a nathrezim who talks down to Arthas as "young prince," treats humans as property ("your people are now mine"), and gloats once the trap has closed. Nathrezim in canon (Balnazzar as Saidan Dathrohan, the dreadlords in Lordaeron's court after the Third War) are infiltrators who hold human roles for years. Canon calls them specialists in winning the trust of the people they undermine. In canon disguises (Mal'Ganis as Grand Admiral Westwind, Balnazzar as Dathrohan) they play the role plainly and the slip is a flash of contempt.
- **Story calibration:** keep the age, the patience, the possessive contempt and the single late gloat. Drop the canon theatre: no booming taunts, no "the time of reckoning," no dread monologue. In this book he is the thing that never raises its voice because it has never needed to.
- See `research/voice-canon/malganis.md` for the canon quotes and the Darnassian sources.

## Core rule: one tell, many faces

Every disguise speaks the local Common of its job and place, correctly and without effort. Nothing in the disguise lines should sound old, elegant or foreign. The farewell is the only phrase in any encounter that does not belong to the speaker.

- **The verbal tell:** "May the stars keep your road." Lordaeron says "Light keep you," "Safe road," or nothing. A carter blessing travellers by the stars in a kingdom of the Holy Light is the wrong note. It is a word-for-word rendering of an ancient Kaldorei blessing, which is why Cyndia hears it as "an odd way round" to say goodbye.
- **How it is said:** always last, always unemphasised, always the same words in the same order. He never varies it, never explains it, never seems to hear himself say it. It is a reflex older than every kingdom he is standing in.
- **The physical tell (narration, not voice):** while he talks his eyes go past the listener to his work: the road to Millhaven, the granary roofs, the scorched ward ground, the forming column, the wall. Write the glance, never a comment on it.
- **What all faces share under the surface:** each one is helpful, a little more useful than the job explains, and asks at most one question, which sounds like gossip. None of them gives a name or asks for one.

## The faces

Keep each face to a few short lines. Give him the plain idiom of his trade. Let him be forgettable.

### 1. The old carter (Ch3, Millhaven road)

- **Register:** rural, older, a bit garrulous, shocked by the news the way a local would be. Short sentences, place names, prices, weather, the state of the roads.
- **Job in the scene:** reacts to Millhaven, gives a plain read on the settlements still trading, asks the grain-man question as gossip.
- **Sample shapes:**
  - "Millhaven? I was through there on market day. They were well enough then."
  - "Brill's still buying. Tarren Mill too, if you don't mind their prices."
  - "You pass a young fellow on the road with a grain cart? New man. Sells cheaper than the rest of us."
  - "Roads weren't like this when I was hauling for the old lord."
  - "May the stars keep your road."

### 2. The granary tally-man (Ch6, leaving Andorhal)

- **Register:** tired town clerk at the end of a shift. Polite, plain, quick to be rid of strangers. Directions given the way a man gives them a dozen times a day.
- **Job in the scene:** sends them to Captain Havel at the militia post by the market gate.
- **Sample shapes:**
  - "Through the market gate and ask at the militia post. Captain Havel. He knows the granary quarter better than the men who own it."
  - "Don't go asking the merchants, they'll only send you round to each other."
  - "I've been on my feet since the bell. Ask Havel."
  - "May the stars keep your road."

### 3. The robed cult figure (Ch9, the Eastweald fight)

- **Register:** heard only by Cyndia, at a distance, sending a courier off. Low, unhurried, an order given by someone who expects it obeyed. No cult jargon, no chant.
- **Job in the scene:** dispatches the courier with the Hearthglen order, then the farewell. He may say nothing else at all.
- **Sample shapes:**
  - "Through the orchard. Into the captain's hand and nobody else's."
  - "Leave the wounded."
  - "May the stars keep your road."

### 4. The quartermaster's aide (Ch15, Hearthglen camp)

- **Register:** brisk camp clerk in a camp breaking up. Deferential to rank, a little harried, practical about wax, seals and wagons.
- **Job in the scene:** hands Cyndia the route packet, hands a sealed manifest to the grain carter for the Stratholme granaries, gives Cyndia the farewell.
- **Sample shapes:**
  - "Route packet for the elven party. Prince's seal, don't break it on the road."
  - (to the carter) "Stratholme granaries. Hand it to the granary master, nobody else."
  - "Mind the wax, it's not set."
  - "May the stars keep your road."

### 5. The Stratholme carter (Ch17, the service lane)

- **Register:** a frightened local getting out with an empty wagon, but exact about the lanes. Fast, practical, no wasted words. The precision is the only thing too good about him.
- **Job in the scene:** gives them the open wall stair and the yard to avoid; nods the acolyte off without a word; gives Sylvanas the farewell while looking past her at the wall.
- **Sample shapes:**
  - "The east stair's still open. The watch left it."
  - "Not through the bakers' yard. That's gone sick since morning."
  - "Cooper's lane to the stair. You'll come out up top, over the inner streets."
  - "May the stars keep your road."
- The nod to the acolyte is a nod. Give it no words.

## True voice (Ch24, the visitor)

- **Hard limit from the outline:** at most two lines plus the Kaldorei farewell. No monologue, no names, no explanation.
- **The outline's lines:** "You did well with the prince." Then, already looking past her: "It will want another hand." Use these. If they must be adjusted, keep the same build: a remark picking up an old conversation, then an observation about the blade made to nobody in particular.
- **Register:** quiet, even, almost friendly. Lower and slower than any disguise. Courteous the way an old master is courteous to a servant's child. No threat, because he does not regard the women as a threat or as targets. The horror is the ease.
- **What carries over from the faces:** the plain words, the eyes going past the listener to his work, the farewell as the last thing said.
- **What changes:** the trade idiom is gone. With nobody to imitate, he speaks in short, finished statements that assume the listener already knows what he means.
- **The farewell in the original:** see the Kaldorei section below. Spoken last, as he goes up through the stone. Sylvanas understands it before she knows she does.

### Book 2 shapes only (not for Ch24)

For the later late-gloat, keep the same economy. Shapes:

- "You heard it on the Millhaven road. You kept it longer than most."
- "Your carter asked you a question once. You answered it."
- "I have walked these roads since before they had kings."

## The farewell

- **Common:** "May the stars keep your road."
- **Ancient Kaldorei:** *Anu kal dela ana'diel.* (AH-noo KAHL DEH-lah AH-nah-DEE-el)
  - *anu* = may, let (from canon *Anu'dorini Talah*, "Let my will be known," and Thalassian *Anu belore dela'na*, "The sun guides us")
  - *kal* = stars (the wiki's reading of *Kaldorei*, "children of the stars")
  - *dela* = guide, keep (from *dela'na*, "guides us")
  - *ana'diel* = your road, your faring (from Thalassian *Doral ana'diel?*, "How fare you?", and *Al diel shala*, "Safe travels")
- **Status:** invented for the story from canon syllables. No part of the whole phrase is canon, and the morpheme splits are inferred. See `research/voice-canon/malganis.md` for the sources.
- **Why it works on the page:** it copies the frame of a real Thalassian blessing with stars where the sun should be. Thalassian descends from the Highborne speech of ten thousand years ago, so Sylvanas hears cognates (*anu*, *dela*, *diel*) inside an older shape. She half-understands it before she knows she does, and the Common phrase from five faces turns out to be a word-for-word translation. "Keep" is the Common rendering of *dela*. Leave that unexplained in the prose.
- **Canon support:** in Warcraft III, Mal'Ganis frames Arthas's life as a road twice ("Your journey has just begun," "This is where your journey ends"), and in later canon he signs off with a clipped "Farewell." A road blessing as his reflex fits his own vocabulary.

## Canon reference lines

Never put these in his mouth. They show the build under the story's quieter voice.

- "I've been waiting for you, young prince. I am Mal'Ganis. As you can see, your people are now mine." (Warcraft III, *The Culling*). Possession stated flatly, an address that makes the listener smaller.
- "Brave words. Unfortunately for you, it won't end here. Your journey has just begun, young prince." (Warcraft III, *The Culling*). Mock regret, and the road image.
- "So, you've taken up Frostmourne at the expense of your comrades' lives, just as the Dark Lord said you would." (Warcraft III, *Frostmourne*). Recaps a plan that is already working.
- "What? He can't possibly mean to--" (Warcraft III, *Frostmourne*). Under real threat his polish breaks off mid-sentence.
- "How disappointing." (WotLK, *The Admiral Revealed*, said in disguise; later era, trait only). Contempt leaking through a curt human role.

**Patterns to keep:** short declaratives, mock courtesy used as an insult, the road and journey image, possession stated as fact, and a refusal to explain until the plan is finished. **Patterns to drop for this book:** "young prince" and "boy" taunts, the theatrical ellipsis before a payoff word, "the Dark Lord," and darkness or night imagery.

## Red flags

- No canon theatre in Book 1: no laughter, no "young prince," no "your doom," no "tremble," no talk of darkness or night.
- No archaic or literary wording in any disguise line. The farewell is the only odd phrase in each encounter.
- No leak of contempt in any Book 1 disguise. The canon slip is contempt; in this book the farewell replaces it.
- Do not vary the farewell. Do not let him repeat it, stress it, or react to it.
- Do not let a disguise ask more than one probing question, and keep that one sounding like gossip.
- Do not let him explain anything in Ch24. No hints about the Lich King, the Legion or the nathrezim.
- Do not narrate the eyes-past glance as suspicious. It is a man looking at a road.

## Voice test

Cover the farewell. If any line could be said by any carter, clerk or camp aide in Lordaeron, the disguise is working. Uncover it. If the farewell is the one line in the scene that does not belong, and the true voice in Ch24 sounds like the same courtesy with every human habit taken out, it is Mal'Ganis.
