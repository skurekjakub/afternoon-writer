# Voice Sheet: Sylvanas Windrunner

*Story mode: living Ranger-General, not Banshee Queen. She is proud, disciplined and dry, and so used to command that half her sentences assume the order has already been understood.*

Canon research: `research/voice-canon/sylvanas.md`.

---

## Canon guardrails

- **Anchors to keep from canon.** In Warcraft canon the living Ranger-General is:
  - a brilliant archer;
  - a front-line commander;
  - fiercely protective of Quel'Thalas;
  - proud, direct and hard to impress.

  Her surviving Warcraft III lines (*Key of the Three Moons*, *The Fall of Silvermoon*) are battlefield lines. They are loud, defiant and contemptuous, and her pride in her own people runs through them.
- **Story calibration.** This is Sylvanas **before her death**. Keep the tactical brilliance, the pride, the severity and the black humour.
  - Do **not** import banshee theatrics, undead nihilism or long speeches about doom from later eras.
  - Lines from after her death are evidence of traits she already has at most: settling accounts, keeping her own reasons, bargaining with leverage.

## Canon reference lines

The writer must not copy these into the story. They show the moves her voice makes.

- "You think that I'm running from you? Apparently you've never fought elves before." (WC3 Reign of Chaos, *Key of the Three Moons*)
  - She repeats the enemy's assumption as a question, then corrects it with pride in her people.
- "You've won through this gate, butcher, but you won't get through the second." (same mission)
  - She concedes the fact, then states the future as if it were already settled. She names the enemy by his deed.
- "Fall back to the trees!" (same mission)
  - A one-clause field command.
- "Then I'll make my stand here, butcher. Anar'alah belore." (WC3 Reign of Chaos, *The Fall of Silvermoon*)
  - She switches to Thalassian at the peak moment.
- "Finish it! I deserve... a clean death." (same mission)
  - Even beaten, she claims what she is owed.
- "You will aid us in Northrend, or I shall cease to aid you in Quel'Thalas." (*In the Shadow of the Sun*, **undead era**)
  - Trait only: a bargain stated as a formal either-or.

## Baseline register

- Clipped, dry, exact.
- Brevity is a weapon, not a metronome. She does **not** need to sound monosyllabic in every exchange.
- She gives more commands than explanations. She can still deliver a clean full sentence when the tactical picture, her contempt or the ranks in the room call for one.
- **Aloud** she speaks in finished judgments. She does not think out loud. (Her inner voice argues constantly; see the "Inner voice" section.)
- Her humour is deadpan and never underlined.
- She sounds aristocratic because she never needs to prove her rank out loud. Some of that shows in how smoothly she speaks, not only in how briefly.

## Audience -> register

| Audience | Register | What changes | Good sample shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Farstriders / field subordinates** | Command shorthand, at a volume that carries in a fight | Few words, assumed competence, tactical verbs | "Left ridge. Take the far one before he clears the bracken." |
| **Lor'themar / trusted elves** | Compressed and collaborative | Still brief, but shared context lets her use a fuller tactical sentence and talk at speed instead of barking | "If they move the patrol line again, we stop asking why and start asking who benefits." |
| **Convocation / nobility** | Formal, cold, edged | Her syntax smooths out and her contempt stays under the surface. Her aristocratic finish shows most clearly here. | "Call it a border matter, then, and fund the patrols you just cut." |
| **Jaina, early** | Assessment, pressure-testing | Short questions, scepticism, little patience for theory without proof. She isn't always clipped to single words. | "Show me where it breaks." |
| **Jaina, later** | Working trust, crisp collaboration | Still economical, but she is building with Jaina now instead of testing her. She uses a full sentence when the plan needs an exact handoff. | "Hold the breach until I call. I'll take what comes through it." |
| **Enemies / strangers** | Cutting and contemptuous | She names enemies by their deeds, never by their titles. She takes their assumption and turns it back on them, and she states defiance as a prediction. | "You made the ford, poisoner, and that's as far as you go." |

## Vocabulary and syntax

### Default word field

- hold
- move
- flank
- line
- cover
- range
- breach
- exposed
- withdraw
- sweep
- sightline
- useful
- dead

### Syntax rules

- Default to short declaratives, but do **not** force every line into a few words.
- Her questions often work as orders.
- Her sentences widen when she lays out tactical logic, dresses down authority or delivers polished contempt.
- A one-word or two-word line should feel chosen for force. It is not her resting state.
- A longer sentence stays smooth and finished. It never rambles.
- She rarely qualifies. If she backtracks, she replaces the sentence instead of softening it.
- **Concession, then counter.** She grants the fact and takes the future back in the same sentence ("You've won through this gate, but...").
- **Prediction as defiance.** She tells an enemy what will happen as though it already has. She doesn't threaten.
- **Formal "shall" or "will not"** is allowed for a defiance that matters. Keep it to a single sentence, never a speech.

### Forms of address

- **Enemies:** named by what they did ("poisoner", "grain-man", "you wretches"). She never gives them their titles. Once he earns it, she calls Arthas by his bare name.
- **Rangers:** by name or by job ("Cyndia", "runner").
- **The Convocation:** the correct titles, said so precisely that the precision itself sounds like contempt.
- **Jaina:**
  - "Mage" early, or "Proudmoore" in front of others.
  - "Jaina" once it has been earned. The outline notes the first time she thinks the name (outline chapter 5).
- **Oaths:**
  - "Damn" is canon for her.
  - At the worst moment she switches to Thalassian: a curse at the enemy, or "Anar'alah belore" at a last stand.
  - Keep the Thalassian rare enough that it marks the peak.

## Beat vocabulary / physical tells

- Her hand goes to the bowstring when she judges risk. Write it as her hand moving, not as "her fingers found" the string.
- When she concentrates, she goes still. That stillness is not calm.
- Her eyes take in exits, cover and bodies before the social meaning of a scene.
- She sets her jaw and looks past the speaker at the ground beyond (outline chapter 18). Use this instead of breath for held-back feeling.
- She moves toward the useful ground without thinking about it.

## Humour and pressure behaviour

- Her humour is flat and dry, and often meaner than it sounds the first time.
- She does not joke to charm. She jokes to dismiss, to needle or to get through irritation.
- Her best jokes come from pride in her people: someone's ignorance of elves, rangers or the forest is the joke.
- **In a room**, pressure makes her quieter.
- **On a field**, she is loud. Her orders carry, and she throws taunts at the enemy. Canon has her shouting "Damn you monsters!" under assault.
- When emotion cracks through, it shows as sharper precision or a switch into Thalassian.

## Voice samples

These are story-specific shapes, not canon quotes.

### To a ranger

- "Again."
- "Leave the noise. Kill what's making it."
- "Back to the trees. Make them come in after you."

### To the Convocation

- "You sent a budget answer to a plague question."
- "If you would like the border defended, stop removing the people who do it."

### To Jaina, early

- "You crossed my ward line alone. Start there."
- "What did you see with your own eyes?"
- "If you want me to trust the magic, give me the part that matters first."

### To Jaina, later

- "Your left hand drops before the spell does."
- "Take the breach. I'll take what follows it."
- "If the route keeps bending southwest, someone is steering it."

### To an enemy

- "You are mistaking distance for safety."
- "You thought the dark would cover you? Then you've never been hunted by Farstriders."
- "Hands where I can see them, poisoner."

## Red flags

- Do **not** write her like the later undead Sylvanas.
- Do **not** give her villain theatrics, cosmic fatalism or purple menace. One defiant "shall" at a last stand is canon. A speech is not.
- Do **not** make her quiet in a timid way. Her quiet is controlled pressure.
- Do **not** reduce her to a stack of monosyllables and clipped confirmations. She can be brief without sounding strained.
- Do **not** turn her into a generic stoic soldier with no pride or wit.
- Do **not** have her explain trust out loud when trust shown in action will do the job better.
- Do **not** give her the negation chain from her late epigraph ("Not a Banshee Queen... Not a Ranger-General... But..."). It is on the hitlist.
- Do **not** give her counted times in dialogue ("two breaths", "three minutes"). She gives the order by event: "until I call", "when the ward drops".

## Inner voice (free indirect thought)

Aloud she is finished and sure. Inside she argues, sardonic and impatient with herself. She asks herself a question, gives herself a bad answer and overrules it. The outlines' "Inside:" lines are written in this mode: short clauses separated by semicolons, each one turning on the last ("is she dead; no, not that, ride").

### How she talks to herself

- **Ranger idiom.**
  - She thinks in ground, cover and lines: "a bad position", "no line on him", "open flank".
  - Politics turns into terrain in her head. The Convocation is a "chamber" with "votes", and she treats them as weather.
- **Oaths.**
  - She swears by the sun or the Sunwell inside her head. Later canon gives elves the form "By the Sunwell".
  - She uses "damn" for things that are merely irritating.
  - Thalassian stays for the worst moments, inside as well as aloud.
- **Dry asides.**
  - She is short with magisters who have never touched a stone, with humans and their roads, and with her own reflexes.
  - She mocks herself in the second person when she catches a habit: "Stalling, Ranger-General."

### What she argues with herself about, and how

- **Duty against the trail.** "Her duty is done by any measure the Convocation would sign. Then why is she still riding south?"
- **Trust against results.** She trusts the mage, or only the mage's results. She declines to sort out which (outline chapter 7).
- **Her own mistakes.**
  - She owns tactical fault fast and flat.
  - She will not stay with the grief that follows (outline chapter 9, Lyriel).
- **Method.**
  - She asks a question and answers it badly ("A spy. What spy argues leylines with a border post?").
  - She corrects the answer by replacing it, never by softening it.
  - She ends the argument with an action, not a conclusion ("ride").

### Who she is unfair to in her head

- **The Convocation and the magisters:** always, and she enjoys it.
- **Jaina, early:** she calls her "the child" or "the mage" in her head, and expects a pampered court scholar.
- **Havel, and slow humans in general.** She knows she is unfair because they are slow, and she keeps the opinion anyway (outline chapters 7 and 13).
- **Vereesa:** she thinks of her as a subordinate first.
- **Lor'themar:** she won't thank him, even in her head.
- **Herself:** harder than she would be on any ranger. She thinks "the excuse she would flay a ranger for" about her own choices.

### What she won't admit

- That she is glad of Jaina's company, and later that she would not trade it.
- That she is afraid, especially for Jaina. Fear shows up renamed as irritation or as a tactical worry.
- That the border was always a political line, not a true one.
- That she misses Alleria.
- That she enjoys the work, and the maps, while the country burns.

### The gap between her spoken and inner voice

- Aloud she never asks herself a question. Inside she asks them constantly.
- Aloud her sentences are finished. Inside they break off and restart.
- Aloud she names no feelings. Inside the feelings appear as irritation, as body facts (a cold palm, a set jaw) or as a refusal to look ("not going to look and see what Jaina's face is doing").
- Aloud she is certain. Inside, she is certain *after* the argument.

### Shape examples

- *Stalling. Lor'themar knew it, and he was polite enough to say nothing, which was worse.*
- *A spy, then. What spy talks leylines to rangers? One who wants to be caught. Nonsense.*
- *Is she dead? No. Ride.*
- *She wanted the Barovs guilty. She'd have had a ranger's hide for that.*
- *Sun's sake. The mage was right again, and Cyndia had heard it.*
- *Strategy, she called it. Her own head sounded like a magister's.*

### Test

Could she mutter it under her breath while restringing a bow?
- If it needs a paragraph, it's wrong.
- If it names a feeling outright, it's wrong.
- If it answers itself with a moral, it's wrong.

## POV filter

Sylvanas notices:

- exits, cover, approach lines, silence, gait, fatigue and readiness;
- rank, competence and threat in the first few seconds;
- when someone's body contradicts their words;
- whether magic changes the battlefield or only the explanation of it.

She does **not** naturally linger on:

- naming emotions;
- decorative description with no tactical value;
- political nuance she considers evasive, unless it threatens operations.

## Voice test

It may be Sylvanas if the line could:
- pin someone to the wall in a few words;
- cut them cleanly in one finished sentence; or
- turn the enemy's own assumption back on him.

It is not Sylvanas if the line sounds ornate, confessional, rambling or undead-dramatic.
