# Voice Sheet: Captain Falric

*Arthas's captain. Country-born, competent, loyal to the man before the office. In this book he is the camp gatekeeper (Ch11), the officer who turns Arthas's first clear command into movement (Ch15), and the working edge of the Culling (Ch16 to Ch17). Minor sheet.*

Canon is moderately thin: a handful of novel lines and some Warcraft III "Captain" lines whose attribution is disputed. See `research/voice-canon/falric.md`.

## Baseline register

- Plain Lordaeron soldier's speech. Short to medium sentences, report first, then his read on it.
- Deferential in form, honest in content. He asks leave, then says the blunt thing.
- Rural roots show in what he knows (farms, roads, who sells grain where) and in the odd country turn of phrase. No heavy dialect.
- With outsiders he is a wall of rank and procedure. With Arthas he is fast.

## Syntax rules

- Opens with the address when speaking to Arthas: "Sir, ..." or "Highness, ..."
- When he disagrees, he hedges first ("If I may, sir...") and then says it straight. He never disobeys.
- Orders to men are short imperatives with a place in them: "Column on the Stratholme road."
- Under pressure he gets choppier and blunter, and keeps the "sir."

## Forms of address

- **Arthas:** "sir" in private and in the tent; "Prince Arthas" or "Highness" in front of others.
- **Sylvanas:** "Ranger-General," correct and unmoved.
- **Jaina:** "Lady Proudmoore," following his prince's lead in Ch15.
- **Uther:** "Lord Uther," respectful and careful. In Ch17 he does not look at him.
- **Marwyn and the men:** surnames or ranks; "Marwyn" plain.

## Tells

- **Oath:** "Light preserve us." Mild and sincere. No profanity beyond a soldier's "damn."
- **Fear:** he would rather die than rise as one of the dead. In the Culling that fear makes the order easier for him to carry. Never state it; let it show in how quickly he moves.
- **Motion:** he is turning for the tent flap before the order is finished.

## Sample shapes

- (Ch11) "The prince is in council, Ranger-General. You can wait or you can give it to me."
- (Ch11) "If it's the orcs again, he's had three reports today."
- (Ch11) "Grain? Wait here."
- (Ch15) "I don't know him. Half the clerks in this camp came up with the Hearthglen levies."
- (Ch15) "Column forms on the Stratholme road. Move!"
- (Ch17, to Arthas) "If I may, sir. The men will do it. I'd only ask we start where the sick are."
- (Ch17, to the men) "You heard him. Granary gate first."

## Canon reference lines

- "Sir, if I may. I'm... well... I would rather be hacked into a thousand pieces than turn into one of them undead." (Golden, *Arthas: Rise of the Lich King*, via the Warcraft Wiki). Asks leave, stumbles, then says it bluntly.
- "Light preserve us. I'd thought things like this were just stories to scare children." (same novel). His oath, and a countryman's frame.
- "I grew up in this area, sir, and most of these farmers are the independent sort." (same novel). Local knowledge offered as a report.

## Red flags

- No undead gloating from Halls of Reflection, and no relish in the Culling.
- No court flourish, no long speeches, no theology beyond his oath.
- Do not make him a brute or a coward. He is a good officer following the wrong man.
- Do not let him argue with Arthas past one hedge.

## Voice test

Could a farm boy who made captain say it to his prince with his helmet under his arm? Does it sound respectful and still tell the truth? If it sounds like a courtier or a thug, it is wrong.
