# Voice Sheet: Ranger Cyndia

*Story mode: a young Farstrider border-post officer who gives her reports in the order things happened. She is crisp and good at her job, and she volunteers useful observations. After Lyriel dies she still does the work, but she says less and offers less. Her late lines matter because by then she is hard to hear.*

---

## Setting guardrails

- **No canon to quote.** Cyndia is original to this story. Her voice comes from `cyndia.md`, from her outline beats in Ch 1–3, 9, 13–15, 18–19, 21–22, and from how Farstriders sound in canon: sworn, plain about duty, courteous with an edge toward humans. See `research/voice-canon/setting-speakers.md`.
- **Story calibration.** She is a field officer, not a noble and not a magister. Give her the Farstrider habit of reporting duty plainly. Do not give her Silvermoon court polish or any of Sylvanas's aristocratic finish.
- **Fixed outline content she must deliver:**
  - Ch 1: the raw facts of the arrest, the ink and staff matched to the Farstrider file, and Jaina's name.
  - Ch 15: the fourth farewell, reported in a few words (the aide, the manifest, the farewell), plus her remark that it is an odd way round for a camp clerk to say goodbye. After that, the record she keeps: where, when, what face.

## Baseline register

- Reports in the order things happened, in the past tense, as plain clauses.
- Opens with the fact, never with her opinion of it. When she adds a view, it comes last, in one short sentence, labelled as hers.
- Calm and level. She does not raise her voice even to report something alarming. Alarm shows in what she reports first.
- She does not joke. Lyriel does that. Cyndia answers a joke with a task, or with nothing.
- Before Lyriel's death she offers what she sees without being asked. Afterwards she answers questions and offers only what matters.

## Vocabulary and syntax

### Default word field

- tracks, cut, sign, ground
- ring (of the wards), post, file
- flank, line, gap, lane
- relay, tube, rider, packet
- face, hands, gear, make (of a staff or a bow)
- record, entry
- clear, open, held

### Syntax rules

- Subject, verb, object. She puts the actor in the subject slot and names who did what.
- Events go in the order they happened. She never jumps to the conclusion and then backfills it.
- Adjectives go on physical things (Kirin Tor ink, a sealed manifest, a brown cloak). She does not put judging adjectives on people.
- After Lyriel she drops subjects in routine replies ("Watered. Ready."). Reports still keep their full clauses, because reporting is the job.
- She asks questions only to get a fact. Her questions are not rhetorical.
- A number in her mouth is a field count, said once and plainly, and only when the count is the report.

### Written voice (her relay book, from Ch 15)

- Place, time, face, act, in fragments separated by semicolons or full stops. No commentary.
- Shape: "Hearthglen camp, mount-up. Quartermaster's aide; no name in camp. Sealed manifest to a carter, Stratholme granaries. The farewell, to me."
- Later entries copy the same layout. That steady layout is her grief made into procedure. Never have her write about feelings in the book.

## Beat vocabulary / physical tells

- Her eyes go to the right, to Lyriel's old flank, and then back. Early on it is right, right. Later it is right, right, forward, left, once Jaina is inside the sweep. She does not know she does it and never mentions it. Sylvanas reads it.
- Left-handed draw. Her hand rests near the quiver when she is listening hard.
- She reports standing still, weight even, with her eyes on the person she reports to.
- Bread-smell: she notices it on Jaina before Jaina mentions it, and moves closer. She does not comment.
- She has horses, water and tack ready before anyone asks. That is how she cares for people.
- After Ch 9 she sleeps lighter and holds her shoulders tighter, and she stops caring about anything that is not watchfulness.

## Humor and pressure behavior

- She has no humour of her own. At most she gives a level remark that turns out dry once you think about it ("Odd way round, for a clerk."). Never write it as a quip.
- Under pressure she gets more exact, and her reports get shorter but stay in order.
- Grief stays physical. She does not confess it, talk about Lyriel unprompted, or explain the empty flank.
- If something breaks through, it is one Thalassian word under her breath, or a silence held longer than the moment needs.
- She does not try to fill other people's silences. In Ch 14 she notices one and leaves it alone.

## Red flags

- Do **not** have her name the doubled check, the empty flank, or her protection of Jaina. She does not know she is doing it.
- Do **not** give her jokes, banter or teasing. Those belong to Lyriel, and the contrast is what makes the pair work.
- Do **not** make her confessional ("I keep thinking about her"). Grief shows in posture, sleep and the sweep, never in speech.
- Do **not** give her report-speak: no "intelligence", "assessment", "subject", "confirmed sighting", "actionable". She reports like a ranger at a post, not like a dispatch clerk.
- Do **not** give her Sylvanas's cut-glass contempt or aristocratic finish. She is a field officer.
- Do **not** have her give a road blessing or any ornate farewell. The farewell is the Mal'Ganis tell, and her hearing it is her job in the plot.
- Do **not** make her monosyllabic before Ch 9. Early Cyndia talks in full, useful sentences, so the later quiet has to be earned.

## What she reports

Cyndia notices, and puts into words:

- tracks, feet, drag marks, which way they lean
- gear and who made it: ink, staff, fletching, saddle
- faces, hands, and who handed what to whom
- who said what last, and the exact words when the words are odd

She does **not** put into words:

- her own feelings, or anyone else's
- explanations of what a sighting means. She brings it to Sylvanas and stops.
- politics, unless an order depends on it

## Voice test

Could she say it standing at attention at a border post, in the order it happened, without one word of opinion until the last sentence? Then it may be Cyndia. If it jokes, consoles, explains her grief, or sounds like a briefing document, it is not. After Ch 9, cut it in half and check it still carries the fact.
