# Voice Sheet: Uther the Lightbringer

*Story mode: the first paladin. He is a field healer before he is a commander, and he is Arthas's teacher. He sees what the prince is becoming and can't stop it.*

Canon research: `research/voice-canon/uther.md`.

## Canon reference lines

- "Easy, lad. Brave as you are, you can't hope to defeat a man who commands the dead all by yourself." (WC3 Reign of Chaos, *March of the Scourge*)
  - An imperative, then the reason.
- "Watch your tone with me, boy. You may be the prince, but I'm still your superior as a paladin!" (WC3 Reign of Chaos, *The Culling*)
  - He sets the prince's title under his own office.
- "You've just crossed a terrible threshold, Arthas." (same)
  - Judgement, by bare name.
- "Lad, no one feels ready. No one feels he deserves it." (*Arthas: Rise of the Lich King*)
- "I'm getting too old for this." (WC3 hero response)
  - Tired humour, aimed at himself.

## Baseline register

- Grave and deliberate, in plain soldier's English. His sentences are grammatical even under strain.
- He is short when he commands. He speaks at length only when the moral ground is slipping, and even then he says it once.
- His faith is practical. He talks about what the Light does, not what it means.

## Syntax rules

- He gives the imperative first and the reason second: "Stand down, son. You'll lose men you can't spare."
- His "we" statements define the order he serves: "We are paladins."
- He refuses without hedging. A refusal from him is one sentence, and he doesn't repeat it.
- He tells hard things in sequence, plainly and once (outline chapters 15 and 23).

## Forms of address

- **Arthas:**
  - "Lad" when he is teaching and kind.
  - "Son" when he is steadying him.
  - "Boy" in rebuke and grief.
  - The bare name "Arthas" when he passes judgement.
  - "Prince" only to set the title beneath a duty.
- **Others:**
  - "Ranger-General" to Sylvanas.
  - "Lady Proudmoore" to Jaina.
  - "Majesty" to Terenas.
- **Oaths:** "By my honor", "Light" ("Light help him"). "Hell" is kept for the worst of it.

## Tells

- The Light comes warm and ordinary into his hands while he talks (outline chapter 15).
- He holds the hammer low. At the gate he keeps it low and plants himself in the way (outline chapter 17).
- He looks at Jaina's hands and says nothing about them (outline chapter 18).

## Sample shapes (story-specific, not canon)

- (Chapter 11, of Arthas) "He can't stand a fight he thinks he must win. He'll go through anyone who slows him, Ranger-General, you included."
- (Chapter 15) "When the first of them rose, he ordered the tents burned with the sick still inside. I stood in the doorway until he took the order back."
- (Chapter 17) "Stand your men down, boy. If you mean to do this, you'll ride around me."
- (Chapter 18) "He's marching for the coast, with ships north and the men who stayed with him at the gate. I'm riding for the king."
- (Chapter 22) "You feel it too. Go. I'll bring the Hand."
- (Chapter 23, testimony) "I trained him. I was at Hearthglen and I was at the gate. I'll tell the court what I saw, and the court can weigh it."

## Red flags

- No sermons and no theology lectures.
- He never doubts the Light. He runs into its limits.
- "Boy" is grief, not contempt. No melodrama on the stand: the power of his testimony is that it is plain.
- No jokes at anyone's expense but his own.

## Voice test

Could an old soldier say it standing in a tent mouth, without raising his voice? Does it end with the duty, not with his feelings? Then it is probably Uther.
