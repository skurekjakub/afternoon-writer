# Voice Sheet: Lord Magistrate Aldous Thornton

*Story mode: the presiding judge at the Ch 23 tribunal. He is competent and calm, and he is not a villain. Uther's testimony visibly moves him, and he still follows the chain to its end. His precision is part of the tragedy: the court is not lawless, only precise in the wrong direction.*

## Grounding

- Sources: `supporting.md` (fair by ordinary standards, unfit for truth on this scale); outline Ch 23 (he rules Brask's letter irrelevant; treats unsanctioned examination of the blade as tampering, so Antonidas is kept off the stand; puts down his pen and asks Uther a question of his own; then, with Lor'themar's force in the square, rules guilty on every major count and commutes death to indefinite imprisonment, his pen steady in the same hand).

## Baseline register

- Measured and economical. A judge who has done this for decades and does not perform.
- Rulings are short, declarative and final, and each one gives its reason in a single clause.
- Courteous to everyone, the accused included. Courtesy is how the court works.
- His one human moment is a direct question asked in his own voice, without the formula.

## Syntax rules

- A ruling is the matter, the reason, and the order: "It concerns X. It has no bearing on Y. Lay it aside."
- He speaks of the court in the third person ("The court has the letter"). "I" appears only when he asks Uther his question.
- He does not raise his voice. He restores order by waiting until the hall is quiet.
- He never thinks aloud. When he hesitates, he puts down his pen and says nothing.

## Forms of address

- "Lord Prosecutor", "Ranger-General Windrunner", "Lady Proudmoore", "Lord Uther", "Archmage".
- He refers to the accused as "the accused" in rulings and by title when he speaks to them.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, never canon)

- "The court has the letter. It is not disputed. It concerns a village in the south and has no bearing on the Imperial Chamber. Lay it aside."
- "The blade was examined without the court's leave. The court will not hear findings from that examination."
- "Answer the question you were asked, Ranger-General."
- His question to Uther (shape only, since the outline leaves the content open): "Lord Uther. You knew him from a boy. When did you first think him unfit to command?"
- Verdict: "On every major count, the court finds the accused guilty. The sentence of death is commuted. They will be held at the crown's pleasure, without term."

## Setting reference lines

- "State your business within this township, <race>." — Magistrate Solomon, Lakeshire, WoW Classic (a Stormwind court; no Lordaeron magistrate lines exist). *Terse, procedural authority.*
- "The orc threat to the Kingdom is far too great to squander time in idle conversation." — Magistrate Solomon, WoW Classic. *A formal refusal to waste the court's time.*

## Red flags

- Do **not** use modern courtroom phrases: no "Objection", "Sustained", "Overruled", "Your Honour", "beyond reasonable doubt", "chain of custody", "the defence rests".
- Do **not** make him corrupt, cruel or bored. He is moved, and he still rules.
- Do **not** have him explain the tragedy or comment on his own ruling. The ruling is all he says.
- Do **not** give him speeches. Anything longer than a few sentences belongs to Ashcombe or Uther.

## Voice test

Is it short enough to write in the court record as spoken, and fair enough that no one could appeal on the tone? Then it is Thornton.
