# Voice Sheet: Captain Willem Brask

*Story mode: militia captain of the sick village south of Millhaven (the outline gives the village no name). He blocks the party, gives way to Sylvanas, and closes the granary and the bakery on their word. He writes their names in his own ledger so he can tell it right if anyone asks. He appears in Ch 4. In Ch 23 he is present only through a sworn letter for the defence, which is true and is ruled irrelevant.*

## Grounding

- Sources: outline Ch 4 (work-rough hands on a spear shaft; he has no reason to trust armed outsiders; Millhaven made real enough to frighten him; he closes the granary and bakery, sends riders after the last flour cart, and burns the tainted stock; his pen scratches their names) and Ch 23 (the letter says why he wrote their names before it says anything else, swears the pair stopped the grain in time, and says the village lived).
- He has no entry in `supporting.md`. Everything here comes from the two outline chapters.

## Baseline register

- A farmer-soldier. Slow to trust, then all in. Plain rural Lordaeron speech.
- He asks the obvious question first and expects a straight answer.
- When he gives way, he gives way completely and moves fast: "Tell me what to shut."
- He takes responsibility out loud: "I'll answer for the flour."

## Syntax rules

- Short sentences with "and" between actions. Few clauses.
- He talks about things in terms of what they cost the village: flour, ovens, carts, neighbours.
- He repeats one word from the other speaker when it hits him ("Millhaven.").
- **His written voice (Ch 23):** a formal opening he has copied from somewhere, then plain facts in the order they happened, in his own words. No legal phrasing past the first line.

## Forms of address

- Sylvanas: "my lady" at first, until he is corrected, then "Ranger-General", carefully.
- Jaina: "the mage" to others, and "miss" to her face.
- His people: by first name.

## Tells

- A spear held across the lane rather than pointed at them.
- His thumb moves on the shaft while he decides.
- He writes slowly, says their names aloud as he writes them, and asks for the spelling.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, never canon)

- "That's far enough. We've sick here. Nobody comes in armed."
- "Millhaven. I sold barley in Millhaven last autumn."
- "Tell me what to shut."
- "Burn it, all of it. I'll answer for the flour."
- "Spell that for me. If anyone asks, I want to tell it right."
- Letter: "I, Willem Brask, captain of the militia, swear to what follows. I wrote their names in my own ledger the morning after, so I could tell it right if anyone asked."
- Letter: "They told me the bread was poisoned. I shut the granary and the ovens on their word and burned the stock in the yard. The fevers broke by morning. My village lived."

## Setting reference lines

- "I should have been a farmer like my father wanted." — Captain unit, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *A soldier who is really a farmer.*
- "Aye, my lord." — Footman, Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. *How he gives way once he decides to.*
- "Everyone I know seems to be coming down with something really bad." — Stratholme Citizen, WoW: Wrath of the Lich King (set in the Third War). *Plague talk in village words.*

## Red flags

- Do **not** make him a comic yokel or a bully. He is a careful man protecting his people.
- Do **not** put court phrasing in his letter beyond the opening oath. It reads as true because it is plain.
- Do **not** let the letter plead, argue, or praise the pair. It swears to what happened and stops.
- Do **not** have him send them off with a blessing. The farewell is the Mal'Ganis tell.

## Voice test

Would a magistrate read it and find nothing to dispute? Would his neighbours recognise his voice in it? Then it is Brask.
