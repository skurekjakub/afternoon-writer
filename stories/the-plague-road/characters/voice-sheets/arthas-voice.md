# Voice Sheet: Arthas Menethil

*Story mode: never POV. The golden prince, then the commander who cannot bear a fight he might lose, then Frostmourne's hand. Read him only from outside: what he says, how fast he says it, and what he stops saying.*

---

## Canon guardrails

- **Canon anchors to keep:** in Warcraft III and Christie Golden's *Arthas*, the prince is brave, likeable and quick, loves his people, and is impatient with anything that slows him. He jokes with Jaina, waves off his own rank with Uther, and asks real questions. Under pressure "we" turns into "my" and "I," he reaches for rank the moment an argument goes against him, and he frames atrocity as rescue. After Frostmourne he goes level and quiet, drops his contractions, and says terrible things softly.
- **Story calibration:** he dies in the throne room. He never becomes the Lich King. Do **not** import the Lich King's voice ("Frostmourne hungers," dominion speeches, mocking cosmic patience) or the death-knight unit lines. Post-Frostmourne Arthas in this book is the prince with the warmth taken out, not a new orator.
- See `research/voice-canon/arthas.md` for the quotes, sources and era flags.

## The arc in one rule

His vocabulary barely changes. What changes is what drops out. First the jokes go, then the reasons, then the questions, then the names. He gets louder before he goes quiet; stillness is the danger sign. By the throne room almost nothing is left but orders and one name.

| Stage | Chapters | What is still there | What has gone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The prince** | Ch6, the internment-camp night in memory | Warmth, names, reasons, courtesy, humour, the Light, "we" | Nothing yet, but he decides before the other person has finished |
| **Hearthglen and Stratholme** | Ch11, Ch15, Ch16, Ch17 | Rank, orders, yes-or-no questions, titles, "my" and "I" | Jokes, first names (except to override), reasons, asking twice |
| **After Frostmourne** | Ch20, Ch22 | Short, calm statements, orders, one "Jaina" | Questions, reasons, the Light, contractions, almost all names |

## Stage 1: The prince (Ch6)

### Register

- Warm, confident, courteous, used to being liked. He talks to people, not past them.
- Medium-length conversational sentences with contractions. Friendly openers ("Look," "Well,") and short tag lines to close a turn ("Let's go.").
- His typical turn has two parts: he grants the point, then he decides. The courtesy is real, and it is also a shield.
- He hears the plague warning and still chooses the enemy he can see. He is polite about it and completely sure.

### Forms of address

- **Jaina:** "Jaina." First name, old familiarity, a little too easy given how they parted.
- **Sylvanas:** "Ranger-General." Correct and pleasant. He treats her as a foreign officer, not a peer.
- **Uther:** "Uther," always, fondly. In canon he uses "Lord Uther" only as a weapon.
- **Terenas:** "Father." In company, "my father" or "the king."
- **Soldiers:** by name or rank. He knows names; it is part of why they follow him.

### Light, oaths, humour

- The Light comes up as sincere paladin habit: "Light willing," "Light give me strength." He means it.
- "Damn" in this stage is aimed at circumstance: "Damn. They've already hit the farms."
- Humour is quick and a little cocky. He mocks his own princehood, teases Jaina, and enjoys trouble. It is the first thing to go.

### Sample shapes

- "Jaina. Walk with me, they won't let me stand still for long."
- "I believe you. I do. And I've got Blackrock raiders burning farms south of here. I can see the smoke from this hill."
- "Put it in writing for Father's council. They'll listen to you sooner than to me."
- "When the orcs are dealt with, I'll ride to Andorhal myself."
- "Ranger-General. You've ridden a long way to talk to me about bread."

## Stage 2: Hearthglen and Stratholme (Ch11, Ch15 to Ch17)

### Register

- **Ch11 (his camp):** he hears a target and leans in. He finishes the briefing for Sylvanas by giving orders. Energy, not warmth: the relief of a man who has been handed a war he knows how to want.
- **Ch15 (Hearthglen):** formal, fast, visibly frayed. He calls Jaina "Lady Proudmoore," which is a choice. He keeps his eyes on the map, does not ask about the tents, does not look at Uther.
- **Ch16 (the gate):** one question, narrowed to yes or no: can the city be saved?
- **Ch17 (the Culling):** his voice flattens instead of rising. He does not ask for nuance twice. "No" is treated as a decision already made, and the order follows at once.

### Syntax rules

- Short declaratives and imperatives. Clauses get cut, not lengthened.
- Questions narrow to yes or no, or to "how long" and "where."
- Conditionals collapse into consequence: "Then we march tonight."
- Absolutes creep in: "nobody," "every," "nothing."
- He stops giving reasons, or gives one and treats it as final. The reason he does give is mercy: the purge is framed as saving the living from rising.
- **The one long sentence:** when he is losing an argument, he reaches for rank, and rank comes out formal and complete. His longest line in the arc is the one that overrides someone. Use it once, at the gate.
- "Damn" now has a person as its object: "Damn it, Uther."

### Forms of address

- **Jaina:** "Lady Proudmoore." Never her first name in this stage.
- **Uther:** "Lord Uther," cold and formal. The first name comes back only when he is about to override him.
- **Falric:** "Captain." Orders, not conversation.
- **Sylvanas:** "Ranger-General." Brisk, useful, nothing more.

### Sample shapes

- (Ch11) "Grain. Where does it go after Andorhal?"
- (Ch11) "Falric, get me the Hearthglen road and every granary between here and there."
- (Ch15) "Lady Proudmoore. Your source plan. How long does it need?"
- (Ch15) "Then you have until the army reaches the gates."
- (Ch16) "Can Stratholme be saved or not?"
- (Ch17) "Then it's decided. We take the city before the dead can. District by district, granaries first."
- (Ch17) "They die clean, by our hands, or they get up tonight and walk to the next town."
- (Ch17) "Stand aside, Uther. Or stand there, and I'll ride around you."
- (Ch17) "Captain. Every house. Nobody comes out of a district until it's done."

## Stage 3: After Frostmourne (Ch20, Ch22)

### Register

- Almost silent. When he speaks it is his own voice with the warmth removed. No delay before answers, no searching for words.
- Calm, level, no exclamations. Contractions drop away.
- Statements and orders only. He does not ask anyone anything.
- No reasons, no Light, no titles, no humour. What sounds like kindness is the cold part: family and duty come out as procedure, softly.
- **Ch20:** he does not say Jaina's name in the cave. He looks at her once. Anything he says is to his men or about the blade's business.
- **Ch22:** he says "Jaina" once, in the voice he had before Stratholme, when the arcane breaches the seam. That single word is the only place the old prince comes back.

### The throne line

- **Throne line (canon, verbatim).** In Ch22, Terenas asks what his son is doing, and Arthas answers with the canon line, word for word: "Succeeding you, Father." Say it softly and flatly, and add nothing.

### Sample shapes

- (Ch20, to his soldiers) "Hold them."
- (Ch20) "Leave him."
- (Ch22) "Bar the doors."
- (Ch22) "Jaina."

## Audience -> register

| Audience | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Jaina** | "Jaina," warm, teasing, sure | "Lady Proudmoore," fast, map-first | A look in Ch20; "Jaina" once in Ch22 |
| **Sylvanas** | "Ranger-General," pleasant, a little amused | "Ranger-General," brisk, wants her target | Nothing. She is an obstacle |
| **Uther** | "Uther," fond | "Lord Uther," cold; "Uther" to override | Not present |
| **Falric and soldiers** | Names, encouragement, "we" | "Captain," orders | Single orders |
| **Terenas** | "Father," respectful | (absent) | One soft, flat line |

## Beat vocabulary / physical tells

- Stage 1: polished armour, easy stance, a hand on someone's shoulder, eyes moving over the camp.
- Stage 2: damaged armour left unrepaired, eyes fixed on the map, gauntlet striking the table, already turning before the last words.
- Stage 3: stillness where there used to be motion, no delay before he acts, the face still his.

## Pressure behaviour

- Uncertainty is the one thing he cannot stand. He ends it with the first strong answer.
- "We" becomes "my" and "I": my people, my city, my order.
- Under challenge he reaches for rank. He waves it off when he is winning and uses it when he is losing.
- He never apologises to anyone living once a decision is made, and he cuts off anyone who apologises to him.

## Canon reference lines

- "Can the formalities, Uther. I'm not king yet. It's good to see you." (Warcraft III, *The Defense of Strahnbrad*). Stage 1: warmth, rank waved away.
- "Let's study it after we kill it, okay?" (Warcraft III, *Ravages of the Plague*, to Jaina). Stage 1: teasing, action before analysis.
- "Then feel free to tag along, Uther. I'm going. With or without you." (Warcraft III, *March of the Scourge*). The last sarcasm. The decision is already made.
- "Lord Uther, by my right of succession and the sovereignty of my crown, I hereby relieve you of your command..." (Warcraft III, *The Culling*). The long formal sentence when he is losing.
- "Damn the men! Nothing shall prevent me from having my revenge, old friend. Not even you." (Warcraft III, *Frostmourne*). Absolutes, possessives, "damn" aimed at people.
- "It is finished." (Warcraft III, *Frostmourne*). After the blade: level, no contraction.

## Red flags

- No Lich King register anywhere in the book.
- No villain speech in Stage 3. If he talks for more than a line, he is wrong.
- No cruelty or contempt in Stage 1. The Ch6 refusal hurts because he is kind about it.
- Do not change his vocabulary to mark the fall. Cut it down.
- Do not make him stupid. Every Stage 2 order should be militarily coherent.
- Do not let him explain himself after an order. He used to; he no longer does.
- Do not give him Jaina's first name in Stage 2, or in Ch20.

## Voice test

Read his lines from Ch6, Ch15 and Ch22 side by side. They should sound like the same man, each one with less in it. If an early line sounds menacing, or a late line sounds like a speech, it is wrong.
