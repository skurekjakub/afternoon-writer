# Voice Sheet: Lor'themar Theron

*Story mode: Sylvanas's second-in-command, who fights her war in the Convocation chamber.*

He appears in four forms:
- **On the page:** in the field with her in outline chapter 1, and at her cell door in outline chapter 23.
- **In handwriting:** his runner dispatches in outline chapters 9 and 11.
- **Through Vereesa's report:** in outline chapter 18.

His humour is the barometer. When it goes, he is nearly spent.

Canon research: `research/voice-canon/lorthemar.md`. **Canon is thin for this era.** Warcraft III gives him no lines, and every quote found is from his later years as regent. The traits carry over. The register is inferred.

## Canon reference lines

- "Working for Sylvanas has its... challenges. Being dead has made her even bossier." (WoW Battle for Azeroth, **late era**)
  - He teases his commander behind her back.
- "Queen's champion or no, I can out-shoot Nathanos any day of the week." (same)
  - A marksman's vanity.
- "I am the regent lord. Not the king." (*In the Shadow of the Sun*, **late era**)
  - He is exact about what authority he holds.
- "There is no choice here, Sylvanas." (*In the Shadow of the Sun*, **late era**)
  - Plain, and he uses her name when it is serious.
- "Bow in hand, warm sun on my skin, promises of adventure whispered on the wind." (*Blood of the Highborne*, **late era**)
  - He misses the field.

## Baseline register

- **Field voice with Sylvanas:** dry shorthand and old-command efficiency. He is the only one who teases her.
- **Convocation voice:** quiet, precise, one sentence at a time, with every title correct.
- **Written voice (dispatches):** clipped and factual. The jokes vanish first on the page, and the handwriting tightens (outline chapter 11).

## Syntax rules

- He needles with a mock-formal question: "Shall I tell the chamber...?"
- He is exact about rank and authority, his own included. He says what he can do and where it stops.
- When serious, he drops the joke and says her name, then one plain sentence.
- He can state a vote count once, in dialogue, as a fact. He doesn't tally.

## Forms of address

- **Sylvanas:** "Ranger-General" when teasing or in public, "Sylvanas" when it matters.
- **The Convocation:** "my lords", "Magister".
- **Rangers:** by name.

## Tells

- His dispatches start neat and get tighter as the book goes on.
- Road dust is on him where court polish should be (outline chapter 23).
- He puts the unused order back inside his coat without a word.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, not canon)

- (Chapter 1) "You checked a stone on this road yesterday, Ranger-General. Shall I tell the chamber you've taken up stone-reading?"
- (Chapter 1) "Go. I'll tell them you were called away by something with teeth."
- (Dispatch, chapter 11) "Mill site at the river cleaned before our people reached it. Chamber sits again in three days. I can delay the recall. I cannot stop it."
- (Chapter 23, cell) "It's your door, and only yours. They wouldn't sign for the human."
- (Chapter 23, after she refuses) "I thought you'd say that. I brought it anyway."

## Red flags

- He is not a courtier who has learned wit. He is a ranger who has learned politics and dislikes it.
- No self-pity, even when he is ruined. The cost shows in what he no longer jokes about.
- He doesn't argue at the cell door. The outline is explicit about that.
- Don't use the later regent's formality ("Long have I...") in this era.

## Voice test

Could he say it while riding beside her, half-smiling, and could it still land in the chamber? If the joke is gone, does the silence say how tired he is? Then it is probably Lor'themar.
