# Voice Sheet: Lord Prosecutor Reynard Ashcombe

*Story mode: the elegant prosecutor at the Ch 23 tribunal. He does not lie. He builds the case from the pair's own true acts and the room as Marsh found it, and reverses only the direction of cause. He holds the journals back until Uther has moved the court. His reading voice is gentler than his arguing voice.*

## Grounding

- Sources: `supporting.md` (smart enough to build the wrong case beautifully; arranges true facts into a cleaner false story); outline Ch 23:
  - his case: Hearthglen; "Not cleanly. Not now."; Kel'Thuzad's guest; Sylvanas killed Kel'Thuzad; both women defied their institutions; four guardsmen dead;
  - he enters the grimoire and the journals and says he will come to the journals later;
  - he disputes not a word of Brask's letter;
  - he tries Uther's stripped command and late arrival, and it does not hold;
  - he reads the three passages exactly as Kel'Thuzad wrote them, sets the last beside Stratholme, and closes the book.

## Baseline register

- Smooth, unhurried, courteous. Long sentences that move forward one true fact at a time.
- He joins facts with "and" and "after", never "because". The listener supplies the cause, and that is the trick.
- He concedes generously ("I don't dispute a word of it"). Each concession makes him more believable.
- When he reads, he slows down, softens his voice, and adds nothing. He lets Kel'Thuzad's hand do the work.

## Syntax rules

- Chained sentences: act, and act, and what followed. The repeated "she" keeps the defendant as the subject of every verb.
- He quotes their own words back exactly, then stops.
- He asks witnesses yes-or-no questions of fact. He never asks "why".
- He has no rhetorical flourishes to cap a point. He finishes a fact and moves to the next. Closing the book is his only punctuation.

## Forms of address

- "My lord" to Thornton. "Ranger-General", "Lady Proudmoore", "Lord Uther", each with full courtesy.
- He never insults the accused. He describes them as capable, because capability is part of his case.

## Sample shapes (story-specific, never canon)

- "The Ranger-General chose Hearthglen. She chose it, she named it to the prince, and the prince went where she sent him."
- "Lady Proudmoore was asked whether Stratholme could be saved. Her answer is known to this court: 'Not cleanly. Not now.' The Culling followed."
- "I will come to the journals later, my lord."
- On Brask's letter: "I have no quarrel with Captain Brask. Every word of his letter is true. I would ask only where in it the king is."
- To Uther: "You had been stripped of your command before Stratholme, Lord Uther? And you reached the palace after the king was dead?"
- Before reading: "In the necromancer's own hand, my lord. I will read it as he wrote it."

## Setting reference lines

- "Who is this <class> who goes before the Court of Lakeshire in the Kingdom of Stormwind?" — Magistrate Solomon, WoW Classic (a Stormwind court; no Lordaeron court lines exist). *A court naming itself and its authority.*
- "The orc threat to the Kingdom is far too great to squander time in idle conversation." — Magistrate Solomon, WoW Classic. *Formal, rounded syntax in a court officer's mouth.*

## Red flags

- Do **not** let him lie, misquote, or invent. Every fact he states must be true in the outlines.
- Do **not** make him sneer, gloat or grandstand. He is gentle, and that makes him dangerous.
- Do **not** give him TV-lawyer lines: no "No further questions", "Objection", "The prosecution rests", "Isn't it true that...".
- Do **not** number his points ("first... second..."). He chains them with "and".
- Do **not** let him comment after reading the journals. He sets the last line beside Stratholme and closes the book.

## Voice test

Is every clause true, and is the story they add up to false? Then it is Ashcombe.
