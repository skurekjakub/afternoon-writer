# Subplot: Jaina and Arthas

- Use this as backstory pressure, not as a live romance.
- Core wound (canon, *Arthas: Rise of the Lich King*): Arthas ended the romance, saying she should focus on her studies in Dalaran while he focused on his commitments to Lordaeron. Jaina was very hurt and did not fight him on it, and they agreed to stay friends. She has seen the gap in him and never made him hear it.

## Before the story
- They meet in Dalaran. Arthas loves her intensity. Jaina loves his certainty.
- For a while they sharpen each other: she slows him down, he gets her moving.
- The mismatch is not affection. It is judgment speed. He values clean decisions more than diagnostic pause.
- Diagnostic incident (canon, *Rise of the Lich King* ch. 3): on the road to Dalaran, Arthas talks her into slipping past the guards and watchtowers of an orc internment camp at night. Jaina sees the females and infants and has doubts. Arthas is adamant that the camps are necessary. Jaina sees the future in miniature.
- Arthas ends it, for her studies and his duties. Jaina lets him, partly because she wants to get back to her books. Her guilt grows from that.

## What Arthas carries
- Courtesy gone rigid. He uses formality where warmth used to be.
- He distrusts analysis because analysis is what, in his mind, Jaina chose over him.
- By Stratholme he treats hesitation as betrayal, not caution.

## What Jaina carries
- She was right about the shape of him and still could not change the outcome.
- Every later scene with Arthas feels like the old diagnostic incident at larger scale.
- The guilt is not leaving. It is failing to make him hear what she saw.

## What Sylvanas reads
- She knows there is history.
- After the Scholomance crack, that history becomes another reason to watch Jaina carefully around dangerous men.
- Sylvanas reads the surface accurately and the interior badly.
